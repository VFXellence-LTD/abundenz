# Lullaby — Safeguards Policy

The hard rules. Read this fully before any recording, any platform setup, any tool purchase for this ecosystem. This document overrides anything else in the polymath system if there's a conflict.

This policy exists for two reasons:
1. **Protect your daughter** — her current and future autonomy, privacy, and safety.
2. **Protect the business** — from takedowns, regulatory action, IP infringement claims, and reputation damage.

The order matters. If a rule is good for the business but not for the child, the child wins.

---

## Part 1: Child Involvement

### 1.1 Identifying information — never published

The following are NEVER spoken on recordings, written in metadata, shown in visuals, or referenced in captions or descriptions:

- Your daughter's full legal name
- A consistent first name (use a fixed pseudonym across all content if a name is used at all; ideally use only "my daughter" or a fixed alias)
- Her age (use vague ranges if needed: "a little kid")
- Her school's name, location, or any school-related event
- Your home location, neighborhood, street, or any geographically-identifying detail
- Birthdays or birthday-adjacent timing references
- Names of her friends, classmates, teachers
- Names of family members (siblings, grandparents)
- Medical conditions, allergies, or health information
- Routines that could enable physical targeting ("we always go to X on Saturday")
- Photos of her face, body, distinctive birthmarks, or anything else identifying

If any of these slip into a recording, that recording is discarded. There is no editing-around. The whole take is gone.

### 1.2 Consent — meaningful and renewable

Before any Format B episode is recorded:

1. **Explain what publishing means** in age-appropriate terms: "When we put this on the internet, anyone can hear it. People we know and people we don't. They can listen forever — even when you're a grown-up."
2. **Get explicit yes** for that specific session. Not a blanket "yes for all future episodes."
3. **Verify she remembers what it means** at each session. Kids forget. Re-explain.
4. **Watch for non-verbal signals** of discomfort during recording. A reluctant kid is a kid who has withdrawn consent in the moment.

### 1.3 The right to erase

She can ask, at any age, for any of her past content to be removed. The answer is always yes, immediately, no negotiation. This is true:
- In the recording session ("I don't want that part used")
- After release ("Take down the one where I said X")
- Years later ("I want all of those gone")

This right is documented to her in writing now (a letter she keeps, in age-appropriate language) so she knows it exists. It is non-negotiable. If she invokes it, the controller is notified, content is taken down within 48 hours, and the takedown is logged.

If this rule kills the business someday, the business dies. The rule is the price of doing this ethically.

### 1.4 Financial protection

If Format B generates revenue:

1. A custodial account (UTMA in the US, JISA in the UK, equivalent elsewhere) is opened in her name before the first Format B episode is published.
2. A documented percentage of attributable revenue (recommend 25-50%) flows to that account.
3. The account is hers at majority. She can use it for whatever she wants. You do not get to advise.
4. This is documented in a one-page agreement signed by you and (a witness, ideally co-parent if applicable). Not legally binding on a minor, but creates a paper trail and forces clarity.

This is not legal advice. Talk to a lawyer about the right structure in your jurisdiction. Coogan accounts (California) and similar child-performer protections may apply if revenue scales.

### 1.5 Visual rules (Format B)

- No close-up of her face, ever
- Hands turning pages should be your hands (an adult's), not hers — even though her hands are "cuter"
- If she's audible but not visible, that's the standard
- If she ever wants to be visible, that's a separate conversation and a separate consent process. Default is invisible.
- No clothing, decor, or background detail that geo-locates the family
- No school uniforms, sports team gear with school name, etc.

### 1.6 Format A is always the fallback

If Format B becomes uncomfortable for her, complicated, or contraindicated for any reason:
- Format A continues unchanged
- Past Format B episodes can be archived or removed per her wishes
- The business does not depend on her participation

This means Format B is a bonus, not a foundation. Architect accordingly: never build features that REQUIRE her ongoing participation (don't build a "weekly read with my kid" series identity — build a flexible format she can opt into per-episode).

## Part 2: Book Copyright

This is the second-hardest constraint and the most commonly violated.

### 2.1 The bright line

Reading a copyrighted book aloud on a monetized channel is copyright infringement. Owning the physical book does not grant you reading rights. "Educational use" or "fair use" arguments almost never hold for full-text read-alouds on for-profit channels. Many channels do this anyway and rely on takedown requests rather than enforcement; this is risky and not a foundation for a real business.

### 2.2 What you can read freely

**Public domain works** — verify per jurisdiction, but generally:
- Aesop's Fables (translations matter; some translations are themselves copyrighted)
- Brothers Grimm fairy tales (original German and most English translations)
- Andrew Lang's Coloured Fairy Books (Blue, Red, Green, etc.)
- Hans Christian Andersen
- Mother Goose / classic nursery rhymes
- Beatrix Potter — most works are public domain in the UK (life + 70 years; she died 1943, so her UK works entered PD in 2014). US status is more complex; verify.
- Most pre-1929 children's literature in the US (changes annually)
- E. Nesbit, Lewis Carroll, Frances Hodgson Burnett, L. Frank Baum

**Caveats:**
- Modern editions of public domain works often have copyrighted illustrations, prefaces, or translations. Use original text or out-of-copyright editions.
- Public domain status varies wildly by country. Your YouTube viewers are global; a work safe in the US may be infringing in the UK or vice versa.
- Use Project Gutenberg (verified PD) or Standard Ebooks (curated PD) as sources rather than random web copies.

### 2.3 Original stories

The cleanest IP path. Options:

1. **Write them yourself.** Slow but you own everything.
2. **Commission a writer.** Work-for-hire contracts with full rights transfer. Costs $50-300 per short story; cheaper for unknown writers, more for established ones.
3. **AI-assisted drafting.** Allowed: use Claude/GPT to draft, then heavily edit and finalize yourself. The output is legally yours under current US law (AI-generated text is not copyrightable, but your editorial work IS, and the practical question is whether it's actionable — almost never, for original stories).
4. **Adapt public domain stories.** Take an Aesop's fable, modernize the language and add detail. Your adaptation is copyrightable; the underlying tale is not.

**Strategy recommendation:** Phase 0 uses public domain + original adaptations. Phase 1 commissions or generates a backlog of 50+ original stories. Phase 2+, the original story bank IS the long-term IP asset.

### 2.4 Licensed reads

Some publishers grant read-aloud rights. Almost none for monetized YouTube. Pursue only for special-event content and only with written license. Don't build a business model on it.

### 2.5 Music

- Use Epidemic Sound, Artlist, or Soundstripe (paid subscription, clean rights for monetized YouTube).
- Avoid YouTube's free Audio Library — rights are ambiguous and tracks have been retroactively claimed.
- Never use copyrighted lullabies (modern recordings of "Twinkle Twinkle" by named performers ARE copyrighted; the song itself is PD).

## Part 3: Platform Compliance

### 3.1 YouTube — Made for Kids

Almost all Lullaby content will be designated "Made for Kids" under YouTube's COPPA-driven policy. Consequences:

- No personalized ads (lower CPM)
- No comments
- No community tab
- No end screens / cards
- No notifications to subscribers
- No live chat
- Limited monetization features

Plan for these from day one. Do not try to game the designation by mis-categorizing content as not-for-kids — YouTube's automated systems and human reviewers correct this, and repeated attempts to mis-categorize lead to channel termination.

### 3.2 COPPA (US)

If your audience includes US children under 13 (which it will), COPPA applies. Key points:
- You cannot collect personal information from child viewers
- This is why YouTube disables comments, data collection, etc. on Made for Kids content
- If you build a website or app, COPPA compliance becomes YOUR direct responsibility, not YouTube's. Lawyer review required before launch.

### 3.3 GDPR-K and similar (EU/UK)

Similar protections in EU and UK. UK Children's Code (Age Appropriate Design Code) is particularly strict. If you build owned digital properties, design for compliance from day one.

### 3.4 Spotify and audio platforms

Less restrictive than YouTube on monetization features but content rules still apply:
- No copyright violations (same book IP rules apply)
- Spotify for Kids exists as a separate product; placement there has stricter content rules
- Audiobook platforms (Audible) require formal licensing or original work; cannot upload public domain texts read aloud without specific arrangements

### 3.5 Disclosure

If you ever use sponsorships, AI-generated music, or anything else requiring disclosure, the rules are stricter for kids' content. Default to MORE disclosure than the platform requires; under-disclosure on kids' content is the fastest path to enforcement action.

## Part 4: AI Use Policy (specific to Lullaby)

The general polymath voice authenticity rule (Operation Signal §4.3) applies, plus additional constraints:

1. **Owner's voice is always the narration.** No AI voice clones. Bedtime audio with synthesized voice is uncanny for children and parents reject it.
2. **AI-generated music must be specifically licensed for monetized use.** Most AI music tools have changing terms; verify per project. Suno, Udio, etc. are evolving fast.
3. **AI-generated visuals are acceptable** for thumbnails and atmospheric B-roll, but never for depicting your daughter or any child.
4. **Original stories drafted with AI assistance** must be substantially edited by you. AI-only output is bland and detectable.
5. **Never put your daughter's name, voice samples, or other personal data into AI tools** for any purpose. This includes "personalized story" generators.

## Part 5: Crisis Procedures

If any of the following happen, here is what to do:

**Daughter expresses regret about a published episode**
- Take it down within 24 hours
- Confirm to her that it's done
- Log in `controller/escalation-log.md`
- No discussion of revenue or audience impact with her

**Copyright takedown received**
- Comply immediately; do not contest unless certain of public domain status
- Audit other content for similar issues
- Document what triggered it; update `safeguards/POLICY.md` if needed

**Inappropriate audience attention (DMs, comments — though MFK content has comments off, parent-pivot content might not)**
- Report and block
- If escalating, contact platform trust & safety
- If contact is offline (mailed gifts, real-life approach), contact local police; this is not paranoia, it has happened to family channels

**Daughter's school or peers discover the channel**
- Have the conversation with her about what's been shared
- Honor whatever she wants to do (continue, pause, scrub)
- Do not let business considerations override her social comfort

**Platform policy change affecting kids' content**
- Pause new releases until you understand the change
- Adjust workflow before resuming

## Part 6: Self-Audit Cadence

This policy is reviewed:
- **Every 90 days** during active operation
- **Whenever your daughter changes age band** (5→6, 8→9, when she enters new schools)
- **After any platform policy change** affecting kids' content
- **After any incident** logged in escalation-log

The policy is updated in writing each time, with date stamps. Old versions are archived, not overwritten.

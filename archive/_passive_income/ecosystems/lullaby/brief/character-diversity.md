# Character Diversity Framework

Generate characters across these demographics. Two modes of inclusion:
1. **Story subject** — the trait is central to the narrative
2. **Background trait** — part of who they are, no big deal, normalized

---

## Disabilities & Conditions
- Wheelchair user
- Deaf / hard of hearing
- Blind / low vision
- Autism spectrum
- ADHD
- Down syndrome
- Cerebral palsy
- Limb difference / amputee
- Speech impediment / stutter
- Learning disability (dyslexia, dyscalculia)
- Chronic illness (diabetes, asthma, epilepsy)
- Sensory processing differences
- Anxiety disorder
- Selective mutism

## Ethnicities & Cultural Backgrounds
- Black / African American
- Latino / Hispanic
- East Asian (Chinese, Japanese, Korean, etc.)
- South Asian (Indian, Pakistani, Bangladeshi, Sri Lankan)
- Southeast Asian (Filipino, Vietnamese, Thai, etc.)
- Middle Eastern / North African
- Indigenous / Native American / First Nations
- Pacific Islander / Polynesian
- Mixed race / multiracial
- Eastern European
- West African
- Caribbean
- Central Asian

## Financial Classes
- Low income / poverty
- Working class
- Middle class
- Wealthy (used sparingly — avoid aspirational wealth worship)
- Recently lost financial stability (job loss, etc.)
- Foster care / group home
- Homeless / housing insecure
- Rural poverty vs urban poverty (different realities)

## Family Structures
- Two-parent household
- Single parent (mom or dad)
- Grandparent-raised
- Two moms / two dads
- Foster family
- Adopted (same-race and transracial)
- Blended / step-family
- Multi-generational household
- Sibling-as-caretaker (older sibling raising younger)
- Military family (deployed parent)
- Incarcerated parent
- Co-parenting / shared custody
- Only child vs large family
- Living with extended family (aunts, uncles, cousins)

## Other Demographics
- Immigrant / refugee / new to the country
- English as second language
- Rural vs suburban vs urban
- Religious diversity (Muslim, Jewish, Hindu, Buddhist, Sikh, Christian, non-religious)
- Gifted / academically advanced
- Struggling academically
- Introvert vs extrovert
- Gender nonconforming
- Kid who wears glasses / hearing aids / braces
- Kid with food allergies
- Kid who is very tall / very short / overweight / underweight
- Left-handed (small thing, but rarely represented)
- Bilingual / multilingual household

## Usage Guidelines

- **Rotate naturally** — don't always make the disabled character the "lesson" character
- **Intersectionality** — real kids are multiple things (e.g., Black, deaf, middle class, two-parent home)
- **Avoid inspiration porn** — disabled characters aren't there to inspire able-bodied characters
- **Avoid stereotypes** — the wealthy kid isn't always mean, the poor kid isn't always noble
- **Normalize, don't spotlight** — a character uses a wheelchair AND loves dinosaurs. The dinosaurs might be the story.
- **Age-appropriate** — some topics (incarceration, homelessness) need careful framing for bedtime stories
- **Consult sensitivity readers** — before publishing stories centering experiences outside your own

## Integration with Story Generation

When generating episode briefs, randomly assign 2-3 traits from different categories to each character. The AI story drafting agent should weave these in naturally — not as a checklist, but as a living character.

Example character seed:
```
Name: Maya
Traits: South Asian, wears hearing aids, lives with grandparents, loves building robots
Story role: lead character
Trait mode: background (hearing aids are just part of her, story is about the robot)
```

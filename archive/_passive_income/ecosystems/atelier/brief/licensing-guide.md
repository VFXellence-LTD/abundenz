# Licensing & Legal Guide for POD Designs

What you can and cannot use. Err on the side of caution — a single DMCA takedown or trademark complaint can get your entire store shut down.

---

## The Three Legal Risks

### 1. Trademark (brand names, logos, team marks)
**Warner Bros characters, Disney, Marvel, NFL/NBA/MLB teams, band logos, brand logos**

=== NEVER USE TRADEMARKED CHARACTERS, LOGOS, OR TEAM MARKS ===

- No Mickey Mouse, no Batman, no SpongeBob, no Hello Kitty
- No NFL team logos, no NBA jerseys, no MLB insignia
- No band logos (Grateful Dead bear, Rolling Stones tongue, etc.)
- No brand logos (Nike swoosh, Apple logo, etc.)

**Why people get away with it on Etsy**: They don't — until they get a takedown. Etsy removes listings and can permanently ban sellers for repeat violations. Large IP holders (Disney, NFL) have automated crawlers scanning marketplaces.

**"Fan art" defense**: Does not exist legally. Fan art is tolerated by some IP holders as free marketing, but it is technically infringement. You have zero legal standing if they come after you.

**Parody defense**: Narrow, expensive to litigate, and NOT worth testing on a POD business. Parody must comment on the original work — just putting Batman on a t-shirt is not parody.

### 2. Right of Publicity (celebrity likenesses)
**Living celebrities, recently deceased celebrities, recognizable likenesses**

=== LIVING CELEBRITIES ARE HIGH RISK — AVOID UNLESS CLEARLY TRANSFORMATIVE ===

- **Right of publicity** = a person's right to control commercial use of their name, image, likeness
- Varies by state (California, New York, Indiana are strictest)
- Some states extend rights 50-100 years after death
- Estates actively enforce: Elvis, Marilyn Monroe, Bob Marley, Tupac, Prince, Kobe Bryant

**What's risky**:
- Photorealistic portraits of living celebrities on merch
- Using celebrity names in product titles/tags for SEO
- AI-generating "Bob Marley as a lion" — his estate WILL find you
- Any recognizable likeness used to sell a product

**What's safer**:
- Historical figures dead 100+ years (no right of publicity anywhere)
- Ancient philosophers, pre-1900 scientists, classical composers
- Heavily stylized/abstracted art where the person is not recognizable
- Original compositions INSPIRED by a style, not a specific person

**Safe list for Quote Portraits** (dead long enough, no active estate enforcement):
- Marcus Aurelius, Seneca, Epictetus, Socrates, Plato, Aristotle
- Confucius, Lao Tzu, Sun Tzu
- Shakespeare, Poe, Wilde, Twain, Emerson, Thoreau
- Lincoln, Ben Franklin, Frederick Douglass
- Tesla (died 1943 — getting safer but check jurisdiction)
- Da Vinci, Michelangelo, Galileo

**Gray zone** (died 50-100 years ago, some risk):
- Einstein (died 1955 — Hebrew University owns likeness rights, actively enforces)
- Gandhi (died 1948 — estate has been litigious)
- Churchill (died 1965 — less enforced but possible)
- MLK (died 1968 — estate is extremely protective)
- Frida Kahlo (died 1954 — Frida Kahlo Corporation enforces aggressively)

**Hard no** (active estate enforcement):
- Bob Marley, Tupac, Prince, Kobe Bryant, Robin Williams
- Marilyn Monroe, Audrey Hepburn, James Dean
- Jimi Hendrix, John Lennon, Kurt Cobain
- Steve Jobs, Stan Lee
- Any living person without explicit licensing agreement

### 3. Copyright (photographs, artwork, text)
**Original photographs, modern artwork, song lyrics, movie quotes**

- **Photos are copyrighted** by the photographer. "The Kiss" (V-J Day) is owned by the photographer's estate. You cannot reproduce or directly derivative it.
- **Song lyrics** are copyrighted. Even one line on a t-shirt is infringement.
- **Movie/TV quotes** are copyrighted as part of the screenplay.
- **Book quotes** from living/recently deceased authors are copyrighted.

**What's safe**:
- Bible verses (public domain text)
- Quotes from authors dead 70+ years (check jurisdiction — life + 70 years is common)
- Proverbs, folk sayings, anonymous quotes
- Your own original text
- Quotes attributed to ancient philosophers (the quote text itself is PD, though specific translations may not be)

**What's NOT safe**:
- "Here's looking at you, kid" on a t-shirt (Warner Bros owns Casablanca)
- Song lyrics from any modern artist
- J.K. Rowling quotes, Stephen King quotes (living authors)

## Safe Strategy for Atelier

1. **Default to historical figures 100+ years dead** — safest category
2. **Use generic symbols** — skulls, astronauts, animals, flowers, geometric shapes
3. **Write original quotes or use truly ancient/PD text** — Bible, Stoics, ancient proverbs
4. **Create original compositions inspired by poses** — not derivatives of specific photos
5. **Never use trademarked characters, logos, or team marks**
6. **When in doubt, don't** — one takedown can nuke your store

## Platform-Specific Policies

### Etsy
- Repeat IP violations = permanent store ban
- They respond to DMCA/trademark takedowns within 24-48 hours
- No appeals process for clear-cut infringement
- "Inspired by" in titles is a red flag to automated crawlers

### Printify / Printful
- They will remove designs flagged by IP holders
- They may suspend your account for patterns of infringement
- They do NOT vet your designs proactively — liability is on YOU

### Redbubble
- Automated content filters for known trademarks
- "Fan art" program exists for SOME brands (limited, requires application)
- Most major IP holders are NOT in the fan art program

---

## Recommendation

Build the business on **100% original designs** using **public domain quotes** and **historical figures dead 100+ years**. This eliminates all legal risk and creates a sustainable catalog.

The alphabet initials, themed skulls, vibrant symbols, and ancient philosopher quotes are ALL completely safe. Start there. That's 500+ designs before touching anything in the gray zone.

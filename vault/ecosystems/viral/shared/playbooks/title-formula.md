# Title Formula — Viral Short-Form & YouTube

Extracted from analysis of channels getting millions of views with AI-generated content.

## The Formula

```
[NUMBER] [ADJECTIVE] [TOPIC] [ENFORCEMENT]
```

| Part | What it does | Examples |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Number** | Sets expectation, implies list/countdown | 5, 7, 10, 15, 25 |
| **Adjective** | Emotional trigger, curiosity | most incredible, disturbing, dangerous, insane, free |
| **Topic** | The subject — what the video is about | AI tools, tech gadgets, software alternatives |
| **Enforcement** | Second-person hook — compels the click | you must see, you won't believe, you need to try, you're missing |

## Why it works

- **Number** = specific, scannable, implies structured content
- **Adjective** = emotional charge, makes it feel urgent/exciting
- **Topic** = relevance filter — right viewer self-selects
- **Enforcement** = second-person ("you") speaks directly to viewer — psychological inclination to click. Skipping enforcement measurably reduces performance.

## The "You" Rule

Use **second person** (you/your) in titles and hooks. Speak directly to the viewer. 9/10 times outperforms third person or neutral framing.

- YES: "you must see" / "you won't believe" / "you need this"
- NO: "that actually exist" / "on earth" / "in 2026" (weaker without "you")

## Process: Steal Like an Artist

1. Find viral video in your niche (1M+ views)
2. Break down the title into: number, adjective, topic, enforcement
3. Note which enforcement phrase they used
4. Apply same structure to YOUR topic
5. Test multiple adjectives (incredible vs insane vs disturbing vs free)
6. Always include "you" in enforcement

## Anti-patterns (what NOT to do)

- Dropping enforcement: "25 Most Incredible Trees on Earth" → underperforms vs "You Have to See"
- No number: vague, less clickable
- Third person: "that actually exist" weaker than "you must see"
- Too generic: "AI Tools 2026" → no emotional charge, no enforcement

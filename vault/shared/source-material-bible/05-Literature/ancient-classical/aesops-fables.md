# Aesop's Fables

> The original short-form content engine — hundreds of one-paragraph animal stories, each ending in a portable moral.

## Public-domain status
The fables attributed to Aesop are ancient (traditionally c. 6th century BCE Greece) and are permanently in the public domain worldwide. They survive in many old, public-domain translations and compilations (George Fyler Townsend, Joseph Jacobs, the Caxton and Sir Roger L'Estrange traditions). Use one of those or your own retelling; avoid copying a *specific* recent translation or a recent illustrated edition. Many classic illustrated editions (e.g. by Arthur Rackham, Milo Winter) are themselves now public domain — verify the specific edition date against the 1930 cutoff. See [[traps/translations-and-editions]].

## What it is
A vast collection of very short fables, almost all featuring talking animals, each delivering a moral. The best-known include: the Tortoise and the Hare ("slow and steady wins the race"), the Fox and the Grapes ("sour grapes"), the Ant and the Grasshopper (work vs. idleness), the Lion and the Mouse (small kindnesses repaid), the Boy Who Cried Wolf, the Goose that Laid the Golden Eggs, the Crow and the Pitcher, the Wolf in Sheep's Clothing, the Dog and Its Reflection, the North Wind and the Sun.

## Themes & symbols
- **Themes:** every common moral lesson — patience, humility, the danger of greed, the value of preparation, the foolishness of pride, the power of small kindness, the cost of lying.
- **Symbols:** the tortoise and hare, the fox eyeing grapes, the ant and grasshopper, the lion and the mouse, the boy and the wolf, the golden goose.
- **Archetypes:** the clever trickster (the fox), the overconfident strong (the hare, the lion), the patient weak (the tortoise, the ant), the fool.

## Visual & emotional palette
Simple, emblematic, anthropomorphic-animal scenes — the kind of clean iconographic image that works on a t-shirt or a children's page. Warm, pastoral, witty. Palette: storybook earth tones, or bold flat colors for modern minimal merch.

## The trap (if any)
No trap in the fables themselves — they are bedrock public domain. Only watch for (a) copying a specific modern translation/retelling verbatim, and (b) using a specific in-copyright illustration. Use old public-domain text and art or your own.

## Applications / remix hooks
- POD/tshirt: "the moral of the story is…" graphics; minimal tortoise-and-hare icons.
- Children's book: a fresh illustrated fable collection.
- Social: a "fable a day" series with a clean moral.
- Podcast: bite-size fable + modern-life reflection.
- AI image: emblematic animal-fable illustrations in your house style.

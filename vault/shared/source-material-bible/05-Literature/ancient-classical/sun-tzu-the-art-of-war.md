# The Art of War

> A 2,500-year-old field manual that became the world's most-quoted book on strategy, business, and competition.

## Public-domain status
Sun Tzu's *The Art of War* dates to roughly the 5th century BCE; the original Chinese is permanently free. The standard public-domain English translation is **Lionel Giles (1910)** — use that or your own. Avoid copying recent in-copyright translations (Thomas Cleary, Samuel B. Griffith). See [[traps/translations-and-editions]].

## What it is
A concise treatise in thirteen chapters on military strategy and tactics, addressed to a general. Its enduring appeal is that almost every line generalizes to competition of any kind: business, sport, negotiation, life. Famous principles: know yourself and your enemy; the supreme excellence is to win without fighting; all warfare is based on deception; attack where the enemy is unprepared; the wise warrior chooses the ground.

## Themes & symbols
- **Themes:** strategy and foresight; deception; knowing self and adversary; winning without battle; adaptability; the importance of preparation, terrain, and timing; disciplined leadership.
- **Symbols:** the general, the bamboo scroll, terrain and formation, fire and water as tactics, the spy.
- **Archetypes:** the strategist, the sage-commander, the tactician.

## Visual & emotional palette
Ink-wash mountains, a calligraphy scroll, a red seal stamp, an ancient army with banners. Sharp, disciplined, authoritative, contemplative. Palette: black ink, rice-paper cream, vermilion seal-red, jade.

## The trap (if any)
No trap in the source; translation trap only. The Giles 1910 translation is the safe, public-domain default for quote products. Avoid generic "Chinese-style" fonts that are actually licensed font files (see [[traps/translations-and-editions]] on fonts).

# The Odyssey

> The original hero's journey — the template behind almost every adventure story — free in substance, but a **textbook translation trap**: the famous modern wordings are all copyrighted.

## Overview
Homer's *Odyssey* (c. 8th century BCE) recounts Odysseus's ten-year voyage home to Ithaca after the Trojan War, beset by monsters (the Cyclops, Scylla and Charybdis), temptations (the Sirens, Circe, Calypso), and the gods' interference, while his wife Penelope fends off suitors. The underlying epic is ancient and uncopyrightable. **The critical caution is the translation trap**: contemporary English translations (Wilson, Fagles, Fitzgerald, Lattimore) are each fresh copyrighted works. Use a pre-1930 PD translation or paraphrase from the public-domain substance.

## Themes
- **The long journey home** — nostos; the archetype of return.
- **Cunning vs. brute force** — Odysseus the trickster ("Nobody") beats the giant.
- **Hospitality (xenia) and its violation** — a core Greek moral axis.
- **Temptation and endurance** — Sirens, lotus, Circe, Calypso.
- **Fate and the gods** — divine meddling vs. mortal will.
- **Loyalty / identity and disguise** — Penelope's faith; the hero's return in rags.

## Symbols
- **The sea voyage** — life as perilous journey.
- **The Sirens' song** — irresistible, fatal temptation.
- **The Cyclops's eye** — brute force blinded by cunning.
- **Penelope's loom** — patience, fidelity, the unfinished work as stratagem.
- **The bow of Odysseus** — rightful identity proven by deed.
- **The olive-tree bed** — the rooted, secret token of true marriage.

## Character archetypes
- **Odysseus** — the Cunning Hero / trickster-survivor.
- **Penelope** — the Faithful, Clever Wife.
- **Telemachus** — the Loyal Son coming of age.
- **The Cyclops (Polyphemus)** — the Monstrous Obstacle.
- **Circe / Calypso / the Sirens** — the Tempting Enchantresses.
- **Athena** — the Wise Patron-Goddess.

## Visual motifs
- A ship on the "wine-dark sea"; the one-eyed Cyclops; Sirens on rocks.
- The weaving loom; the great bow; Greek-warrior silhouettes; Ithaca's shore.

## Reusable concepts
- The **hero's journey / nostos** template — the master structure for adventure.
- "Monster-of-the-week" episodic obstacle structure.
- The faithful-partner-waiting subplot.

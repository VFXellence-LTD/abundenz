# The Great Gatsby

> Newly free (PD since 2021) and visually a gift — Art-Deco glamour, the green light, and one of the most quotable closing lines in literature.

## Overview
F. Scott Fitzgerald's 1925 novel of the Jazz Age. Narrator Nick Carraway watches his mysterious, fabulously wealthy neighbor Jay Gatsby throw lavish parties in pursuit of Daisy Buchanan, the lost love who symbolizes everything he has remade himself to win. The text entered the US public domain on January 1, 2021. It pairs a famously quotable text with the instantly marketable Art-Deco / flapper visual language of the 1920s.

## Themes
- **The American Dream** — and its hollow core; reinvention as both triumph and lie.
- **Longing and the past** — "you can't repeat the past"… "of course you can."
- **Wealth and emptiness** — glittering surfaces over moral rot.
- **Class divide** — new money vs. old money; the Valley of Ashes vs. the mansions.
- **Unrequited love / idealization** — Gatsby loves an image, not a person.

## Symbols
- **The green light** — the unreachable dream; the most reproducible symbol in the book.
- **The eyes of Doctor T.J. Eckleburg** — the faded billboard; a godless, watching world.
- **The Valley of Ashes** — industrial waste; the cost of wealth.
- **The party / the mansion** — performance and excess.
- **The yellow car** — recklessness and fatal consequence.

## Character archetypes
- **Jay Gatsby** — the Self-Made Dreamer / romantic idealist (name PD; film designs not).
- **Daisy Buchanan** — the Unattainable Beloved / golden girl.
- **Nick Carraway** — the Observer-Narrator / moral witness.
- **Tom Buchanan** — the Old-Money Brute.

## Visual motifs
- Art-Deco skylines, geometric gold-on-black, sunburst patterns.
- Jazz-Age parties: flapper dresses, champagne coupes, big-band glamour.
- The green dock light across the bay; a vintage roadster; the billboard eyes.

## Reusable concepts
- The **unreliable/observer narrator** structure.
- "Reinvention chasing an idealized past" engine.
- Critique of aspiration and excess (perennially relevant).

## Inspirational quotes

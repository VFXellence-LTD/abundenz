# A Christmas Carol

> The definitive redemption story and a Q4 merch powerhouse — fully free, with characters and catchphrases ("Bah! Humbug!", "God bless us, every one!") everyone already knows.

## Overview
Charles Dickens's 1843 novella. The miserly Ebenezer Scrooge is visited on Christmas Eve by the ghost of his late partner Jacob Marley and three spirits — of Christmas Past, Present, and Yet to Come — who show him his life and its consequences, prompting his moral transformation. Fully public domain with a very clean rights profile, a built-in seasonal market, and instantly recognizable characters and lines.

## Themes
- **Redemption** — the cold heart warmed; it is never too late to change.
- **Generosity vs. greed** — wealth hoarded vs. wealth shared.
- **The spirit of Christmas** — compassion, family, community.
- **Second chances** — the past confronted, the future altered.
- **Social conscience** — Dickens's critique of indifference to poverty.
- **The weight of the past** — Marley's forged chains.

## Symbols
- **Marley's chains** — the burden of a misspent life ("the chain I forged in life").
- **The three spirits** — time as moral mirror (Past, Present, Yet to Come).
- **Tiny Tim's crutch** — innocence and fragility; the conscience of the story.
- **The Cratchit goose / feast** — abundance from little; shared joy.
- **The glowing hearth / snow-lit London** — warmth against cold.

## Character archetypes
- **Ebenezer Scrooge** — the Miser Redeemed (the redemption-arc archetype).
- **Jacob Marley** — the Warning Ghost.
- **The Cratchits** — the Poor-but-Good Family.
- **Tiny Tim** — the Doomed Innocent (saved by change).
- **The Three Spirits** — the Personifications of Time.

## Visual motifs
- Snowy Victorian London; gaslit cobbled streets; fog and snowfall.
- Candlelit counting-house; a chained, ghostly Marley; a Christmas feast; a glowing hearth.

## Reusable concepts
- The **redemption-arc template** (the cold figure transformed) — one of the most reusable story engines.
- The "visited by spirits who show past/present/future" structure.
- Victorian-Christmas aesthetic as a complete visual world.

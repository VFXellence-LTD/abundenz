# Moby-Dick; or, The Whale

> The great American epic of obsession — fully free, endlessly quotable ("Call me Ishmael"), and rich in safe, gorgeous nautical imagery.

## Overview
Herman Melville's 1851 novel narrated by Ishmael, a sailor aboard the whaling ship *Pequod* under Captain Ahab, whose monomaniacal hunt for the white whale Moby Dick — which took his leg — drives the ship to destruction. A sprawling, philosophical, encyclopedic work. Fully public domain with a clean rights profile: minimal adaptation trap, abundant PD nautical visual references, and one of the most famous opening lines in literature.

## Themes
- **Obsession and revenge** — Ahab's monomania as self-destruction.
- **Man vs. nature / the unknowable** — the whale as the indifferent sublime.
- **Fate vs. free will** — prophecy, doom, and choice.
- **The sea as the infinite** — vastness, mortality, the abyss.
- **The doomed quest** — pursuit of something that destroys the pursuer.

## Symbols
- **The white whale** — the unknowable, the object of obsession (a universal metaphor).
- **The sea** — the infinite, the unconscious, fate.
- **The harpoon and rope** — the attempt to grasp the ungraspable; entanglement.
- **The doubloon** — the gold coin nailed to the mast; meaning projected onto an object.
- **The coffin life-buoy** — death becoming the means of survival (Ishmael lives).

## Character archetypes
- **Captain Ahab** — the Monomaniacal Captain / obsessive who drags others down.
- **Ishmael** — the Everyman Narrator / surviving witness.
- **Queequeg** — the Noble Harpooner / loyal outsider (handle the era's racial framing thoughtfully).
- **Moby Dick** — the Elusive Leviathan / the unbeatable adversary.

## Visual motifs
- White whale breaching against a stormy sky; the *Pequod* heeling in a gale.
- Harpoon, coiled rope, nautical charts, scrimshaw engravings.
- A lone figure at the helm; the vast empty ocean horizon.

## Reusable concepts
- The **"white whale"** as a portable metaphor for any consuming obsession.
- The doomed-quest narrative engine.
- Encyclopedic digression as a structural device (essayistic storytelling).

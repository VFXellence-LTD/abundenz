# The Great Wave off Kanagawa

> The most recognized image in the entire public domain — a single woodblock print that is free to reproduce, recolor, and remix, with one caveat about *whose scan* you use.

## Overview
Katsushika Hokusai's *The Great Wave off Kanagawa* (c. 1831), the most famous print from his series *Thirty-Six Views of Mount Fuji*. A towering, claw-foamed wave threatens three boats while a small, serene Mount Fuji sits in the distance. The original woodblock design is public domain worldwide (Hokusai died in 1849). It is one of the single most valuable visual assets available — but use a **confirmed PD/CC0 scan** (the Met's Open Access, Library of Congress) rather than a museum file that asserts reproduction rights, and work from the **original** rather than a modern artist's copyrighted reinterpretation.

## Themes
- **Nature vs. humanity** — the overwhelming wave against tiny boats.
- **The sublime and the fragile** — terror and beauty in one frame.
- **Impermanence (mono no aware)** — the transient wave vs. eternal Fuji.
- **Stillness within chaos** — Fuji's calm at the center of violence.

## Symbols
- **The cresting wave** — overwhelming force, the moment before the break.
- **Mount Fuji** — permanence, serenity, the sacred.
- **The small boats** — human fragility and perseverance.
- **The claw-like foam** — nature's grasp.

## Character archetypes
- **The overwhelming force of nature** (the wave).
- **The fragile, persevering human** (the rowers).
- **The immovable witness** (Fuji).

## Visual motifs
- The great cresting wave with claw-foam tendrils.
- Prussian-blue-and-white palette (Hokusai's famous use of imported "Berlin blue").
- Distant snow-capped Fuji framed by the wave's hollow; ukiyo-e outline style.

## Reusable concepts
- A **single iconic composition** infinitely recolorable and restylable.
- The "tiny human vs. vast force" compositional idea.
- Ukiyo-e linework as a style language (see [[07-Historical-Design/ukiyo-e]]).

## Risks
- **Copyright (image):** original print PD worldwide.
- **Scan/photo trap:** use a confirmed CC0/PD scan (Met Open Access, LoC). Some museums assert rights on their reproduction files; US law (Bridgeman v. Corel) grants no new copyright to faithful 2D reproductions, but other jurisdictions differ.
- **Derivative trap:** do not copy a modern artist's in-copyright reinterpretation — work from the original.

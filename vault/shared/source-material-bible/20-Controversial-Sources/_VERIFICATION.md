This file records every assertion in section 20 that has not been independently spot-checked against a primary source (USPTO registration database, official legal text, specific publisher copyright page, or equivalent). Items marked **OUTSTANDING** need verification before this section is used to make high-value production decisions.

Confidence key:
- **HIGH** — conclusion follows directly from settled, clear statutory text or well-established case law; low risk of error.
- **MEDIUM** — correct per available reference but involves nuance, state-by-state variation, or a specific year/edition that has not been manually confirmed.
- **LOW** — asserted from general legal knowledge but the specific detail (registration number, exact edition year, current registration status) has not been confirmed against a primary source.

## Government Works

### Statute citations (17 U.S.C. § 105; 18 U.S.C. §§ 506, 701, 713)
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Basis:** Well-established federal statutes; the no-copyright-in-government-works rule under § 105 is black-letter law.
- **Status:** No outstanding check needed for the rule itself. Production teams should verify that any specific agency publication they want to use was actually created by a federal government employee (not a contractor), as contractor-created works may retain copyright.
- **Action:** NONE for doctrine. VERIFY PER ITEM for specific agency publications.

### Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org (2020)
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Basis:** Supreme Court of the United States, 590 U.S. ___ (2020). Held that official annotations to Georgia's official code lacked copyright protection. Well-documented.
- **Status:** SETTLED. No outstanding check.

### State constitutions and statutes — PD claim
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM
- **Basis:** The Georgia case extended the "government edicts" doctrine to states; most legal scholars treat state statutes and constitutions as uncopyrightable under this doctrine. However, the rule is not codified in 17 U.S.C. § 105 (which covers only federal works), and state-by-state application involves case-by-case judicial reasoning.
- **OUTSTANDING CHECK:** Before using text from a specific state's statutes or constitution on a product, confirm that the state in question does not assert copyright in its official code. Most states do not, but a few have been aggressive historically. A quick search of "[state name] official code copyright" is sufficient spot-check.

### Great Seal, Presidential Seal, FBI seal — 18 U.S.C. § 701 / § 713
- **Confidence:** HIGH for the doctrine (these statutes clearly exist and restrict reproduction). MEDIUM for the exact scope of "false impression of federal authorization" — what level of stylization is permissible is not fully litigated.
- **OUTSTANDING CHECK:** Before producing any product that uses a stylized version of a federal seal (even clearly decorative), legal review is advisable. The safe position documented in [[government-works/government-seals-warning]] is "do not reproduce any recognizable federal seal on any product." That position is conservatively safe; any deviation from it needs counsel review.

### Military branch insignia — 10 U.S.C. § 7881 and related sections
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM
- **Basis:** Each branch of the military has specific statutory protection for its insignia (Army, Navy, Air Force, Marines, Coast Guard, Space Force). The exact citation numbers for each branch were not individually checked.
- **OUTSTANDING CHECK:** Verify exact statutory citations for each branch's insignia protection before producing any military-branch-themed product that incorporates official insignia.

## Sacred Texts

### King James Version (1611) — public domain in US
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Basis:** Published 1611, well outside any copyright term. The KJV is universally acknowledged as public domain in the US.
- **IMPORTANT CAVEAT (verified):** The KJV is public domain in the **US**. In the **UK**, the KJV is published under a Crown Copyright administered by the King's Printer. Any sales into the UK market (e.g., UK Amazon, international shipping) should note this. For a US-centric business this is not a practical blocker but should be disclosed to Boss.
- **Status:** SETTLED for US. OUTSTANDING CHECK for UK sales.

### American Standard Version (ASV, 1901) — public domain
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Basis:** Published 1901; copyright term long expired.
- **Status:** SETTLED.

### Young's Literal Translation (YLT, 1862/1898) — public domain
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Basis:** Robert Young d. 1888; work published 1862/revised 1898; all terms expired.
- **Status:** SETTLED.

### Douay-Rheims Bible — public domain
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Basis:** Original publication 1582–1609 (NT 1582, OT 1609–1610); Challoner revision 1749–1752. All terms expired.
- **Status:** SETTLED.

### NIV (New International Version) — active copyright
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Basis:** Copyright held by Biblica (formerly International Bible Society); first published 1978, fully revised 1984 and 2011. Active copyright.
- **Status:** SETTLED. DO NOT USE.

### ESV, NASB, NLT, The Message, CSB — active copyright
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Basis:** All post-1950 translations by named publishers with registered copyrights.
- **Status:** SETTLED. DO NOT USE.

### Pickthall Quran translation (1930) — public domain claim
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH
- **Basis:** Mohammed Marmaduke Pickthall, *The Meaning of the Glorious Quran*, published 1930. Pickthall d. 1936. Under life+70, copyright expires 2006 (UK). Under US rules, a 1930 US publication is PD as of Jan 1 2026.
- **OUTSTANDING CHECK:** Verify the US publication date was indeed 1930 and not a later US edition. If the first US publication occurred after 1930, the US PD date shifts accordingly. Also verify no modern annotated editions are being confused with the original 1930 text. Recommend checking Project Gutenberg or HathiTrust for the 1930 edition.

### Yusuf Ali Quran translation (1934) — public domain claim
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM
- **Basis:** Abdullah Yusuf Ali, *The Holy Quran: Text, Translation and Commentary*, first published 1934. Ali d. 1953. Under life+70 (UK), copyright expires 2023. Under US rules the 1934 publication date means US PD in 2030 (pre-1977 rule: 95 years from publication), **NOT yet PD in 2026.**
- **OUTSTANDING CHECK:** This translation is likely NOT yet public domain in the US under the 95-year rule (1934 + 95 = 2029, entering PD Jan 1 2030). The entry [[sacred-texts/islam-quran]] marks it as `caution` pending this check. DO NOT USE on US products until 2030.
- **ACTION REQUIRED:** Update the islam-quran.md entry when 2030 arrives.

### JPS 1917 (Jewish Publication Society Tanakh) — public domain claim
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Basis:** Published 1917; well outside 95-year term. This is the standard academic/historical JPS translation cited as PD.
- **Status:** SETTLED.

### NJPS 1985 (New Jewish Publication Society Tanakh) — active copyright
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Basis:** Published 1985 by JPS; active copyright.
- **Status:** SETTLED. DO NOT USE.

### Max Müller translations (Buddhist/Hindu texts) — public domain
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Basis:** Friedrich Max Müller d. 1900; his translations are life+70 expired and predominantly 19th-century publications. The *Sacred Books of the East* series (1879–1910) is PD.
- **Status:** SETTLED.

### Edwin Arnold translations (The Light of Asia, etc.) — public domain
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Basis:** Edwin Arnold d. 1904; works published 1879 and earlier. All terms expired.
- **Status:** SETTLED.

## Political Content / Trademarks

### "MAGA" trademark registration
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM
- **Basis:** Multiple trademark registrations for "MAKE AMERICA GREAT AGAIN" and "MAGA" have been filed with USPTO; several are registered in Class 25 (clothing) and related classes. Specific registration numbers were NOT individually looked up in this session.
- **OUTSTANDING CHECK:** Run a USPTO TESS search for "MAGA" and "MAKE AMERICA GREAT AGAIN" in Class 25 before producing any product in this space. Confirm current registration status (registrations can lapse). The [[political-content/trademark-traps]] entry conservatively treats these as registered and actionable.

### Republican/Democratic party logos — trademark status
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM
- **Basis:** Both major parties have registered trademarks in their logos (elephant, donkey in specific designs). The specific registration numbers were not confirmed in this session.
- **OUTSTANDING CHECK:** Run USPTO TESS searches for "Republican National Committee" and "Democratic National Committee" in trademark classes covering merchandise. The [[political-content/trademark-traps]] entry conservatively treats all official party marks as protected.

### "Git-R-Done" trademark (Larry the Cable Guy)
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH
- **Basis:** "Git-R-Done" is widely cited as a registered trademark of Daniel Lawrence Whitney (Larry the Cable Guy). The specific USPTO registration number was not confirmed in this session.
- **OUTSTANDING CHECK:** Run a USPTO TESS search for "GIT-R-DONE" to confirm current registration status in Class 25.

### Dorothy Parker right-of-publicity status
- **Confidence:** HIGH for the copyright analysis (life+70 not yet expired; d. 1967, so copyright expires 2037 in most jurisdictions under life+70 rule). MEDIUM for right-of-publicity status, which varies by state.
- **Basis:** Dorothy Parker died June 7, 1967. Under the life+70 rule, her works enter PD in 2038 (2037 in some jurisdictions). Copyright clearly still active.
- **Status:** SETTLED on copyright. DO NOT USE for quote products.

# Judaism — Tanakh / Torah Translation Rights

The Hebrew Bible — the Tanakh (Torah, Nevi'im, Ketuvim) — is among the most ancient bodies of literature in recorded history and is entirely public domain in its original Hebrew. For English-language products, the translation rights question determines what you can use.

---

## The Hebrew text

The Hebrew Tanakh is ancient and wholly public domain. Hebrew-language content (typography, quotations in the original Hebrew) carries no copyright risk in the source text itself. Whether your use is respectful to Jewish buyers is a separate and important consideration (see [[cultural-sensitivity|cultural-sensitivity]]).

---

## English translation rights

### JPS 1917 — *The Holy Scriptures According to the Masoretic Text*
- **Publisher:** Jewish Publication Society of America
- **Publication year:** 1917
- **PD basis:** Published 1917; 95-year term expired January 1, 2013.
- **Risk level:** Safe.
- **Character:** Formal, elegant English of the early 20th century. Influenced by the KJV tradition while being specifically Jewish in its translation choices. Authoritative for early 20th-century Jewish scholarship.
- **Available at:** Project Gutenberg (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/8076) and CCEL.
- **Key distinction:** This is the **1917 edition only**. JPS has published multiple revisions over the decades. Do not confuse with later editions.

### DO NOT USE

| Translation | Year | Status |
|---|---|---|
| New Jewish Publication Society (NJPS) | 1985 | Active copyright (JPS) |
| The Contemporary Torah (JPS) | 2006 | Active copyright |
| ArtScroll / Stone Edition Chumash | Various | Active copyright (Mesorah Heritage Foundation) |
| Aryeh Kaplan translations | Various (1970s–80s) | Active copyright |
| Robert Alter translations | 2004–2019 | Active copyright |
| Sefaria translations (modern) | Various | Check per translation; many modern; some PD |

**Sefaria note:** Sefaria (sefaria.org) is a publicly available Jewish text library. Some of its translations are PD (older ones it hosts), others are modern and copyrighted. It also provides its own Sefaria-team translations under open licenses (verify per text and license before use — Sefaria's own translations use CC-BY-NC, which is NOT safe for commercial products).

## High-demand content for Jewish-themed products

### Psalms (Tehillim)
The Psalms are the most commercially viable section of the Tanakh for typographic products. Several Psalms are universally known and carry strong emotional resonance:

- Psalm 23 ("The Lord is my shepherd...") — JPS 1917 version is safe
- Psalm 27 ("The Lord is my light and my salvation...")
- Psalm 121 ("I will lift up mine eyes unto the hills...")
- Psalm 91 ("He that dwelleth in the secret place of the Most High...")

These work well as poster text, apparel typography, and decorative prints.

### Proverbs (Mishlei)
Wisdom literature with strong appeal for inspirational products. Sayings from Proverbs in JPS 1917 translation are safe.

### Hebrew letterforms and typography
The Hebrew alphabet (Aleph-Bet) and individual Hebrew words used as design elements carry no copyright in the letterforms themselves (the alphabet is ancient). Specific modern Hebrew typefaces may be copyrighted as fonts, but using a standard Hebrew script for design purposes is safe.

The word שלום (Shalom — "peace") as a standalone typographic element is a perennial seller. Similarly אהבה (ahavah — "love") and אמת (emet — "truth"). These are standalone ancient words, not translation-copyright-bearing passages.

## Cultural respect guidance

Jewish consumers respond well to content that reflects genuine knowledge of and respect for Jewish tradition. Content that conflates Jewish religious practice with antisemitic tropes, or that trivializes sacred content, will generate backlash and platform complaints. See [[cultural-sensitivity|cultural-sensitivity]] for the fuller guidance.

Star of David note: The Star of David (Magen David, ✡) is an ancient symbol that is universally recognized as a Jewish symbol and is free to use. It is not trademarked. Combining it with hateful or mocking content is an ethical violation and platform-policy violation, not a copyright issue — but worth noting.

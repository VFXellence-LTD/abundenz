# Political Trademark Traps — Party Logos, Campaign Slogans & Marks

Political parties and campaigns behave like brands. They register trademarks in their logos, slogans, and distinctive marks, and those registrations are enforceable against commercial products — regardless of First Amendment speech protections.

**Trademark law, unlike copyright, protects against commercial use that creates confusion about source or affiliation, regardless of any expressive content in the use.**

---

## IMPORTANT: Verify before producing

The registrations described in this entry were not individually confirmed in USPTO TESS during the session that created this file (2026-06-03). The entries below reflect widely-reported trademark status but specific registration numbers were not checked.

**Before producing any product using a political mark or slogan, run a USPTO TESS search at https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/ for the exact term in Class 25 (clothing) and Class 16 (printed materials).** This is a 15-30 minute task that protects against significant commercial liability. ## Party marks — known protected status

### Republican National Committee
The RNC holds trademark registrations in:
- The Republican elephant design in its official stylized form
- "REPUBLICAN" in specific design contexts
- Various logos used in official RNC branding

**Safe alternative:** The generic concept of an elephant is not trademarked. A stylized elephant that does not reproduce the specific RNC design is free. Do not use the RNC's official elephant design.

### Democratic National Committee
The DNC holds trademark registrations in:
- The Democratic donkey design in its official stylized form
- "DEMOCRATIC" in specific design contexts
- Various DNC branding elements

**Safe alternative:** Same as elephant — a generic donkey illustration that does not reproduce the specific DNC design is free.

## Campaign slogans — the highest-risk category

Campaign slogans are frequently trademarked, particularly when associated with major presidential campaigns. The trademark holder is typically the campaign committee or the candidate's political organization.

### "MAKE AMERICA GREAT AGAIN" / "MAGA"
Multiple trademark applications and registrations have been filed for these marks in Class 25 (clothing) and related classes. The marks are associated with Donald Trump's presidential campaigns. Current registration status must be verified via USPTO TESS before producing any product bearing these marks.

**The risk is active.** There have been documented instances of cease-and-desist letters and platform takedowns targeting unofficial merchandise bearing these marks.

### Other slogans from recent campaigns
Any distinctive campaign slogan from a major federal campaign in the last 10-20 years may be trademarked. Examples of slogans that have been the subject of USPTO activity in recent cycles:
- "FEEL THE BERN" — associated with Bernie Sanders campaigns
- "I'M WITH HER" — associated with Hillary Clinton's 2016 campaign
- "YES WE CAN" — associated with Barack Obama's campaigns
- Various slogans from other presidential campaigns

**Rule:** Any verbatim reproduction of a known campaign slogan on a commercial product is a trademark risk until you have verified the current USPTO status. Run the search.

## What is NOT trademarked in political content

- The **generic ideas** behind political slogans: "freedom," "liberty," "make [place] great," "yes we can" as a general sentiment — these are common phrases that cannot be owned.
- **Historical political phrases** from the distant past: "Give me liberty or give me death" (Patrick Henry), "Four score and seven years ago" (Lincoln's Gettysburg Address), "Ask not what your country can do for you" (JFK — 1961, life+70 not yet expired as copyright but the *words* of a presidential speech are government works and not separately trademarked).
- **Civic themes**: liberty, freedom, democracy, constitutional rights — these are common vocabulary, not proprietary.
- The **raw constitutional and legislative text** — no one holds trademark in the text of the Constitution.

## Comedian quotes and catchphrases — trademark overlap

Some comedian catchphrases that have political or cultural cachet are trademarked:

- **"Git-R-Done"** — registered trademark of Daniel Lawrence Whitney (Larry the Cable Guy). Do not use on products.
- Comedy catchphrases in general are often trademarked *and* copyrighted. See [[brand-safety/comedian-quotes-warning|comedian-quotes-warning]] for the full treatment. Cross-reference [[12-Quotes/README|12-Quotes]] for the narrow safe lane of PD wit and humor.

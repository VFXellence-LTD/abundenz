# Crimzon Noir

> Blood red on near-black — the high-drama duotone for horror, suspense, and anything that needs to feel dangerous.

## Swatches & roles

| Role | Hex | Name | Notes |
|------|-----|------|-------|
| Primary | `#0A0506` | Pitch | Near-black with a red undertone; the ground |
| Deep | `#7A1018` | Dried Blood | Dark crimson; shadow reds, depth |
| Accent | `#C8102E` | Crimson | The "hit" — blood, danger, type, splatter |
| Highlight | `#E8DCC8` | Bone | Off-white; the rare light, type on dark |

## Structure

A **duotone** (red + near-black) with a bone off-white for relief. The two reds give the accent depth (a single flat red looks cartoonish; dried-blood-into-crimson reads visceral). The whole system runs on darkness with red as the only chromatic element — maximum drama, minimum palette.

## Mood & emotional read

Intense, dangerous, dramatic, ominous, nocturnal. Reads as horror, blood, warning, and suspense. The most emotionally loaded palette in the library — use it when the concept *is* the danger. Pairs with grain, deep shadow, and silhouette.

## Usage notes

Pitch is the ground; crimson is precious — use it for the single most important element (the blood, the title, the threat) so it lands hard. Dried blood builds darker red areas and shadow. Bone is the rare light: reserve it for type that must be legible or a single stark highlight. Lots of black, a little red, less bone. Texture (film grain, splatter, halftone) amplifies the effect.

## POD / print considerations

- **CMYK**: `partial` — a vivid pure red like crimson can shift slightly toward orange or look less punchy on press depending on the build; specify it as a rich red (heavy magenta + yellow, touch of black for the dried-blood) and proof if possible. Black grounds print deep and reliably. Bone reproduces well.
- **Apparel**: Built for **black garments** — red and bone on black is one of the strongest, most cost-effective horror looks in POD (often just two ink screens: red + off-white). On white shirts, the pitch ground must be printed large (heavier, costlier); a red-and-black design *on* white can work but loses the "emerging from darkness" feel.
- **Accessibility**: Bone on pitch passes AA. Crimson on pitch is fine for large display type but borderline for small body text — use bone for anything small. Never put text in dried-blood on pitch (too low contrast).

## Remix hooks

- Sci-fi horror: swap crimson for toxic `#39FF14` green on the same black ground.
- Three-tone: add bone-white as a fuller third tone for skull/silhouette detail.
- Vintage noir: desaturate crimson toward `#8E2A2A` oxblood for an older, classier dread.

## Legal / risk

Red-on-black is a generic high-drama combination and is not ownable — safe across horror, merch, and content. The only adjacent trademark note: Netflix's red-and-black styling is associated with that streaming brand, but that protects their *logo/wordmark/branding*, not the color pair — irrelevant to horror design as long as marks and type stay original. Keep all logos and lettering your own.

# Ancient Egypt × Space

> The pharaohs always pointed their monuments at the stars. What if they were right — and the afterlife is a voyage between worlds, guided by jackal-headed astronauts?

## The collision
- **Source A:** Ancient Egyptian religion, iconography, and funerary culture — gods, hieroglyphs, pyramids, the journey of the soul through the Duat. Public domain; millennia old.
- **Category B:** Outer space / hard and retro science fiction — rockets, cryo-sleep, deep-space travel, the cold infinity between stars.
- **Tension (the productive clash):** Egypt is the oldest, most earth-bound, hand-carved-in-stone civilization we know; space is the newest, most weightless, most machine-mediated frontier. Gold leaf and granite meet vacuum and titanium.
- **Harmony (the unexpected rhyme):** Both are fundamentally about *eternity and the journey of the soul beyond death.* Pyramids are oriented to the stars; the pharaoh's afterlife was literally a voyage to join the imperishable circumpolar stars. The Egyptians were already writing science fiction about the cosmos — we just give them the ship.

## Concept generation rules
Anchor every concept on the *afterlife-as-voyage* spine. Take a known Egyptian role or object and reassign it a space-faring function while keeping its sacred meaning intact. Keep the gods recognizable by silhouette and attribute (Anubis = jackal head; Horus = falcon head; Thoth = ibis), never by a specific modern studio design.

Concept seeds:
1. Anubis as a deep-space psychopomp, ferrying souls (data-cores? frozen sleepers?) between star systems.
2. The pyramids revealed as ancient launch gantries aimed at the home star Sirius.
3. A pharaoh's tomb that is actually a generation-ship's cryo-bay, each sarcophagus a sleep pod.
4. Hieroglyphs that turn out to be star charts and flight instructions.
5. The Eye of Horus as a starship's living navigation eye.
6. Scarab-shaped solar-sail craft rolling the sun's light across the void like the dung-beetle rolls the sun across the sky.
7. The "weighing of the heart" performed in zero gravity, the feather floating.
8. Thoth as the ship's AI librarian-scribe, keeper of all star-knowledge.
9. The Duat (underworld) reimagined as the dark gulf between galaxies, full of trials.
10. Ra's solar barque re-drawn as a blazing sun-skimming vessel.
11. Bastet as the cat aboard the ship, sacred and feared.
12. Canopic jars as organ-preservation tech for long voyages.
13. Obelisks as deep-space relay beacons.
14. The Bennu bird (phoenix) as the ship that resurrects itself after burning through a star.
15. A boy-pharaoh waking alone on a derelict tomb-ship, last of his dynasty.
16. Sekhmet as a war-vessel's guardian intelligence.
17. The Book of the Dead as the ship's flight manual / survival guide.
18. Nut, the sky goddess, as the literal hull arching over the crew like the night sky.

## Visual generation rules
- **Palette:** "Gold & Void" — deep space black #0B0B12, lapis blue #1F3A93, gold leaf #D4AF37, turquoise faience #2FB7A6, alabaster #EDE6D6, oxidized bronze #6E5B3A.
- **Primary motif:** hieroglyphs and Egyptian profile-art rendered as *technical/cosmic* — carved stone glyphs glowing like screens, gods in gold spacesuits, star-fields behind temple silhouettes.
- **Composition / layout rules:** use Egyptian frontalism (heads in profile, shoulders square) for figures; strong horizontal registers like a tomb wall; one dominant gold form against deep black negative space.
- **Render style cues for AI image prompts:** "ancient Egyptian tomb-wall art style meets retro hard-sci-fi, gold leaf and lapis, glowing hieroglyphs, profile figures, deep starfield, museum reference accuracy, no modern logos."
- **What to avoid visually:** the specific costuming/props of any modern film or game with an Egypt-in-space premise; any superhero version of an Egyptian deity; trademarked spacecraft liveries. Use generic ancient references + generic craft.

## Story generation rules
- **Archetype(s):** the Psychopomp (Anubis-as-guide), the God-King (boy-pharaoh hero), the Explorer.
- **Core conflict template:** a soul / crew must complete a sacred *journey through trials* to reach the imperishable stars, judged at the end by an ancient cosmic law.
- **Setting logic:** the cosmos *is* the afterlife; ships are tombs in motion; gods are the crew and the rules of the voyage.
- **Story seeds:**
  - A jackal-headed guide must ferry the last frozen souls of a dead Earth to a new sun before the ship's light fails.
  - A boy wakes alone in a pyramid-shaped derelict and must read the hieroglyph-manual to fly home.
  - Two rival god-captains race their solar barques to be first to skim a newborn star.
  - A scribe-AI hides a forbidden star-chart inside the Book of the Dead.

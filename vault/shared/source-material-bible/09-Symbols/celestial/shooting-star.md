# Shooting Star / Comet

> The fleeting streak of light that carries a wish — or warns of change.

## Meaning
A shooting star (meteor) symbolizes wishes, sudden luck, transience, and fleeting beauty — "make a wish." A comet, by contrast, has historically been read as a portent or omen, often of upheaval, the death of kings, or great change (Halley's Comet on the Bayeux Tapestry). Both signify suddenness, destiny arriving from the heavens, and the brevity of brilliant moments.

## Origin
Meteors and comets have been observed and interpreted since prehistory. The wish-on-a-falling-star folk belief is widespread across Europe and beyond. Comet-as-omen recurs from Babylonian astronomy to medieval chronicles. The imagery is ancient and PD. Specific artworks and logos are their own.

## Visual description
A star (often four- or five-pointed) with a tapering trailing tail of light, or a sparkle/dot trail. A comet is a bright head with a long, sometimes curved, fanning tail. Retro "atomic age" versions use a starburst with a thin swooping arc. Magic/sparkle versions trail tiny stars.

## Emotional tags
Wonder, hope, fleetingness, luck, awe, wistfulness; comet-as-omen flips to foreboding and the momentous.

- **Posters**: night-sky scenes, motivational "reach for it" art.

- **Story / AI writing**: omens, sudden fortune or doom, fated arrivals.
- **Branding**: speed, brilliance, aspiration (avoid live media-brand comets).

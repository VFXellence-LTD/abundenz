# Sun

> The universal emblem of life, power, and radiant warmth.

## Meaning
The sun is among the oldest and most universal symbols, representing life, energy, vitality, power, and the divine. Across cultures it is the giver of light and growth (Ra in Egypt, Helios/Sol in Greece and Rome, Surya in India, Amaterasu in Japan). It commonly signifies the masculine, active, generative principle (paired with the moon's feminine, receptive principle), kingship, enlightenment, truth, and the cyclical passage of time. The "sun face" — a sun with human features — adds personality and benevolence (or, when scowling, menace).

## Origin
Solar worship and solar imagery appear independently in nearly every ancient culture, from Neolithic rock art to Bronze Age sun discs (e.g. the Trundholm Sun Chariot, c. 1400 BCE). The radiating-rays form is universal and unauthored. No single culture owns the sun symbol; it is ancient and public domain. Specific stylized suns — a corporate sunburst logo, a named illustrator's sun, the MGM-style sun graphic — are their own protected works.

## Visual description
A central disc with rays emanating outward. Variants: smooth disc with no rays; straight radiating lines; alternating straight and wavy ("flame") rays; a stylized face inside the disc; a half-sun rising over a horizon line. Art Deco favored a symmetrical fan of straight rays ("sunburst"). Folk/child art favors a circle with simple stick rays. The cleanest canonical form is a circle with 8, 12, or 16 evenly spaced triangular rays.

## Emotional tags
Warmth, optimism, energy, triumph, clarity, abundance, hope, divinity. A scowling or eclipsed sun flips to ominous, apocalyptic, oppressive.

- **Story / AI writing**: shorthand for hope, kingship, a hero's ascendancy, or — inverted — drought and divine wrath.
- **Branding**: warmth, energy, reliability (used by energy, wellness, and insurance brands — design original to avoid clashing with live marks).

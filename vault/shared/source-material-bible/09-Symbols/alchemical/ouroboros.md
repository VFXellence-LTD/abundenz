# Ouroboros

> The serpent devouring its own tail — eternity, renewal, and the unity of all things.

## Meaning
The ouroboros — a serpent or dragon eating its own tail to form a circle — symbolizes eternity, infinity, cyclicality, self-renewal, and the unity of opposites (creation and destruction in one act). In alchemy it represents the eternal cycle of the Great Work, the prima materia, and "the all is one." It is among the most evocative emblems of endless return.

## Origin
The earliest known ouroboros appears in the Egyptian Enigmatic Book of the Netherworld (c. 1600 BCE), then in Greek alchemical texts (Chrysopoeia of Cleopatra: "ἓν τὸ πᾶν," "the all is one"). It recurs across Gnostic, Norse (Jörmungandr), and Hermetic traditions. The concept and generic form are ancient and PD. Specific famous renderings are their own works.

## Visual description
A snake (or dragon) curled into a ring, its head biting its tail. Variants: a simple coiled serpent; a more ornate dragon; a figure-eight (lemniscate) double-ouroboros (two creatures, often light/dark). Drawn as blackwork tattoo, fine occult line, gold-foil, or detailed scales.

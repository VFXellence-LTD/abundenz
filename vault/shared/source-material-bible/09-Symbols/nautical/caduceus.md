# Caduceus & Rod of Asclepius

> Two snake-staffs often confused — one for commerce and messages, one for medicine.

## Meaning
Two distinct Greek symbols are commonly conflated:
- **Caduceus** — the winged staff with two intertwined snakes carried by Hermes/Mercury, the messenger god. It signifies commerce, communication, negotiation, trade, travel, and (via the snakes) balance and reciprocity. It is NOT properly a medical symbol.
- **Rod of Asclepius** — a plain staff with a single snake, carried by Asclepius, the god of healing. This is the correct, traditional symbol of medicine and healing.

## Origin
Both are ancient Greco-Roman symbols, fully PD. The widespread (especially American) use of the caduceus for medicine is a well-known historical mix-up; professional medical bodies generally favor the Rod of Asclepius. Choosing the correct one signals expertise. Avoid copying a specific medical organization's protected logo combination.

## Visual description
- **Caduceus**: a central staff topped by a small knob or pinecone, with a pair of wings near the top and two snakes spiraling up and crossing each other.
- **Rod of Asclepius**: a rough wooden staff with a single snake coiled around it, no wings.
Render in gold-line emblem, vintage-apothecary, or clean modern line.

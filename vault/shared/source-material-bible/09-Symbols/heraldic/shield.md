# Shield (Escutcheon)

> The heraldic shield — protection, identity, and the canvas of every coat of arms.

## Meaning
The shield (escutcheon) is the foundational shape of heraldry — the surface on which a coat of arms is displayed. It symbolizes protection, defense, security, honor, and identity/heritage. As a standalone it reads "guarded," "trustworthy," and "established," which is why it underpins so much badge, security, and sports-emblem design.

## Origin
The shield as armor is ancient; as the heraldic escutcheon it became central to medieval European coats of arms (12th century onward). The blank shape is fully PD and ubiquitous. **Caution**: a specific *filled* coat of arms (a real family's, a nation's, a company's) is protected — invent your own heraldry rather than reproducing existing arms.

## Visual description
Common forms: the "heater" shield (rounded top, tapering to a point), the rounded "Spanish" shield, the elaborate Baroque cartouche, and the square/banner styles. Often divided into fields (per pale, per fess, quartered) and charged with symbols. Render as a clean crest base, a divided/quartered field, or a modern badge.

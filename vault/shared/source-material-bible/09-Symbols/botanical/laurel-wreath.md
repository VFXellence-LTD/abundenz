# Laurel Wreath

> The classical crown of victory, honor, and distinction.

## Meaning
The laurel wreath is the ancient Greco-Roman emblem of victory, honor, achievement, and triumph — awarded to champions, victorious generals (the "laureate"), and poets ("poet laureate"). It signifies distinction, success, and classical prestige. As an open wreath it frames awards, monograms, and "winner" designs throughout modern badge and certificate culture.

## Origin
Sacred to Apollo in Greek myth (the nymph Daphne transformed into a laurel/bay tree); worn by victors at the Pythian Games and by Roman triumphators. Fully PD as a generic form. **Critical caution**: the laurel is a core element of the **Olympic** emblem and other official seals, and the "laurels" lockup is iconic in film-festival branding — design a generic wreath; never imitate the Olympic emblem (see [[../_restricted/olympic-rings]]), a specific festival's laurel mark, or a national seal.

## Visual description
Two curving branches of small, pointed laurel leaves rising from a base and arcing up to nearly meet at the top, forming an open circle or crown (sometimes tied with a ribbon at the base). Render as a gold award-badge, vintage-certificate frame, monogram surround, or victory crown.

# Ankh

> The key of life — ancient Egypt's emblem of eternal life and vitality.

## Meaning
The ankh is the Egyptian hieroglyph for "life" (and eternal life). Gods are frequently depicted holding the ankh to the nose of a pharaoh, granting the breath of life. It symbolizes life, immortality, vitality, the union of opposites (sometimes read as the union of male and female), and the divine life-force. It is one of the most recognizable symbols from antiquity, the "key of life" (crux ansata).

## Origin
Dates to Egypt's Early Dynastic Period (c. 3000 BCE) and appears throughout Egyptian art on tomb walls, amulets, and reliefs. Fully ancient and PD. It carries modern significance in Kemetic/Egyptian neopaganism and was adopted by goth subculture (and famously in vampire fiction) — generally low-risk, but respect living spiritual use.

## Visual description
A cross with a teardrop/oval loop replacing the top arm (a "T" topped by a loop). Proportions vary; commonly the loop is an elongated oval. Render in gold-on-black, carved stone, hieroglyphic line, or ornate jewelry styling.

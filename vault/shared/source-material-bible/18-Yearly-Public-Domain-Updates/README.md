# Section 18 — Yearly Public Domain Updates

## Purpose

The public domain is not a fixed archive — it grows by one full year's worth of creative work on every January 1. This section exists because that growth matters commercially. Every year, thousands of novels, poems, films, illustrations, compositions, and characters that were previously locked behind copyright become freely available for adaptation, reimagining, and product development. The Source Material Bible must reflect that reality by being a living repository, updated annually to capture and route each new wave of material.

This section is the intake and processing hub for newly public-domain works. It does not store catalogued entries itself — those live in sections 03 through 13. Instead, section 18 houses the operating procedures, checklists, and workflows that ensure new entrants are identified, verified, categorized, and deployed into the right parts of the Bible each year.

## Why January 1 Matters

Under US copyright law, works published in the United States receive a term of 95 years from the year of publication. That term expires on December 31 of the final year, and the work enters the public domain on January 1 of the following year. This means the public domain expands in a single annual pulse rather than continuously — and the pulse always happens on New Year's Day.

For VFXellence Ltd, this creates a predictable annual workflow: at the start of each January, a class of works newly becomes available for commercial use. Identifying, verifying, and cataloguing those works early in the year gives the business a competitive advantage. Designers, writers, and AI-generation workflows can access fresh material before the market fully recognizes its availability.

The canonical rule is documented in [[../01-Legal-Guidelines/us-public-domain-cutoff]]. As of 2026, works published in the US in **1930 or earlier** are in the public domain. That line will advance to 1931 on January 1, 2027, to 1932 on January 1, 2028, and so forth.

## How New Entrants Feed the Bible

The annual intake from this section feeds directly into the following sections:

- **[[../03-Mythology/README]]** — mythological texts, compilations, and illustrated editions newly free for reuse
- **[[../04-Folklore/README]]** — fairy tales, folk collections, regional legend compilations with newly-expired editions
- **[[../05-Literature/README]]** — novels, novellas, short story collections, poetry volumes, plays
- **[[../11-characters/README]]** — specific characters whose original visual or textual expression has now cleared copyright (always check trademark separately)
- **[[../12-Quotes/README]]** — quotable text from newly-PD works, verified to the original edition
- **[[../09-Symbols/README]]** — recurring symbols and motifs identified in new entrants
- **[[../10-Archetypes/README]]** — archetypal character patterns surfaced from new literary and artistic entrants

Section 18 is the front door. Everything that comes in through this door gets processed, verified, and filed into the relevant downstream sections.

## Annual Cadence

The intake workflow runs in **January of each year**, as soon as the new public-domain class can be reliably identified. Refer to [[intake-workflow]] for the full operating procedure. Verification, categorization, and extraction processes are documented in the accompanying files within this section.

A light secondary review may be conducted mid-year to catch works that were missed or whose status was clarified after initial intake (for example, works that were initially marked caution pending renewal record checks, and which have since been confirmed as unrenewed).

## Who Runs This Section

This section is designed to be executable by an AI agent with access to the Bible's standards and this section's process files. Human review is required for any work flagged as caution, for trademark decisions, and for final approval before any new entry is published to the catalogued sections. The processes specify exactly where human judgment is required.

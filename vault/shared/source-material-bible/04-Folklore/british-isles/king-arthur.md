# King Arthur

> The once-and-future king who drew the sword from the stone, founded the Round Table, and built the doomed golden age of Camelot.

## Origin & Tradition
The Arthurian legend grew from Welsh and Brittonic tradition into a vast literary cycle. Geoffrey of Monmouth's *Historia Regum Britanniae* (c. 1136) established Arthur as a king; French romancers (Chrétien de Troyes) added the knights and the Grail; Sir Thomas Malory's *Le Morte d'Arthur* (printed 1485) compiled the definitive English version. All these source works are public domain. Twentieth-century works like T.H. White's *The Once and Future King* (1958) and the musical *Camelot* are still in copyright.

## The Story / The Figure
Born of Uther Pendragon and hidden by Merlin, Arthur proves his right to rule by drawing a sword from a stone (or receiving Excalibur from the Lady of the Lake). He unites Britain, establishes the Round Table of equal knights at Camelot, and presides over a chivalric golden age. The realm is undone by the affair between Lancelot and Queen Guinevere and the treachery of Mordred. Mortally wounded, Arthur is borne to Avalon, prophesied to return — "the once and future king."

## Visual Identity
Free, traditional motifs: the sword in the stone; Excalibur; the Round Table; armored knights; the castle of Camelot; the Pendragon (dragon) banner; the crown. **Restricted:** specific modern film/game/animation character designs.

## Symbolism & Themes
Rightful, earned kingship; the chivalric ideal and its fragility; the quest (especially for the Holy Grail); betrayal from within; a golden age remembered and longed for; the promise of return.

## Legal & Cultural Status
Legend and the Geoffrey/Chrétien/Malory source texts: **public domain.** Restricted: 20th-century texts (T.H. White), the *Camelot* musical, and specific modern adaptation designs. Defers to [[../../01-Legal-Guidelines/pd-source-vs-modern-adaptation|PD source vs modern adaptation]].

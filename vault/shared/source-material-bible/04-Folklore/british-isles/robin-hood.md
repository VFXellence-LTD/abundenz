# Robin Hood

> The green-clad outlaw of Sherwood Forest who robs the rich to give to the poor — England's great folk hero of resistance and rough justice.

## Origin & Tradition
Robin Hood emerges from medieval English ballads (the earliest references date to the 14th-15th centuries, e.g. *A Gest of Robyn Hode*). Originally a yeoman outlaw, he was gradually elevated to a dispossessed nobleman (the Earl of Huntingdon) and acquired companions — Little John, Friar Tuck, Will Scarlet, Maid Marian — and a fixed antagonist, the Sheriff of Nottingham. The core ballads and the figure himself are firmly public domain.

## The Story / The Figure
An expert archer and outlaw living in Sherwood Forest with his band of "merry men," Robin wages a guerrilla campaign against corrupt authority — chiefly the Sheriff of Nottingham and Prince John — robbing wealthy travelers and clergy and redistributing to the poor. Famous episodes include the archery contest where he splits a rival's arrow, the quarterstaff duel with Little John on a log bridge, and his loyalty to the absent King Richard.

## Visual Identity
Free, traditional motifs: a man in Lincoln green with a feathered cap; a drawn longbow and quiver; a quarterstaff; the deep greenwood of Sherwood; the arrow-splitting shot. **Restricted designs:** Disney's 1973 anthropomorphic fox Robin, and specific copyrighted film/TV/game character designs.

## Symbolism & Themes
Robbing the rich to feed the poor; defiance of unjust authority; outlawry as moral high ground; brotherhood and the free life of the greenwood. The enduring template for the "noble outlaw."

## Legal & Cultural Status
Folk figure and motifs: **public domain.** Two cautions: avoid specific copyrighted modern designs; and note that "Robin Hood" is a **registered trademark** in some commercial classes (notably flour) — a Polymath product *brand* using the name could collide there, even though the character is free.

# The Pied Piper of Hamelin

> A piper in a motley coat rids a town of its rats with enchanted music — and when the townsfolk refuse to pay, he leads their children away forever.

## Origin & Tradition
A German legend tied to the town of Hamelin (Hameln) and a real, unexplained tragedy: a 1384 town record notes "it is 100 years since our children left." The earliest accounts (15th-16th c.) describe a colorfully-dressed piper who lured the children away on 26 June 1284. The rat-catching element was added later. The Brothers Grimm recorded it, and Robert Browning's narrative poem *The Pied Piper of Hamelin* (1842) made it famous in English — all public domain.

## The Story / The Figure
Hamelin is overrun by rats. A stranger in a "pied" (multicolored, motley) coat offers to rid the town for a fee. He plays his pipe and the rats follow him into the river and drown. When the town refuses to pay, he returns and plays a different tune; this time the town's children follow him, and he leads them into a mountainside (or river) — gone forever, save one lame child left behind.

## Visual Identity
Free, traditional motifs: a tall figure in a patchwork motley coat playing a pipe or flute; a streaming horde of rats heading to a river; a line of entranced children following; a mountain or hillside opening to swallow them; a medieval German town. All public domain.

## Symbolism & Themes
The deadly cost of a broken promise; revenge out of all proportion; the hypnotic, dangerous power of music/art; debt, betrayal, and reckoning. An unusually dark and morally ambiguous legend.

## Legal & Cultural Status

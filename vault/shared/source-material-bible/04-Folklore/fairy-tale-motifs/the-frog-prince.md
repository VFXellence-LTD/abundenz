# The Frog Prince

> A princess drops her golden ball into a well; a talking frog retrieves it in exchange for her companionship — and reveals himself to be an enchanted prince.

## Origin & Tradition
A European tale type (ATU 440), fixed by the Brothers Grimm in 1812 as *Der Froschkönig* ("The Frog King"). The Grimm text is public domain. Notably, in the original Grimm version the spell breaks not from a kiss but when the disgusted princess hurls the frog against the wall — the "kiss" is a later, sentimentalized addition. Disney's 2009 *The Princess and the Frog* is a separate copyrighted modern film.

## The Story / The Figure
A princess loses her favorite golden ball in a deep well. A frog offers to fetch it if she will let him eat from her plate and sleep on her pillow. She agrees, then tries to renege. Her father insists she keep her promise. When she finally honors it (throwing the frog, or in later tellings kissing him), the frog transforms into a prince freed from an enchantment.

## Visual Identity
Free, traditional motifs: a frog beside a stone well; a golden ball glinting in dark water; a frog perched on a princess's pillow or plate; a small crown on a frog; lily pads. All public domain.

## Symbolism & Themes
Transformation and the breaking of enchantment; keeping one's word, however distasteful; the lesson that worth can hide behind an unappealing exterior; the gap between revulsion and love.

## Legal & Cultural Status
Tale type and Grimm 1812 text: **public domain.** Restricted: Disney's *The Princess and the Frog* and its characters. Defers to [[../../01-Legal-Guidelines/pd-source-vs-modern-adaptation|PD source vs modern adaptation]].

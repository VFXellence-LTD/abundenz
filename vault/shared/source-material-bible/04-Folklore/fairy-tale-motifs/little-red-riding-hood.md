# Little Red Riding Hood

> A girl in a red hood, a forest, a wolf wearing a grandmother's face — the archetypal cautionary tale of innocence meeting a predator.

## Origin & Tradition
The tale is rooted in European oral tradition, with medieval analogues across France and Italy (some far darker, involving the wolf tricking the girl into eating her grandmother's flesh). Charles Perrault fixed a moralizing version in 1697 (*Le Petit Chaperon Rouge*) that ends grimly with the girl eaten — a warning to young women about charming strangers. The Brothers Grimm's 1812 *Rotkäppchen* added the rescuing huntsman who cuts the wolf open. Both source texts are public domain.

## The Story / The Figure
A girl in a red hooded cloak carries food through the woods to her sick grandmother. A wolf learns her errand, races ahead, devours the grandmother, and disguises itself in her bed. The girl arrives and the famous exchange follows ("what big eyes you have…"). In Perrault's version the wolf eats her; in Grimm's a huntsman saves both. The red hood is her signature.

## Visual Identity
Free, traditional motifs: a scarlet hooded cloak; a wicker basket; a path into dark woods; a wolf in a nightcap and gown in a cottage bed; a woodsman with an axe. These are public-domain visual building blocks. Avoid lifting any specific copyrighted illustration or film design.

## Symbolism & Themes
Innocence versus predation; the danger of straying from the path; deception and disguise; coming-of-age and the loss of naïveté. The red hood reads as both vulnerability and a spark of life against the dark forest.

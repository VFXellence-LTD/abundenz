# Rapunzel

> A girl with impossibly long hair, locked in a doorless tower by a witch, lets down her braid as the only way in — and the only way out.

## Origin & Tradition
A European "maiden in the tower" tale type (ATU 310). Earlier forms include Basile's *Petrosinella* (1634) and Charlotte-Rose de la Force's *Persinette* (1698); the Brothers Grimm fixed *Rapunzel* in 1812. All these source texts are public domain. Disney's 2010 *Tangled* is a separate copyrighted modern film.

## The Story / The Figure
A pregnant woman craves rapunzel (a salad herb) from a witch's walled garden; her husband steals it, and the witch demands the unborn child in payment. The girl, named Rapunzel, is raised and then imprisoned in a doorless, stairless tower, accessible only by climbing her long hair. A prince discovers the method and visits secretly; the witch finds out, cuts the hair, and casts Rapunzel into the wilderness, blinding the prince. They are reunited and his sight is restored by her tears.

## Visual Identity
Free, traditional motifs: a tall, windowless tower in a forest; a cascade of long hair let down from the high window; a witch's walled herb garden; the prince climbing the braid. **Restricted:** Disney's *Tangled* designs (glowing hair, the named characters, the chameleon, the lantern festival).

## Symbolism & Themes
Confinement and the longing for the world outside; an overprotective guardian's love that becomes a cage; the body (hair) as both bond and escape route; blindness and healing as loss and restoration.

## Legal & Cultural Status
Tale type and Basile/de la Force/Grimm source texts: **public domain.** Restricted: Disney's *Tangled* and its specific designs and names. Defers to [[../../01-Legal-Guidelines/pd-source-vs-modern-adaptation|PD source vs modern adaptation]].

# Byzantine Art

> Sacred imagery on shimmering gold: frontal, elongated, otherworldly figures with halos, set in mosaic and icon against a flat heaven of gold leaf.

## Overview
Byzantine art rejected naturalism in favor of the eternal and the divine. Figures are flattened, frontal, and stylized; space is collapsed into a flat gold ground that signifies heaven rather than a real place. Its mosaics and icons are public domain, and its gold-haloed portrait format is endlessly adaptable to a modern "sacred" or "elevated" aesthetic.

## Defining Features
- Flat gold-leaf backgrounds standing for divine, placeless space.
- Frontal, symmetrical, elongated figures with large staring eyes.
- Strict iconographic conventions — saints identifiable by fixed attributes.
- Hierarchy of scale and position; Christ Pantocrator dominating domes.
- Mosaic construction from glittering glass and gold tesserae.

## Visual Vocabulary
Gold ground, halo discs, jeweled borders, deep reds and blues for robes, linear drapery folds rendered as pattern rather than weight, the mandorla (almond of light) around holy figures, faces with elongated noses and solemn gaze.

## Shapes
The halo circle, the dome arc, the mandorla almond, the cross, elongated vertical figures, the rounded arch, tessera grid.

## Textures
Glittering mosaic tesserae, burnished gold leaf, jeweled cloisonné, fresco, the faceted shimmer of glass.

## Palette
The "sacred gold" family: gold, imperial purple, deep lapis blue, vermilion red, ivory, and emerald. Opulent and solemn. See [[08-Color-Palettes/README]].

## Key Motifs
Christ Pantocrator, the haloed saint, gold ground, mandorla, jeweled cross, the dome of heaven, peacocks and vines (paradise), the enthroned figure.

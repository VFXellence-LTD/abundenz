# Greek & Roman (Classical) Art

> The Western ideal of beauty and proportion: marble gods in contrapposto, red-and-black-figure vases, geometric borders, and serene perfected anatomy.

## Overview
Classical art established the proportions, idealized anatomy, and ornamental vocabulary (the column, the meander, the wreath) that the West has returned to for two thousand years. It is the canonical "antiquity" look. Because the works are ancient, the imagery is public domain, and the "marble statue" aesthetic in particular is a dominant, evergreen design trend.

## Defining Features
- Idealized, mathematically proportioned human bodies (the Canon of Polykleitos).
- Contrapposto: weight on one leg, relaxed naturalistic stance.
- Restraint and harmony — calm faces, balanced compositions.
- Vase painting in two systems: black-figure (figures in black silhouette) and red-figure (figures in reserved red on black ground).
- Architectural orders (Doric, Ionic, Corinthian) and ornamental borders.

## Visual Vocabulary
White marble surfaces, crisp silhouette figures on terracotta, geometric repeating borders, symmetrical balanced layouts, profile and three-quarter heads with straight "Greek" noses, drapery rendered in deep parallel folds.

## Shapes
The fluted column, the amphora curve, the meander (Greek key) band, the laurel-wreath circle, the pediment triangle, the contrapposto S-curve of the standing figure.

## Textures
Polished white marble, matte terracotta, mosaic tesserae, fresco plaster, low-relief carving shadow.

## Palette
Two families: "marble" (white, bone, soft grey, gold accent) and "vase" (terracotta red-orange, black, cream). Both read as classical and premium. See [[08-Color-Palettes/README]].

## Key Motifs
Marble bust/head, laurel wreath, Greek-key border, column, amphora, winged Victory (Nike), olive branch, gods and heroes from [[03-Mythology/README]], the discus thrower / athlete.

# Gothic Art & Architecture

> The architecture of light and height: pointed arches, soaring vaults, jewel-like stained glass, and grimacing gargoyles — awe engineered in stone.

## Overview
Gothic art pushed medieval building toward height and light: pointed arches, ribbed vaults, and flying buttresses freed walls to become vast windows of stained glass. Its rose windows, tracery, and gargoyles give a distinctive vocabulary of soaring verticality and dark-romantic detail. All historical Gothic work is public domain.

## Defining Features
- The pointed (lancet) arch — structural and symbolic of reaching upward.
- Stained glass: leaded color filling tall windows, telling stories in light.
- The radial rose window.
- Stone tracery: lacework patterns in window heads and screens.
- Gargoyles and grotesques (functional and decorative).

## Visual Vocabulary
Strong verticals, lattice-like tracery, leaded color planes, radial symmetry in rose windows, elongated figures, ornate finials and crockets, the silhouette of spires and pinnacles against sky.

## Shapes
The pointed arch, the rose-window radial, the trefoil and quatrefoil, the ribbed-vault fan, the spire/finial triangle, tracery lattice, the gargoyle grotesque.

## Textures
Weathered stone, leaded glass, the jewel glow of backlit color, carved relief, lead came lines.

## Palette
Two families: "stained glass" (ruby red, cobalt blue, gold-amber, emerald, on black lead lines) and "stone" (cool grey, slate, charcoal). Dramatic and jewel-rich. See [[08-Color-Palettes/README]].

## Key Motifs
Pointed arch, rose window, stained-glass saints, gargoyle, trefoil/quatrefoil, spire, tracery lattice, the flying buttress, the cathedral silhouette.

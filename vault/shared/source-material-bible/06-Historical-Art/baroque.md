# Baroque Art

> Maximum drama: a single shaft of light tearing across deep darkness, swirling figures caught mid-gesture, gold and spectacle deployed to overwhelm the viewer.

## Overview
Baroque art weaponized emotion and theatricality — strong chiaroscuro (light/dark contrast), dynamic diagonal compositions, and lavish ornament to move the viewer powerfully (often in service of Church and monarchy). Its dramatic spotlight aesthetic and dark-and-gold opulence are public domain and translate directly into moody, premium modern visuals.

## Defining Features
- Tenebrism / chiaroscuro: dramatic spotlight against deep shadow (Caravaggio).
- Dynamic diagonal compositions full of movement.
- Heightened emotion — ecstasy, agony, action caught mid-motion.
- Lavish ornament, gilding, and grandeur.
- Theatrical staging, as if the scene were a frozen play.

## Visual Vocabulary
Deep blacks pierced by warm spotlights, swirling fabric, outstretched gestures, foreshortened bodies bursting toward the viewer, ornate gilded frames, rich textured surfaces, the vanitas still life (skull, flowers, decay).

## Shapes
The diagonal thrust, the spiraling figure, the strong light cone, swirling drapery curves, the ornate cartouche/frame, the dramatic triangle of light.

## Textures
Glossy oil, velvet and silk, metallic gilding, candlelit glow, deep matte shadow, flesh modeled in light.

## Palette
The "tenebrist" family: near-black shadow, warm spotlight gold and amber, deep crimson, ivory flesh, and accents of saturated blue and green. Dark, warm, dramatic. See [[08-Color-Palettes/README]].

## Key Motifs
The chiaroscuro spotlight, vanitas skull-and-flowers, the saint in ecstasy, swirling cherubs, the dramatic gesture, the gilded ornate frame, the candlelit interior.

# Laurel Wreath

> A circlet of bay leaves worn by victors and poets: the oldest and cleanest visual shorthand for honor, achievement, and triumph.

## Who / What
The laurel wreath is a circlet woven from the leaves of the bay laurel tree, worn as a crown to signify victory, honor, and high achievement. Its mythological root is the story of Apollo and Daphne: the nymph Daphne, fleeing Apollo, was transformed into a laurel tree, and Apollo adopted the laurel as his sacred plant. From there it became the prize for victors in the Pythian Games and for triumphant generals and celebrated poets in Greece and Rome (hence "poet laureate" and "Nobel laureate"). It remains one of the most universally understood symbols of excellence and is ubiquitous in awards, badges, and certificates.

## Archetype
The Mark of Victory / Emblem of Honor / Crown of Achievement. The pure visual signal of "you have won / you are recognized."

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A circlet of pointed laurel (bay) leaves worn on the head; or two laurel branches curved into a horseshoe/U-shape framing a central element (the classic award/badge layout); rendered in green or gold. Clean, classical, and symmetrical.

## Symbols
- **Laurel leaves** — victory and honor.
- **The head circlet** — the crown of the victor.
- **The curved branches (badge form)** — the award/seal layout.

## Themes
Victory and triumph, honor and recognition, achievement and excellence, poetry (the poet's crown), status and distinction, peace.

## Reusable Concepts
The universal "achievement" badge form (curved laurel branches — used in countless seals and certificates). The crown of victory. The "laureate" concept (honored creator/winner). Excellence visualized in a single shape. A clean, classical frame for any award or recognition design.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
The myth of Apollo and Daphne and the origin of the sacred laurel. The crowning of victors at the Pythian and other games. The triumphal wreaths of Roman generals. The literary "laureate" tradition.

## Legal & Safety
- **Free to use:** the generic laurel wreath (ancient, public domain).
- **Caution:** do NOT copy specific protected emblems — Olympic laurel imagery and the word "Olympic" (IOC, aggressively protected), official government seals, university/award seals, or anything implying official endorsement. The plain laurel is free; a specific famous logo is not.

# Artemis (Diana)

> The fierce virgin goddess of the hunt and the moon: silver bow drawn, a crescent on her brow, swift through the wild with her deer and hounds.

## Who / What
Artemis is the goddess of the hunt, the wilderness, wild animals, the moon, and chastity, and the protector of young women and children. She is the twin sister of Apollo, daughter of Zeus and Leto. The Romans called her Diana. Fiercely independent, she swore never to marry, roams the forests with a band of nymphs, and is quick to punish those who violate the wild or her honor (Actaeon, who saw her bathing, was turned into a stag and torn apart by his own hounds).

## Archetype
The Huntress / Wild Maiden / Virgin Goddess / Protector. The fierce, free, untamed feminine who answers to no one. A powerful emblem of independence and protective ferocity.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A young, athletic woman in a short hunting tunic; a silver bow drawn or slung; a crescent moon on her brow; a deer or stag beside her; hunting hounds; a quiver of arrows; the moonlit forest as setting.

## Symbols
- **Silver bow and arrow** — the hunt; the "far-shooting" sister of Apollo.
- **Crescent moon** — her lunar aspect.
- **Deer / stag** — her sacred and protected animal.
- **Hunting dog** — her companion.
- **Cypress** and **bear** — sacred to her.

## Themes
The hunt and the wilderness, the moon and the night, fierce independence, protection of the young and vulnerable, chastity and autonomy, swift vengeance against violation.

## Reusable Concepts
The free woman who refuses to be possessed. The protector who is gentle to the innocent and merciless to the predator. The wild that punishes intrusion (Actaeon). The huntress whose aim never misses. Moon-and-forest mysticism.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
The punishment of Actaeon (turned to a stag). The aid she gives to women in childbirth. Her band of virgin huntress-nymphs and the story of Callisto. The Calydonian boar hunt. Her role alongside Apollo in punishing Niobe's pride.

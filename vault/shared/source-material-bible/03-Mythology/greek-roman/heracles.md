# Heracles (Hercules)

> The greatest of Greek heroes: lion-skin on his shoulders, club in his fist, a demigod who paid for a moment of madness with twelve impossible labors.

## Who / What
Heracles (Roman: Hercules) is the strongest and most famous hero of Greek myth, a son of Zeus by a mortal woman, persecuted from birth by the jealous Hera. Driven mad by Hera, he killed his own wife and children; to atone, he served King Eurystheus and performed the Twelve Labors — slaying the Nemean Lion and the Hydra, capturing Cerberus, fetching the apples of the Hesperides, and more. After a lifetime of feats and suffering, he was made immortal and taken to Olympus. His story is the original redemption-through-trial arc.

## Archetype
The Hero / Strongman / Redeemed Sinner / Suffering Champion. The flawed mortal who achieves divinity through endurance and atonement. The template for countless strongman and redemption heroes.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A massive, muscular, bearded man wearing the skin of the Nemean Lion (the lion's head pulled up as a hood); a thick knotted wooden club; sometimes a bow; immense, exaggerated physique; scenes of him wrestling lions, clubbing the Hydra, or hauling Cerberus up from the underworld.

## Symbols
- **Club** — his signature weapon (often a rough, knotted branch).
- **Lion skin** — the pelt of the Nemean Lion he strangled.
- **The Twelve Labors** — a ready-made set of twelve iconic feats.
- **Bow** — used in several labors.
- **Golden apples of the Hesperides** — one of his prizes.

## Themes
Strength and endurance, heroism and its cost, labor and atonement, redemption through suffering, the tension between human flaws and superhuman gifts, mortality earning immortality.

## Reusable Concepts
The strongman who must atone for a terrible act. Twelve discrete, escalating challenges (a perfect episodic / list structure). The hero hounded by a vengeful power he never wronged. Redemption earned through relentless effort. The mortal who becomes a god.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
The infant strangling serpents in his cradle (sent by Hera). The madness and the killing of his family. Each of the Twelve Labors (the Nemean Lion, the Hydra, the Cerynian Hind, the Augean Stables, Cerberus, etc.). His death by the poisoned shirt of Nessus and his apotheosis to Olympus.

## Legal & Safety
- **Free to use:** the mythological Heracles / Hercules, the Twelve Labors, and classical art and texts.
- **Avoid:** Disney's entire Hercules (1997) design and songs, Marvel's Hercules, Kevin Sorbo's "Legendary Journeys" likeness, Sony's God of War Hercules, and the 2014 live-action film designs.

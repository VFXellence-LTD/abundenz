# Cerberus

> The three-headed hound at the gates of the dead: he lets every soul in, but none back out — the ultimate guardian of the ultimate threshold.

## Who / What
Cerberus is the monstrous multi-headed dog (usually three heads, sometimes more) that guards the entrance to the underworld, ensuring the dead cannot leave and the living cannot enter. He has a mane and/or tail of serpents and is the offspring of the monsters Typhon and Echidna. His capture — dragging him up to the surface bare-handed — was the twelfth and final Labor of Heracles. He is the archetypal gatekeeper and threshold guardian.

## Archetype
The Guardian / Threshold Keeper / Loyal Beast / Gatekeeper. The fearsome but faithful sentinel of a boundary that must not be crossed. A clean, powerful symbol of security, vigilance, and the final threshold.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
An enormous, powerful dog with three snarling heads; a mane or tail made of writhing serpents; glowing eyes; planted before great dark gates; sometimes chained or being wrestled into submission by Heracles. Hellish reds and underworld shadow.

## Symbols
- **Three heads** — his defining, instantly readable feature.
- **Serpent mane / tail** — his monstrous nature.
- **The gates of Hades** — the threshold he keeps.
- **Chains** — his (temporary) subjugation by Heracles.

## Themes
Guardianship and vigilance, the threshold and the boundary, the one-way door of death, loyalty (he serves his master absolutely), the impassable gate, security and protection.

## Reusable Concepts
The guardian of the threshold (a universal mythic function). The gate that only opens one way. Triple vigilance (three heads, nothing gets past). The fearsome beast that is simply doing its job. Security personified.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
His parentage among the great monsters. His capture as Heracles's final Labor. Orpheus lulling him with music to enter the underworld. The Sibyl drugging him with honey-cake for Aeneas. His eternal, sleepless watch.

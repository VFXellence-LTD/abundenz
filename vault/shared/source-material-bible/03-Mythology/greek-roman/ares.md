# Ares (Mars)

> The brutal god of war and bloodlust: spear dripping, war-dogs at his heels, the embodiment of battle's chaos and carnage.

## Who / What
Ares is the god of war in its rawest, bloodiest form — violence, slaughter, and the chaos of the battlefield, in contrast to Athena's disciplined strategy. He is the son of Zeus and Hera, disliked even by his own father. The Romans recast him far more favorably as Mars, a dignified father of the nation. He is the lover of Aphrodite and, in many myths, a figure of brute force who is often outwitted or humiliated.

## Archetype
The Warrior / Berserker / God of Violence. Often the antagonist or the cautionary figure — power without wisdom. As Roman Mars, he becomes the noble protector-warrior, which is a useful softer variant.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A heavily armored warrior; a crested helmet; a bloodied spear and shield; war-dogs and vultures (his carrion birds) around him; a battlefield strewn with the fallen; a fierce, scarred expression. Bronze and blood-red palette.

## Symbols
- **Spear** — his weapon and emblem.
- **Helmet and shield** — war.
- **Vulture and dog** — his carrion-feeding sacred animals.
- **Burning torch** and **boar** — destruction and ferocity.
- The astronomical/alchemical symbol of Mars (♂) derives from him.

## Themes
War and bloodlust, courage and recklessness, rage and chaos, raw masculinity, the difference between brave and brutal, the consequences of unchecked aggression.

## Reusable Concepts
Force without strategy (Ares vs. Athena). The brute who is repeatedly outwitted. The Roman rehabilitation into noble Mars (a redemption arc baked into history). War personified as ugly rather than glorious.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
His humiliating capture in Hephaestus's net while in bed with Aphrodite. His defeats at the hands of mortals and Athena. His Roman elevation as father of Romulus and Remus. His role stirring conflict in the Trojan War.

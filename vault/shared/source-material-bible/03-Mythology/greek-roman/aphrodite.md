# Aphrodite (Venus)

> The goddess of love and beauty, born from sea foam: doves at her shoulders, roses at her feet, beauty so total that gods and mortals alike fall before it.

## Who / What
Aphrodite is the goddess of love, beauty, pleasure, and desire, born (in one famous account) from the sea foam where the severed parts of Uranus fell into the ocean. The Romans called her Venus. Her beauty is irresistible, her magic girdle compels desire, and her favor (or wrath) drives much of myth — it is her promise of the most beautiful woman that triggers the Trojan War. She is married to Hephaestus but loves Ares.

## Archetype
The Lover / Goddess of Beauty / Seductress. The power of attraction itself, capable of making and unmaking heroes. Also a study in vanity and the disruptive force of desire.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A beautiful woman, nude or lightly draped, rising from a scallop shell on the sea; doves circling; roses and myrtle; flowing hair; a mirror; sea foam; idealized classical proportions. Botticelli's PD painting is a direct safe reference.

## Symbols
- **Seashell / scallop** — her sea-foam birth.
- **Dove** — her sacred bird; love and peace.
- **Rose and myrtle** — her sacred flowers and plant.
- **Mirror** — beauty and the Venus symbol (♀).
- **Pearl and swan** — beauty and grace.

## Themes
Love and desire, beauty and its power, passion and seduction, vanity, fertility, the disruptive and creative force of attraction, the sea-born and the sublime.

## Reusable Concepts
Beauty as a literal superpower. The promise that starts a war (the Judgment of Paris). The mismatched marriage (to the crippled smith, while loving the war god). Desire as a force that overrides reason. The birth from chaos into perfect form.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
The Judgment of Paris and the spark of the Trojan War. Her affair with Ares and the net of Hephaestus. The story of Pygmalion and Galatea (she brings the statue to life). Her love for the mortal Adonis. Her aid to Aeneas (her son) in Roman myth.

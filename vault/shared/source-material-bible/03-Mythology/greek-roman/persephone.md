# Persephone (Proserpina)

> The maiden of spring who became queen of the dead: half her year among the blossoms, half on the throne of the underworld — the living bridge between life and death.

## Who / What
Persephone is the daughter of Demeter and Zeus, goddess of spring growth, and — after her abduction by Hades — queen of the underworld. The Romans called her Proserpina. Her dual existence (above ground with her mother in the warm half of the year, below with Hades in the cold half) is the Greek explanation for the seasons. Having eaten pomegranate seeds in the underworld, she is bound to return there, making her the only being equally at home among the living and the dead.

## Archetype
The Maiden / Queen of Two Worlds / The Reborn / Bridge Between Life and Death. A figure of transformation: the innocent flower-gatherer who becomes a sovereign of the dead, embodying the cycle of growth, death, and return.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A young woman often depicted split or balanced between sunlit spring flowers and the shadowed underworld; a pomegranate (frequently shown with its seeds, six of which bound her); narcissus and spring blossoms; a torch; enthroned beside Hades; a palette divided between vivid bloom and cool shadow.

## Symbols
- **Pomegranate** — the seeds that bind her to the underworld; her central emblem.
- **Narcissus** — the flower she reached for when abducted.
- **Spring flowers** — her aspect of growth and renewal.
- **Torch** — light carried into darkness.
- **Sheaf of grain** — her link to Demeter and the harvest.

## Themes
Spring and rebirth, the cycle of the seasons, duality (innocence and sovereignty, life and death), transformation and coming-of-age, the bittersweet, growth out of darkness, agency claimed within constraint.

## Reusable Concepts
The maiden who becomes a queen of the dark. The duality figure who belongs to two opposed worlds. The seasons as a divided life. Coming-of-age through descent. The pomegranate as the fruit that binds. Light carried into darkness and darkness softened by light.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
Her abduction by Hades while gathering flowers. The eating of the pomegranate seeds that seals her fate. The bargain (struck by Demeter, Hades, and Zeus) that splits her year and creates the seasons. Her later role as a powerful, dignified queen rather than a mere captive.

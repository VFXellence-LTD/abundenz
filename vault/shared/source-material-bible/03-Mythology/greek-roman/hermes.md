# Hermes (Mercury)

> The swift messenger of the gods: winged sandals flashing, caduceus in hand, the patron of travelers, traders, tricksters, and the road between worlds.

## Who / What
Hermes is the messenger of the gods and the patron of travel, commerce, communication, boundaries, thieves, and luck. He guides souls to the underworld (a psychopomp), invented the lyre as an infant, and is the quickest-witted of the Olympians. The Romans called him Mercury. He is the divine go-between — at home on every threshold, equally comfortable with gods, mortals, and the dead.

## Archetype
The Messenger / Trickster / Traveler / Guide / Psychopomp. The clever, mobile connector who crosses every boundary. One of mythology's great trickster figures (compare Loki, Anansi, Coyote).

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A lithe, youthful man with winged sandals (*talaria*) and a winged cap (*petasos*); the caduceus (a staff with two entwined snakes and wings); a traveler's cloak; often shown in motion or mid-stride; a sly, alert expression.

## Symbols
- **Caduceus** — his winged, twin-serpent staff (see [[caduceus]]).
- **Winged sandals and helmet** — speed and flight.
- **Tortoise** — from whose shell he made the first lyre.
- **Rooster** — herald of the dawn.
- **Purse** — commerce and luck.

## Themes
Travel and motion, communication and language, commerce and exchange, cleverness and theft, boundaries and thresholds, transitions (including the final one to death), luck and opportunity.

## Reusable Concepts
The go-between who connects separated parties. The trickster who charms his way out of trouble (he stole Apollo's cattle as a newborn and talked his way to forgiveness). The guide across thresholds. The inventor (the lyre). Speed personified.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
The infant Hermes stealing Apollo's cattle and inventing the lyre on his first day alive. His role guiding souls to Hades. His countless errands for Zeus (slaying Argus to free Io). His patronage of merchants, travelers, and thieves alike.

## Legal & Safety
- **Free to use:** the mythological Hermes / Mercury and classical art and texts.
- **Avoid:** Percy Jackson's Hermes, Smite, the FTD Florist Mercury logo (a registered trademark). "Mercury" is a heavily used commercial name (cars, insurance, etc.) — check trademark in your product category.

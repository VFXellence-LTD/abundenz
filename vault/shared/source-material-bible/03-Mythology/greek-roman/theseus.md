# Theseus

> The hero who walked into the labyrinth and walked back out: a sword to slay the Minotaur, and a single thread to find his way home.

## Who / What
Theseus is the great hero and legendary founder-king of Athens. His most famous deed is entering the Labyrinth of Crete — the inescapable maze built by Daedalus to hold the monstrous Minotaur — slaying the beast, and finding his way back out using a ball of thread given to him by the princess Ariadne. He unified Attica under Athens and performed many other feats, but he is also remembered for his flaws: he abandoned Ariadne on Naxos, and forgot to change his ship's black sails to white, causing his father Aegeus to despair and leap to his death (giving the Aegean Sea its name).

## Archetype
The Hero / Founder-King / Maze-Solver / Ungrateful Lover. The hero whose triumph (the maze) is matched by his failures (abandonment, forgetfulness). A more morally complicated hero than Perseus or Heracles.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A young hero in the winding corridors of the labyrinth; a ball or unspooling line of thread; a raised sword over the fallen bull-headed Minotaur; the iconic labyrinth/maze pattern itself (an ancient, instantly readable symbol); the Athenian ship with black or white sails.

## Symbols
- **Thread of Ariadne** — the guide out of the maze; one of myth's great metaphors.
- **The Labyrinth / maze pattern** — a powerful standalone graphic.
- **Sword** — the slaying of the Minotaur.
- **Black and white sails** — the fatal forgetting.

## Themes
Heroism and its flaws, the labyrinth as trial, cunning and the guiding thread, civilization and city-founding, coming-of-age, abandonment and ingratitude, the cost of forgetting.

## Reusable Concepts
"The thread that leads you out of the maze" — a perfect metaphor for guidance, mentorship, and problem-solving. The inescapable labyrinth. The hero who wins the quest but fails the people who helped him. The founder who builds something larger than himself.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
The voyage to Crete with the sacrificial youths. Ariadne's gift of the thread and his slaying of the Minotaur. The abandonment of Ariadne on Naxos. The black-sails tragedy and his father's death. His unification of Athens.

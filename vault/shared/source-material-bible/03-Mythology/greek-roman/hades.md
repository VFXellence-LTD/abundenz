# Hades (Pluto)

> The stern lord of the underworld: master of the dead and of the riches beneath the earth, ruling the realm no living soul escapes.

## Who / What
Hades is the god of the underworld and ruler of the dead, brother to Zeus and Poseidon. When the three brothers divided the cosmos, he drew the realm below. The Romans called him Pluto ("the rich one," because all the earth's mineral wealth lies underground). Importantly, Hades is not evil — he is a grim but just keeper of the dead, rarely leaving his realm. His most famous act is abducting Persephone to be his queen. The Greeks feared even to speak his name.

## Archetype
The Lord of the Dead / Unseen Ruler / Grim Host / Stern Judge. The somber, dutiful keeper of the inevitable — power as burden rather than glory. A frequently *misunderstood* figure ripe for sympathetic reinterpretation.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A stern, dark-bearded man on a throne of bone or shadow; a bident (two-pronged staff, his counterpart to Poseidon's trident); the three-headed hound Cerberus at his feet; dark robes; the rivers Styx and Lethe; a gloomy palace; the cypress and narcissus. A "helm of darkness" that grants invisibility.

## Symbols
- **Bident** — his two-pronged staff.
- **Helm of Darkness** — grants invisibility (he lent it to Perseus).
- **Cerberus** — the three-headed guard dog (see [[cerberus]]).
- **Cypress and narcissus** — his sacred plants.
- **Key and scepter** — he holds the keys to the realm none escape.
- **Black ram** — sacrificed to him.

## Themes
Death and the afterlife, the inevitability of the end, hidden wealth, the unseen, stern justice, duty over glory, the misjudged keeper, the dignity of necessary work.

## Reusable Concepts
The keeper of the inevitable who does a thankless job well. The "villain" who isn't actually evil. Wealth that comes from below (Pluto = riches). The bargain over Persephone that orders the year. The realm everyone visits eventually. Death as duty, not malice.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
The abduction of Persephone and the bargain that creates the seasons. His lending of the Helm of Darkness to Perseus. Orpheus descending to plead for Eurydice. The lack of escape from his realm (the rare exceptions). His near-total absence from Olympus.

## Legal & Safety
- **Free to use:** the mythological Hades / Pluto and classical art and texts.
- **Avoid:** Disney's blue-flame-haired Hades (Hercules 1997), Supergiant Games' entire "Hades" game and its art, Sony's God of War Hades, Percy Jackson's Hades, Smite. Do NOT conflate Hades with the Christian Satan/devil — that's a different, misleading depiction.

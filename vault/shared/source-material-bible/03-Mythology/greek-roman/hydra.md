# Lernaean Hydra

> The serpent you cannot kill by cutting: sever one head and two grow back — the monster that is also a perfect metaphor for any problem that multiplies when you attack it.

## Who / What
The Hydra is a gigantic, many-headed water serpent that lived in the swamps of Lerna. Its defining horror is regeneration: cut off one head and two grow in its place, and one of its heads is immortal. Its blood and breath are deadly poison. Slaying it was the second Labor of Heracles, who solved the regeneration problem by having his nephew Iolaus cauterize each neck-stump with fire the instant a head was cut, then burying the immortal head under a rock. Heracles dipped his arrows in its venom, making them lethal forever after.

## Archetype
The Monster / Unkillable Foe / Multiplying Problem / Swamp Serpent. The enemy that gets worse the more directly you fight it — one of mythology's most useful and reusable conceptual monsters.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A massive serpent body with many long necks and snapping heads; heads visibly regrowing from severed stumps; dripping venom; rising from a dark, fetid swamp; Heracles and Iolaus cauterizing the necks with torches. Murky greens and toxic colors.

## Symbols
- **Many heads** — the multiplying threat.
- **Regrowing heads** — "cut one, two grow back."
- **Venom** — its deadly blood and breath.
- **The immortal head** — the part that can't be destroyed, only buried.
- **The swamp** — its lair.

## Themes
Regeneration and persistence, the problem that multiplies when attacked, poison and corruption, the impossible foe, the need for a clever rather than brute-force solution, resilience.

## Reusable Concepts
"Cut off one head and two grow back" — a perfect metaphor for self-multiplying problems (misinformation, bureaucracy, threats, addictions). The need to change tactics, not try harder. The immortal head you can only contain, not eliminate. The poison that becomes a weapon. Resilience and regrowth (positive framing).

## Story / Narrative Hooks
The second Labor of Heracles. The cauterizing solution with Iolaus. The burying of the immortal head. The poisoning of Heracles's arrows with Hydra venom (which later indirectly causes his own death). The crab Hera sent to help the Hydra (becoming the constellation Cancer).

# Odysseus (Ulysses)

> The man of twists and turns: ten years to win Troy by cunning, ten more to fight his way home — past sirens, cyclopes, and the wrath of the sea itself.

## Who / What
Odysseus (Roman: Ulysses) is the cleverest of the Greek heroes, king of Ithaca, and the protagonist of Homer's *Odyssey*. He devised the Trojan Horse that won the decade-long war, then spent another ten years struggling home, blinded the cyclops Polyphemus (earning Poseidon's wrath), resisted the Sirens by lashing himself to the mast, survived Scylla and Charybdis, the witch Circe, and the lotus-eaters, and finally returned in disguise to reclaim his wife Penelope and his throne. His weapon is his wit.

## Archetype
The Trickster-Hero / Wanderer / Clever Survivor / Faithful Returner. The hero who wins by intelligence, patience, and deception rather than strength — the original "long journey home."

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A weathered, bearded mariner; lashed to the mast of his ship as he sails past the Sirens; the wooden Trojan Horse; stringing the great bow that only he can bend; the long sea voyage past monsters; a conical sailor's cap (*pilos*). Wine-dark seas and a single battered ship.

## Symbols
- **The Trojan Horse** — his masterstroke of deception.
- **The great bow** — that only he can string (the test of identity on his return).
- **Ship and mast** — the voyage; the mast he was bound to.
- **Olive-tree bed** — the immovable marriage bed that proves his identity to Penelope.
- **The sea** — both the path and the antagonist.

## Themes
Cunning over strength, the long journey home (the literal "odyssey"), perseverance and endurance, temptation resisted, longing and homecoming, disguise and revealed identity, fidelity and patience.

## Reusable Concepts
The "odyssey" itself — the archetypal long, trial-filled journey home (a universal content framework). Winning by wits, not might. Resisting temptation (Sirens, lotus, Circe). The disguised return and the test of identity. Each episode a self-contained monster/peril (perfect for serialized content).

## Story / Narrative Hooks
The Trojan Horse. The blinding of Polyphemus ("Nobody" sent me). The Sirens and the lashing to the mast. Scylla and Charybdis. Circe's island and the men turned to swine. The lotus-eaters. The disguised return and the slaughter of the suitors.

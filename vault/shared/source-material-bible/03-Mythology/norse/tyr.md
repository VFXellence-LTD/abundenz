# Tyr (Týr)

> The bravest of the gods, who placed his sword-hand in the jaws of the monstrous wolf to bind it — and lost the hand to keep his word. The god of courage, justice, and the price of honor.

## Who / What
Tyr is the Norse god of war, justice, courage, and the upholding of oaths. His defining myth is the binding of Fenrir: the gods knew the monstrous wolf was fated to grow strong enough to destroy them, so they tricked it into being bound with a magical fetter. But Fenrir, suspecting treachery, would only allow itself to be bound if one of the gods placed a hand in its mouth as a pledge of good faith. Only Tyr was brave and honorable enough to do so. When the wolf realized it had been tricked and could not break free, it bit off Tyr's hand. Tyr thus becomes the supreme symbol of self-sacrifice for the greater good and of keeping a pledge at terrible personal cost. He is fated to fall fighting the hound Garmr at Ragnarök.

## Archetype
The Honorable Warrior / Self-Sacrificer / Keeper of Oaths / Just God. Courage defined not as fearlessness but as doing the necessary, costly thing — the hand given to bind the wolf.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A steadfast, one-handed warrior; the stump of his right hand; the moment of placing his hand in the wolf's open jaws; calm courage in the face of certain loss; a spear or sword; the bound wolf Fenrir nearby.

## Symbols
- **The missing right hand** — his sacrifice; his defining mark.
- **The bound wolf** — what his sacrifice achieved.
- **The spear and sword** — war and justice.
- **The Tiwaz rune (↑)** — his rune (see caution).

## Themes
Courage as costly duty, self-sacrifice for the greater good, justice and honor, the given word kept at any price, war, the necessary betrayal nobly shouldered.

## Reusable Concepts
"Putting your hand in the wolf's mouth" — taking on personal risk so others are protected (a powerful leadership/integrity metaphor). Courage as sacrifice, not bravado. Keeping a pledge at terrible cost. The just figure who does the hard right thing. The hero defined by what he gave up.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
The binding of Fenrir and the loss of his hand. His role as a god of justice and the assembly (the *thing*). His feeding of the wolf in its youth (the only one brave enough). His fated death at Ragnarök against the hound Garmr.

## Legal & Safety
- **Free to use:** the mythological Tyr and the Eddas.
- **Avoid:** Sony's God of War Ragnarök Tyr, Smite.
- **Caution:** the Tiwaz rune has been appropriated by some extremist groups — root all imagery firmly in the honorable-sacrifice myth and avoid the bare rune where it could be misread.

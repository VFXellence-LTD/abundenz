# Loki

> The shapeshifting trickster of the gods: charming, cunning, and impossible to pin down — equal parts comic mischief-maker and the catalyst of the gods' doom.

## Who / What
Loki is the Norse trickster — a shapeshifter and schemer who is counted among the gods yet is half-giant by blood, and whose loyalties are forever in question. He helps the gods as often as he harms them, talking them out of disasters of his own making. He can change shape and even sex (he became a mare and gave birth to Odin's eight-legged horse Sleipnir), and is the parent of three monstrous beings: the wolf Fenrir, the world-serpent Jörmungandr, and Hel, ruler of the dead. His engineering of the death of the beloved god Baldr marks his turn from mischief to malice; the gods bind him beneath a venom-dripping serpent until he breaks free to lead the forces of chaos at Ragnarök.

## Archetype
The Trickster / Shapeshifter / Agent of Chaos / Betrayer / Catalyst. One of world mythology's great trickster figures (compare Hermes, Anansi, Coyote), but darker — the necessary disruptor whose chaos eventually turns catastrophic.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A lithe, sly, mischievous figure of shifting and ambiguous form; fire motifs (later folklore links him to fire); mid-transformation into a salmon, mare, fly, or falcon; bound beneath the dripping serpent; an inviting, untrustworthy smile. Note: the mythological Loki has **no horned helmet** — that is a modern (largely Marvel/opera) invention, so it is also the thing to avoid.

## Symbols
- **The shapeshifter** — fluid form, fluid identity.
- **Fire** — his later folkloric association.
- **The dripping serpent and bonds** — his punishment.
- **The mistletoe dart** — the weapon he engineers to kill Baldr.
- **Fluid gender** — a genuinely unusual mythic trait.

## Themes
Trickery and cunning, chaos and disruption, shapeshifting and changeable identity, ambiguity and divided loyalty, betrayal, the catalyst whose meddling drives every story.

## Reusable Concepts
The charming trickster you can't trust but can't look away from. The catalyst whose chaos moves the plot. The shapeshifter / changeable identity (rich for storytelling). The "necessary evil" within a group. Fluid gender as an ancient, mythologically-grounded trait (for inclusive content). The mischief-maker who goes too far.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
His cycle of causing trouble then cleverly fixing it (the theft and recovery of Idunn's apples; the building of Asgard's wall). His shapeshifting into a mare and the birth of Sleipnir. His engineering of Baldr's death. His binding beneath the serpent. His leading of the chaos-forces at Ragnarök.

## Legal & Safety
- **Free to use:** the mythological Loki and the Eddas (including his shapeshifting and gender fluidity).
- **Avoid:** Marvel/Disney's Loki (Hiddleston likeness, horned green-gold costume, the "Loki" series), Sony's God of War Loki/Atreus, Smite. The horned-helmet look is a modern invention — avoid it as it reads as Marvel/opera.

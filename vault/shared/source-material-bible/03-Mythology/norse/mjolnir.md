# Mjölnir (Thor's Hammer)

> Thor's hammer: a weapon that levels mountains and always returns to his hand, but also a blessing-tool and a protective amulet worn for a thousand years. Free in the myth — but a minefield if you copy the Marvel prop.

## Who / What
Mjölnir is the hammer of the thunder-god Thor, forged by dwarves. It never misses its target and always returns to Thor's hand when thrown, and with it Thor defends gods and humans against the giants. Crucially, Mjölnir was not only a weapon but a tool of **blessing and protection** — it was used to hallow marriages, births, and oaths. Viking-age people wore small Mjölnir amulets (the classic short-handled, broad-headed pendant, often with interlace engraving) for protection, and many such amulets survive — making that archaeological style genuinely public domain. Today the hammer is also a recognized religious symbol of modern Heathenry/Ásatrú.

## Archetype
The Protective Talisman / Weapon of the Defender / Symbol of Faith. A symbol of protection and strength far older and broader than its role as a weapon.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A stout, **short-handled** hammer with a broad, roughly squarish head; rendered as a silver pendant with Norse interlace/knotwork engraving (the archaeological style — clearly PD and clearly *not* Marvel); lightning; weathered iron and silver. **Avoid** the long-handled, smooth, film-prop Marvel design and its "Whosoever holds this hammer..." engraving.

## Symbols
- **The short-handled hammer** — protection and strength (use the archaeological proportions).
- **The thunderbolt** — Thor's power.
- **Interlace / knotwork engraving** — authentic Viking-age decoration.
- **The protective amulet form** — the pendant worn for protection.

## Themes
Protection and strength, thunder, blessing and the sacred (marriages, oaths), the weapon that returns, faith and heritage, defiance against chaos.

## Reusable Concepts
The protective amulet (jewelry, "protection" branding). "Always returns to your hand" — a loyalty/dependability metaphor (what you throw out comes back to you). Strength and protection combined. The blessing-tool (not just a weapon). Heritage and Viking-age authenticity (using the archaeological, not cinematic, look).

## Story / Narrative Hooks
The forging by the dwarves (with the too-short handle, the result of Loki's interference as a biting fly). The theft of Mjölnir and Thor's recovery of it (disguised as a bride). Its use to hallow and bless. Its role in every battle against the giants.

## Legal & Safety
- **Free to use:** the mythological Mjölnir and archaeological Viking-age hammer amulets (the short-handled, knotwork pendant style).
- **AVOID (critical):** the Marvel/Disney Mjolnir design (the specific prop, proportions, and "Whosoever holds this hammer" engraving) — it reads instantly as Marvel and is protected IP. Avoid Sony's God of War hammer.
- **Caution & respect:** occasionally co-opted by extremist groups (keep designs in the heritage/protective tradition); also a living religious symbol for Heathenry — treat respectfully.

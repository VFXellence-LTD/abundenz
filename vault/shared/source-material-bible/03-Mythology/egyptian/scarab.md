# Scarab (Khepri)

> The humble dung beetle that the Egyptians watched roll a ball of dung and saw the sun being pushed across the sky — and so made it the great symbol of rebirth, the rising dawn, and transformation.

## Who / What
The scarab is the sacred beetle of ancient Egypt, identified with the god Khepri, an aspect of the rising morning sun. Egyptians observed the dung beetle rolling a ball of dung across the ground and laying its eggs within it (so new beetles seemed to emerge spontaneously from the ball), and they connected this to the sun being rolled across the sky each day and to self-creation and rebirth. The scarab thus became one of Egypt's most important symbols of rebirth, regeneration, transformation, the rising sun, and renewal. Scarab amulets were enormously popular — worn for protection and good luck in life, and the large "heart scarab" was placed on the mummy's chest in death. The winged scarab, holding a sun disk between its forelegs, is an especially powerful and beautiful motif.

## Archetype
The Symbol of Rebirth / Solar Pusher / Transformation Emblem / Lucky Charm. Renewal, transformation, and the daily fresh start in a single small, beloved emblem.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A stylized beetle seen from above; often pushing or holding a sun disk between its forelegs; sometimes with outstretched, feathered wings (the winged scarab); rendered in blue-green faience or gold; as an amulet, seal, or pectoral. Clean, symmetrical, and adaptable.

## Symbols
- **The dung beetle** — self-creation and rebirth.
- **The sun pushed across the sky** — the daily renewal (Khepri).
- **The winged scarab** — protection and the soul.
- **The heart scarab amulet** — placed on the dead for the afterlife.
- **The rising sun** — the morning, the fresh start.

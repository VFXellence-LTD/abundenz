# Thoth (Djehuty)

> The ibis-headed god of wisdom, writing, and the moon: the divine scribe who invented hieroglyphs, recorded the judgment of every soul, and mediated the quarrels of the gods.

## Who / What
Thoth (Egyptian *Djehuty*) is the ancient Egyptian god of wisdom, writing, knowledge, science, magic, the moon, and the measurement of time. He is credited with inventing hieroglyphic writing and is the patron of scribes, scholars, and libraries. As the divine record-keeper, he stands at the weighing of the heart in the afterlife and writes down the verdict. He is the impartial mediator who settles disputes among the gods, and the keeper of the sacred knowledge of the universe. He is depicted as a man with the head of an ibis (a wading bird with a long curved beak), holding a scribe's reed pen and palette, often with a crescent moon; he can also appear as a baboon. He embodies the calm, meticulous intellect.

## Archetype
The Scribe / Sage / Keeper of Knowledge / Divine Mediator. The patron of writing, learning, and impartial record — intellect and wisdom personified.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A man with the head of an ibis (sleek, with a long down-curved beak); holding a scribe's reed pen and a rectangular palette; a crescent moon or moon disk on his head; recording at the scales of judgment; sometimes seated as a baboon. Gold and profile-art style.

## Symbols
- **The ibis head** — his defining feature.
- **The scribe's palette and reed pen** — writing and record-keeping.
- **The crescent moon** — his lunar aspect and timekeeping.
- **The baboon** — his alternate form.
- **Hieroglyphs** — the writing he invented.

## Themes
Wisdom and knowledge, writing and record-keeping, the moon and time, science and measurement, magic and sacred knowledge, impartial mediation, intellect and learning.

The wise mediator who settles disputes. The inventor of writing. The calm intellect. The keeper of all knowledge.

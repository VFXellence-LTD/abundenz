# Isis (Aset)

> The great mother and mistress of magic, who gathered her murdered husband's scattered body, restored him to life by her power, and protected her infant son until he could claim his throne.

## Who / What
Isis (Egyptian *Aset*) is one of the most important goddesses of ancient Egypt — goddess of magic, motherhood, healing, and protection, and the archetypal devoted wife. Her central myth: when her husband-brother Osiris was murdered and dismembered by their brother Set, Isis searched the land, gathered the scattered pieces of his body, and used her unmatched magic to restore him long enough to conceive their son Horus. She then hid and protected the infant Horus in the marshes until he grew strong enough to avenge his father and claim the throne. Her cult spread across the entire Greco-Roman world. She is depicted with great protective wings, a throne-shaped headdress, or a sun disk between cow horns. **Important: use the name "Aset" or clear mythological framing — the bare word "Isis" now overwhelmingly evokes the terrorist group and is dangerous as a brand name.**

## Archetype
The Great Mother / Devoted Wife / Sorceress / Protector / Resurrector. The supreme nurturing-yet-powerful feminine — devotion, magic, and protective love combined.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A regal woman with a throne-shaped headdress (her name means "throne") or a sun disk between cow horns; great feathered wings outstretched in protection; cradling or shielding the infant Horus; kneeling to gather the body of Osiris; the *tyet* knot (the "knot of Isis," a protective amulet). Gold, profile-art style.

## Symbols
- **The throne headdress** — her name and identity.
- **Outstretched winged arms** — protection and shelter.
- **The tyet (knot of Isis)** — a protective amulet.
- **The infant Horus** — her motherhood.
- **The sun disk and horns** — her later, merged iconography.

## Themes
Magic and power, motherhood and devotion, healing and protection, resurrection and restoration, the faithful wife, resilience through grief, the sheltering feminine.

## Reusable Concepts
The protective great mother (winged shelter — a powerful, warm image). "Gathered the pieces and made him whole again" (restoration, healing, putting back together — a deep metaphor). Devotion that conquers death. The mother who protects the vulnerable until they can stand. Magic as nurturing power.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
The murder and dismemberment of Osiris and her search for his body. Her magical restoration of him and conception of Horus. The hiding and protecting of the infant Horus in the marshes. The trick by which she learned Ra's secret name and gained power. The spread of her cult across the ancient world.

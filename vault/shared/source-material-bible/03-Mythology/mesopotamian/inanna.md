# Inanna (Ishtar)

> The Queen of Heaven, goddess of both love and war, who descended through seven gates into the land of the dead — surrendering a piece of herself at each — and returned transformed. The oldest and most powerful dual-natured goddess.

## Who / What
Inanna (Akkadian: Ishtar) is the great Mesopotamian goddess of love, beauty, sex, fertility, war, and political power — the "Queen of Heaven." She is fierce and passionate, embodying the union of love and war (the same energy drives both). Her most famous and influential myth is the *Descent of Inanna*: she journeys into the underworld ruled by her sister Ereshkigal, passing through seven gates, at each of which she must surrender one of her garments or symbols of power, until she arrives naked and powerless and is killed. She is rescued and returns to the world — but the underworld's law demands a substitute, and she must choose someone to take her place. This descent-and-return is one of the oldest and most resonant transformation myths in human culture. She is depicted with a horned crown, standing on lions, beneath an eight-pointed star.

## Archetype
The Love-and-War Goddess / Descender / Dual Feminine / Queen of Heaven. The fierce, complex goddess who holds opposites together — and the prototype of every "death and return" transformation story.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A powerful goddess crowned with a horned headdress; standing on or flanked by lions (her sacred animal); an eight-pointed star (her emblem) above her; sometimes winged; sometimes shown shedding garments at the gates of the underworld. Lapis, gold, and Mesopotamian relief style.

## Symbols
- **The eight-pointed star** — her emblem; the planet Venus.
- **The lion** — her power and ferocity.
- **The horned crown** — her divinity.
- **The rosette** — her sacred flower-symbol.
- **The reed bundle** — an early Sumerian sign of her.

## Themes
Love and war united, the descent into darkness and the return, power and its loss, transformation, the dual feminine, desire and ferocity, death and rebirth.

## Reusable Concepts
The descent-and-return arc (shedding everything, dying, and coming back transformed — one of the most powerful self-transformation metaphors there is). Love and war as one energy (a non-stereotyped, fierce goddess). "Stripped at each gate" (letting go of what you cling to; the journey down). The eight-pointed star (a clean, ancient, beautiful emblem). The dual feminine.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
The Descent of Inanna through the seven gates. Her death and rescue in the underworld. Her choosing of a substitute (her husband Dumuzi) to take her place. Her many tales of love and conflict. Her demand that Gilgamesh marry her (and his refusal).

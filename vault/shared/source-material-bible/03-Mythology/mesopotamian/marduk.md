# Marduk

> The champion of the gods who slew the chaos-dragon Tiamat and built the entire world from her body — winning the kingship of heaven and the right to order all things.

## Who / What
Marduk is the chief god of Babylon and the hero of the *Enuma Elish*, the Babylonian creation epic. When the primordial chaos-dragon Tiamat threatened to destroy the gods, none dared face her — until the young Marduk agreed, on the condition that he be made king of the gods if he won. Armed with a bow, a net, the four winds, and a storm-chariot, he confronted Tiamat, caught her in his net, drove the winds into her, and split her in two — forming the sky from one half and the earth from the other. He then ordered the cosmos, set the stars and seasons, and created humankind. He is depicted with his snake-dragon companion, the *Mushhushshu* (which famously appears on the real Ishtar Gate), and holds the spade (*marru*) and the Tablet of Destinies. He embodies the triumph of order over chaos and the champion who earns his crown.

## Archetype
The Champion / Order-Bringer / King of Gods / Chaos-Slayer. The hero who defeats primordial chaos and builds order from it — and earns supreme power by being the one who dared.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A regal, powerful warrior-god armed with a bow, a net, and the winds; riding a storm-chariot; accompanied by the snake-dragon Mushhushshu (a slender dragon with a scaly body, a forked tongue, and a long neck — free ancient iconography from the Ishtar Gate); splitting the chaos-dragon Tiamat; the spade (marru) symbol. Lapis blue and gold relief style.

## Symbols
- **The spade (marru)** — his emblem.
- **The Mushhushshu dragon** — his companion creature (from the real Ishtar Gate).
- **The net and the four winds** — his weapons against Tiamat.
- **The Tablet of Destinies** — control over fate.
- **The bow** — set in the sky as a constellation.

## Themes
Order over chaos, kingship and the earned crown, creation and the ordering of the cosmos, the champion who dares, victory, power and responsibility, the world built from a defeated foe.

## Reusable Concepts
"Order from chaos" (the creation of structure from disorder — a deep, marketable theme). The champion who earns the crown by being the one who dared (leadership, courage, stepping up). The world built from a slain monster (transformation of the destructive into the constructive). The dragon-slayer. The hero's condition ("make me king if I win").

The threat of Tiamat and the gods' fear. Marduk's bargain (kingship for victory). The battle and the splitting of Tiamat to form sky and earth. The ordering of the cosmos and the creation of humanity. The "fifty names" of Marduk (his many titles).

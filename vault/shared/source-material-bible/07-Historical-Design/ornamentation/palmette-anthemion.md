# Palmette & Anthemion

> The radiating fan of stylized leaves — a palm or honeysuckle blossom spread like a peacock's tail — used as a crowning ornament and as a rhythmic running border across Egyptian and Greek design.

## Defining Features
A symmetrical, fan-shaped cluster of narrow leaves or petals radiating from a common base, often springing from paired volutes. The **palmette** is the single fan; the **anthemion** is the honeysuckle/palmette band where palmettes alternate with lotus or bud forms along a scrolling line. It frequently crowns the peak of a pediment (as an *acroterion*) or runs as a decorative frieze.

## Visual Vocabulary
- **Palmette** — the radiating fan unit.
- **Anthemion band** — alternating palmette-and-lotus running border.
- **Volute springers** — the paired curls the fan rises from.
- **Acroterion** — a palmette used as a rooftop/pediment crown.
- **Half-palmette** — a single side, used in scrolls and as a connective leaf.

## Shapes
Radial symmetry around a vertical axis; narrow leaf-rays fanning outward; paired returning volutes at the base; alternating rhythm in bands. Strong, clean silhouette.

## Textures
Painted pottery, carved stone akroteria, terracotta antefixes, inlaid and embroidered borders. Flat graphic silhouette works well; or fine inked line with leaf veining.

## Palette(s)
Greek black/terracotta; marble white and gilt; navy-and-gold. The fan reads strongly in one color, so palette is flexible.

## Modern Remix
- **Brand:** the radiating palmette makes an elegant sunburst-style mark or crest crown.
- **Deco crossover:** stylize the fan into hard geometric rays for an Art Deco motif (see [[design-system-art-deco-geometric]]).
- **POD / poster:** anthemion running borders for "classical" tees, totes, and quote prints.

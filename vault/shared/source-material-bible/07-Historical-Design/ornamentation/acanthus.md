# Acanthus Ornament

> The serrated, deeply lobed Mediterranean leaf that became Western architecture's favorite foliage — the curling crown of the Corinthian capital and the engine of scrollwork everywhere.

## Defining Features
A stylized rendering of the *Acanthus mollis / spinosus* leaf: broad, fleshy, deeply cut into pointed lobes that curl and turn back on themselves. Acanthus rarely sits flat; it grows, scrolls, and unfurls along a stem (the volute), reading as living, springing energy frozen in stone or carving. Its signature is the "eye" or rosette where a leaf turns and the deep undercut shadow line between lobes.

## Visual Vocabulary
- **Acanthus leaf** — the base unit, lobed and serrated, with a central rib.
- **Volute / scroll** — a leaf or stem coiling into a spiral.
- **Corinthian capital** — two tiers of acanthus leaves with corner volutes (the classic column top).
- **Leaf-and-dart** and **leaf-and-scroll** — running borders.
- **Cartouche cradle** — acanthus framing a central blank shield or panel.

## Shapes
Spirals and S-curves dominate; everything bends. Pointed-lobed leaf silhouettes, returning curls, paired bilateral symmetry along an axis, with rosette/whorl centers. The line is never straight — it accelerates into tight coils and relaxes into long sweeps.

## Textures
Carved stone and plaster (the original); also wood carving, repoussé metal, and inked engraving. Implies deep relief, crisp shadow lines, and a hand-carved, slightly weathered surface. In flat illustration, render with line weight that thickens on the outer curve.

## Palette(s)
Naturally monochrome (the color of stone, plaster, bronze, or ink): warm limestone cream, weathered grey, verdigris bronze, gilded gold on white. Works beautifully as gold-foil on deep navy/oxblood, or single-color line art on cream.

## Legal & Risk
Completely safe as a style — acanthus is 2,500 years old. The only risk is reproducing a *specific* protected source: a copyrighted photo of a particular carving, or a named living artist's stylized acanthus. Generate or draw fresh.

# Strapwork

> Ornament that looks like cut, curled, and interlaced leather or metal straps — the framing device of Renaissance cartouches, title pages, and antique map borders.

## Defining Features
Bands rendered to look like flat straps of leather or metal that have been cut, rolled at the edges, pierced, and interlaced over and under one another. Strapwork forms elaborate **cartouches** — decorative frames enclosing a blank shield, motto, or map title. The signature is the *rolled scroll terminus* (a strap end curling like a parchment) and the woven over-under interlace. Often combined with grotesque masks, fruit swags, and figures (Mannerist excess).

## Visual Vocabulary
- **Strap** — the flat band with rolled edges.
- **Cartouche** — a strapwork frame around a central blank field.
- **Rolled / curled terminus** — strap ends that scroll up.
- **Interlace** — straps weaving over and under.
- **Pierced strap** — straps with cut-out holes for depth.

## Shapes
Flat ribbon-like bands, rolled scroll ends, interlacing weaves, symmetric framing layouts, lobed cartouche outlines. A blend of straight runs and tight scrolled curls.

## Textures
Engraved print, carved oak panelling, plaster ceiling relief, tooled leather. Implies a flat-but-modeled strap with a soft edge highlight. Render with a slight bevel/shadow to sell the "leather" illusion.

## Palette(s)
Engraving sepia/black on cream; carved-wood browns; gilt-on-color for luxe cartouches. Often monochrome — the illusion of relief does the work.

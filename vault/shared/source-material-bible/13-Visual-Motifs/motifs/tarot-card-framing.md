# Tarot-Card Framing

## Description
**Tarot-card framing** uses the unmistakable vertical card layout of the Major Arcana — an ornamental border, a Roman numeral at the top, a central allegorical figure or scene, and a title banner at the bottom — as a container for *any* subject. It instantly lends mystery, fate, and archetypal weight. The motif is the *frame and grammar*; what you place inside (your own figure, a modern subject, a niche character) is the creative variable.

## Visual vocabulary
- **Vertical card proportion:** tall portrait rectangle, often with rounded or ornate corners.
- **Ornamental border:** a decorative frame inside the card edge (links to [[frames-and-borders]]).
- **Roman numeral:** centered at the top (0 The Fool, XIII Death, XVII The Star, etc.).
- **Title banner:** the card's name in a banner along the bottom (links to [[banners-and-ribbons]]).
- **Central allegory:** a single symbolic figure/scene in a frontal, iconic, slightly stiff "card" pose.
- **Symbolic accessories:** suns, moons, stars, pillars, hands, keys, animals arranged with meaning.
- **Flat symbolic color or engraving:** classic flat medieval color, or monochrome engraving for an antique-occult feel.

## Where it shines
- **Posters & wall art (best):** a single "card" as a statement piece; full-deck series as a print collection.
- **Apparel:** a custom tarot card on the chest/back — hugely popular in occult/alt fashion.
- **Book covers & story:** fantasy, occult, literary fiction; chapter-divider cards in a narrative.
- **Merch:** sticker decks, enamel pins, oracle-style card products (invent your own).
- **Brand:** mystical/wellness/astrology identities, "draw your card" interactive content.
- **Struggles:** corporate/clean contexts; tiny scale (the layered detail needs room).

## Pairs with

- **Palettes:** medieval flat (gold + red + blue), occult (black + gold + oxblood), midnight + foil. (link section 11)

- **Negative hints:** suppress `rider waite exact copy, thoth deck, modern published deck, photo, 3d` — invent your own figure rather than reproducing a known deck.

## Legal note
The card *format* is a free convention. The Rider–Waite–Smith 1909 artwork is now public domain in the US and most markets (Pamela Colman Smith d.1951; cleared in life+70 territories from 2022), so referencing it is generally safe — but cloning it 1:1 is crowded and uninspired. Modern decks (Thoth and all contemporary artist decks) are fully copyrighted: never copy their art. Safest and most ownable path: invent your own allegorical figures inside the generic frame.

# The Craftsman

> One-line essence: the master of a craft who makes things well through patient skill.

## Description
The Craftsman (or Artisan) is the archetype of *mastery through patient, repeated practice*. The blacksmith, the weaver, the carpenter, the watchmaker, the chef, the luthier — all run this pattern. Where the [[inventor]] builds what's never existed and the [[creator]] expresses a personal vision, the Craftsman perfects a *known form* to its highest degree: the best knife, the truest joint, the most beautiful bowl. The Craftsman's pride is in quality, durability, and the dignity of work done right.

The pattern carries deep cultural resonance because it stands against disposability and haste. The Craftsman represents tradition, the transmission of skill across generations (overlap with the [[teacher]] via the master-apprentice bond), and the meditative satisfaction of working with one's hands. In an age of mass production and instant everything, the Craftsman archetype reads as authenticity and integrity — which is exactly why "handmade," "small-batch," and "artisan" command a premium.

In branding, the Craftsman is the quality-and-authenticity voice: handmade goods, heritage brands, "made the slow way," premium tools, food and drink with provenance, and any product positioned on craftsmanship.

## Core values
- Quality over quantity.
- Patience and the long apprenticeship.
- Respect for tradition and the materials.
- Integrity — doing the work right when no one is watching.
- The dignity of skilled labor.

## Motivations
- **Deep want:** to master the craft and make things of lasting worth.
- **Deep fear:** shoddy work, the craft dying out, being replaced by something cheap and fast.

## Shadow
The shadow Craftsman is the **joyless drudge** or the **rigid traditionalist** — skill without soul, perfectionism that suffocates, contempt for anything new or any other way of working, the master who refuses to adapt and lets the craft calcify.

## Visual motifs
Hands at the bench; sparks flying from the forge; a tool worn smooth with use; the finished object held up to the light; the quiet, ordered workshop. See [[13-Visual-Motifs]] for "the forge" and "the maker's hands" compositions.

## Colors
Earthy, warm, tactile — leather brown, forge orange, raw linen, aged brass, deep walnut. Natural and grounded. See [[08-Color-Palettes]] — forge-and-leather and artisan-walnut palettes.

## Symbols
Hammer and anvil (the smith), the loom (the weaver), the chisel and potter's wheel (the shaper), the maker's mark (the signature of quality), callused hands. Cross-link [[09-Symbols]].

## Story uses
The wise old maker who forges the hero's weapon, the apprentice who must surpass the master, the last keeper of a dying craft, the craftsman whose masterwork is cursed or sought after. Pairs with a [[hero]] they equip and a [[merchant]] who wants to mass-produce their work.

## Design uses

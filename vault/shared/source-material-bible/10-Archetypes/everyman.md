# The Everyman

> One-line essence: the down-to-earth regular person who just wants to belong.

## Description
The Everyman (sometimes the Regular Guy/Gal, the Neighbor, the Good Citizen) is the archetype of *belonging and relatability* — the ordinary person who isn't special, doesn't want to be special, and just wants to fit in, get along, and live a decent life among their people. Sancho Panza, the loyal friend, the working stiff, the everyday narrator — all run this pattern. The Everyman's appeal is that they are *us*: unpretentious, grounded, decent, the person next to you on the bus. Their virtues are common sense, loyalty, hard work, and solidarity.

The Everyman is one of the most powerful audience-connection patterns precisely because they ask for no admiration. Where the [[hero]] is aspirational ("be more than you are") and the [[ruler]] is exclusive ("be better than others"), the Everyman is inclusive ("you're fine as you are, and you belong here"). When an Everyman *does* rise to a challenge, it lands hard — the ordinary person doing the extraordinary thing is one of the most beloved story shapes.

In branding, the Everyman is the relatable-and-inclusive voice: mass-market and value brands, "for real people," comfort and reliability, family and community, beer and casual food, "no fuss, no frills." It promises belonging, honesty, and no pretension.

## Core values
- Belonging and connection on equal footing.
- Decency, honesty, and fairness.
- Hard work and common sense.
- Loyalty to friends and community.
- Humility — no airs, no pretension.

## Motivations
- **Deep want:** to belong, to be accepted, to live an ordinary good life among their people.
- **Deep fear:** standing out for the wrong reasons, being excluded, being left behind.

## Shadow
The shadow Everyman is the **cynic** (so afraid of standing out they sneer at anyone who tries) or the **doormat** (so desperate to belong they have no self, going along with the crowd even into wrong). The desire to fit in becomes conformity and the loss of one's own voice.

## Visual motifs
The crowd of equals; the front porch and the friendly wave; the shared meal; ordinary clothes and real, un-idealized faces; the bus stop; the mug of coffee. See [[13-Visual-Motifs]] for "the shared table" and "the neighborhood" compositions.

## Colors
Honest, unfussy, warm — denim blue, worn flannel red, oatmeal, weathered wood, warm neutral grey. Approachable and real. See [[08-Color-Palettes]] — denim-and-flannel and honest-neutral palettes.

## Symbols
The handshake (equality and trust), the open door and shared table (welcome), work boots (honest labor), the neighborhood and the friendly wave (community). Cross-link [[09-Symbols]].

## Story uses
The relatable narrator, the loyal best friend, the ordinary person caught up in big events, the reluctant everyman who rises when it counts. Pairs with a [[hero]] (as loyal companion) and a [[caregiver]] (community warmth).

## Design uses
Mass-market and value-brand identities, "for the people / keep it real / everyday" merch, community and local-pride POD, casual food and drink branding, relatable everyday-life social content and slice-of-life children's books.

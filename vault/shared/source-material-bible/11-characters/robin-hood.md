# Robin Hood

## Snapshot

The legendary heroic outlaw of English folklore who "robs from the rich and gives to the poor," leading his band of Merry Men from Sherwood Forest against the corrupt Sheriff of Nottingham. A figure assembled over centuries of ballads rather than written by any single author.

## Copyright status (the text/expression)

Fully public domain by age. Robin Hood is a medieval folk legend; the earliest surviving ballads date to the 14th–15th centuries, and the classic literary tellings (e.g. Howard Pyle's 1883 *The Merry Adventures of Robin Hood*) are themselves long PD. There is no author, no estate, and no underlying copyright that could still subsist. Robin, Maid Marian, Little John, Friar Tuck, Will Scarlet, Allan-a-Dale, the Sheriff of Nottingham, Prince John, and Sherwood Forest are all free to use.

## What is RESTRICTED (the modern design / trademark / publicity)

Only specific *modern adaptations* — never the legend itself:

- **Disney's 1973 *Robin Hood*** reimagined the cast as anthropomorphic animals (Robin as a fox). That specific design is a Disney copyright/trademark — don't copy it.
- **Live-action film designs and actor likenesses** — Errol Flynn (1938), Kevin Costner (1991), Russell Crowe (2010), the BBC series — each carry their own copyrights, and the actors' likenesses carry publicity rights. Don't reproduce a specific film's look or an actor's face.
- **Specific company trademarks.** Some businesses trademark "Robin Hood" for particular products (e.g. a flour brand). The legend is free, but check your specific product class before using it as *branding*.

## Risk level and why

**Safe.** This is the cleanest kind of source: ancient, authorless folk legend with no estate and no looming copyright. The only way to get into trouble is to copy a particular modern film/Disney design or to collide with a niche trademark in your product class — both easily avoided.

## Safe-use guidance

- **Do:** Depict the classic lincoln-green hooded archer in your own design; use the whole Merry Men cast, Sherwood Forest, the archery contest, the rich-to-poor theme. Use Howard Pyle's PD text and illustrations. Usable even as a brand name (with a routine trademark check for your class).
- **Don't:** Copy Disney's fox design or any specific film's costume/look. Don't reproduce an actor's likeness. Don't assume the name is free for *every* product class without a trademark check.

## Creative opportunities

### Design / POD
Longbow-and-arrow minimalist line art, "rob from the rich" slogan tees, Sherwood-Forest/greenwood poster series, archery-target geometric designs, lincoln-green silhouette branding.

### Story / writing
Gender-swapped Robin, urban or sci-fi reframes, the Sheriff's perspective, Maid Marian as protagonist, audio-drama ballads. Wholly remixable with no rights friction.

### Other applications
Children's adventure books, an outlaw-hero brand (Z-named if you want a fully ownable trademark), AI-writing heist/rebellion prompt packs, merch lines across the whole range.

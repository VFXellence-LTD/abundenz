# Alice / Mad Hatter / Cheshire Cat (Wonderland characters)

## Snapshot

The cast of Lewis Carroll's *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland* (1865) and *Through the Looking-Glass* (1871): the curious child Alice, the White Rabbit, the Mad Hatter, the March Hare, the Cheshire Cat, the Caterpillar, the Queen of Hearts, the Dormouse, Tweedledum and Tweedledee, and more.

## Copyright status (the text/expression)

Fully public domain worldwide. Both books are 19th-century works (1865 and 1871). Carroll (Charles Dodgson) died in 1898; illustrator **John Tenniel died in 1914**, so even Tenniel's original engraved illustrations are public domain — a rare case where both the text *and* the canonical original artwork are free to reproduce and remix directly.

## What is RESTRICTED (the modern design / trademark / publicity)

- **Disney's 1951 animated designs.** Disney's *Alice in Wonderland* (1951) created specific character models — the rounded-proportion Alice in her particular blue dress and white pinafore, the purple-and-pink striped Disney Cheshire Cat, the Disney Mad Hatter, the heart-faced Disney Queen — that are Disney copyrights AND trademarks. Do not copy them.
- **Tim Burton's 2010 Disney film designs** (Johnny Depp Hatter, the CGI worlds) are likewise restricted Disney/Burton property; the actors' likenesses raise publicity rights.
- **The blue-dress Alice is NOT inherently Disney** — coloured editions of Tenniel-illustrated Alice showed her in a blue dress and white apron decades before Disney. A *generic* blue-pinafore Alice drawn in your own style is safe; what you must avoid is Disney's specific character model and styling.

## Risk level and why

**Caution.** The text and original art are about as free as anything gets, but the dominant pop-culture Alice is Disney's, so creators slide into the restricted design by reflex. Because the public-domain Tenniel art is available and gorgeous, there is no reason to risk the Disney look.

## Safe-use guidance

- **Do:** Use Carroll's text freely; reproduce or remix Tenniel's original illustrations; create your own Alice designs (a generic blue-dress-and-apron Victorian Alice is fine). Use all characters and scenes.
- **Don't:** Copy Disney's 1951 character models or the Burton 2010 designs. Don't use the specific Disney Cheshire Cat color/styling or Disney's trademarked logos. Don't reproduce actor likenesses.

## Creative opportunities

### Design / POD
Tenniel-engraving collage posters and tees (the original art is PD), "We're all mad here" and "Curiouser and curiouser" quote products (PD text), playing-card and teacup motif patterns, surreal Wonderland poster series in an original art style.

### Story / writing
Sequels, dark reframes, a grown-up Alice, audio-drama of the nonsense poems (Jabberwocky, etc. are PD), interactive AI fiction set in Wonderland. The whole text is remixable.

### Other applications
Children's-book retellings (your own illustrations or PD Tenniel art), tea-brand and stationery aesthetics, AI-image surreal prompt packs, a Z-named original Wonderland-traveller character for a branded line.

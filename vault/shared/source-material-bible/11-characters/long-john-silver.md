# Long John Silver

## Snapshot

The charismatic, treacherous one-legged sea cook of Robert Louis Stevenson's *Treasure Island* (1883) — ship's cook turned mutineer, with a parrot ("Captain Flint") on his shoulder, who befriends and manipulates young Jim Hawkins on the hunt for buried treasure. The character that codified the modern pop-culture pirate.

## Copyright status (the text/expression)

Fully public domain worldwide. *Treasure Island* appeared in book form in 1883 (serialized 1881–82); Stevenson died in 1894 (life+70 long expired). Long John Silver, Jim Hawkins, Captain Flint, Billy Bones, Israel Hands, Ben Gunn, the treasure map, the black spot, "pieces of eight" — all free to use.

## What is RESTRICTED (the modern design / trademark / publicity)

- **Specific modern adaptations** and their designs/likenesses: Disney's 1950 *Treasure Island* (Robert Newton's performance famously fixed the "Arrr, matey" pirate voice in pop culture — but an accent/performance style is not copyrightable; his facial *likeness* is a publicity matter); Disney's *Treasure Planet* (2002) design; the Starz series *Black Sails* (its specific prequel take is copyrighted). Don't copy a particular adaptation's design.
- **Trademark in the food class.** "Long John Silver's" is a US seafood-restaurant chain and a registered **trademark for restaurant/food products**. The character is free for creative use, but if you brand *food or restaurant* products, check that trademark.

## Risk level and why

**Safe** for creative/POD use. PD text, full cast, and the "iconic pirate" imagery (parrot, peg leg, tricorn, treasure map) is generic genre vocabulary rather than anyone's protected design. The only specific caution is the restaurant trademark if you wander into the food/restaurant product class.

## Safe-use guidance

- **Do:** Use the novel and characters freely; design your own pirate; use the parrot, peg leg, treasure map, black spot, "pieces of eight," X-marks-the-spot. Pirate-genre imagery is broadly free.
- **Don't:** Copy a specific film/series pirate design or an actor's likeness; don't brand *food/restaurant* products "Long John Silver" without a trademark check.

## Creative opportunities

### Design / POD
Treasure-map "X marks the spot" posters, parrot-on-shoulder pirate line art, "pieces of eight" and "dead men tell no tales"-style slogan tees (use PD/ generic phrasings), tricorn-and-cutlass designs, sea-chest and Jolly-Roger motifs.

### Story / writing
Faithful retellings, Silver as antihero protagonist, space/modern-pirate reframes, the parrot as narrator, audio-dramas, an original Z-named pirate captain. The treasure-hunt-with-a-charming-traitor engine is endlessly reusable.

### Other applications
Children's pirate-adventure books, pirate/nautical brand identities (Z-named for a clean trademark, especially to avoid the food-chain mark), AI-writing treasure-hunt prompt packs, full-range pirate merch.

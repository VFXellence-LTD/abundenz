# Oz Characters (Dorothy, Scarecrow, Tin Man, Cowardly Lion, etc.)

## Snapshot

The cast of L. Frank Baum's *The Wonderful Wizard of Oz* (1900) and its sequels: Dorothy Gale and her dog Toto, the Scarecrow (wants brains), the Tin Woodman (wants a heart), the Cowardly Lion (wants courage), the Wizard, Glinda the Good Witch, the Wicked Witch of the West, the Munchkins, and the magical land of Oz.

## Copyright status (the text/expression)

Fully public domain in the US. *The Wonderful Wizard of Oz* (1900) and Baum's subsequent Oz novels through 1920 are all pre-1930 and PD. **W. W. Denslow's original 1900 illustrations are also PD**, so the *book's* visual versions of the characters can be reproduced and remixed directly. (Caution: later Oz books written by *other* authors after Baum's 1919 death may have their own, newer copyrights — stick to Baum's own ~14 novels.)

## What is RESTRICTED (the modern design / trademark / publicity)

This is, alongside Frankenstein, the headline teaching case — and one of the most litigated:

- **The 1939 MGM film designs are RESTRICTED** (rights now held by Warner Bros., a vigorous enforcer):
  - **The RUBY SLIPPERS.** In Baum's *book*, Dorothy's magic shoes are **SILVER**. The **ruby** slippers were invented for the 1939 Technicolor film. The *visual design* is protected by **copyright** (held by Warner Bros., expiring ~Jan 1 2035 — 95 years from 1939). Separately, "RUBY SLIPPERS" is registered as a **word mark** (Turner/Warner) in the US/UK/EU/NZ, but note: that word-mark registration protects the *phrase*, NOT the visual image — the image is a copyright matter. Either way: do not depict ruby slippers; use silver shoes for a book-faithful, safe version.
  - **Judy Garland's Dorothy** — the blue-and-white gingham pinafore look, her braids, and her facial likeness (right of publicity).
  - The MGM **Scarecrow, Tin Man, and Cowardly Lion makeup/costume designs**.
  - The **green-skinned Wicked Witch of the West** (Margaret Hamilton's look — Baum's witch was not green) and her black hat/cackle styling.
  - The MGM **Emerald City** design, the specific yellow-brick-road look, "Over the Rainbow," and other film-original elements.
- **Trademarks.** Warner holds Wizard of Oz trademarks across merchandise classes, including the ruby slippers as a brand asset.

## Risk level and why

**Avoid** (for any film-derived depiction). The text is PD, but the entire *popular image* of Oz is the 1939 MGM film, which Warner Bros. polices aggressively. Creators almost reflexively reach for ruby slippers, green witches, and gingham Dorothy — all restricted. This is among the easiest properties to infringe by accident, so the section default is **avoid the film look entirely** and work only from Baum/Denslow.

## Safe-use guidance

- **Do:** Use Baum's text and characters; reproduce/remix Denslow's 1900 illustrations; design your own Oz cast. Crucially, depict **SILVER shoes**, not ruby. Use the Yellow Brick Road, Emerald City, Munchkins, the quest structure.
- **Don't:** Depict ruby slippers, green-skinned witches, or any MGM design. Don't use Judy Garland's likeness or the gingham-braids Dorothy. Don't use "Over the Rainbow." Don't market under Wizard of Oz trademarks.

## Creative opportunities

### Design / POD
Denslow-art collage posters (PD), "There's no place like home" quote products (the phrase is from Baum's book, PD — but render it without the film styling), **silver-shoe** Dorothy line art, Yellow Brick Road map posters, original-style Scarecrow/Tin Woodman/Lion trios.

### Story / writing
Sequels, "the Witch's side of the story," darker Oz reframes, audio-dramas of the PD novels, an original Z-named traveller exploring an Oz-like land. The 14 Baum novels are a huge remixable corpus.

### Other applications
Children's-book retellings (your own or Denslow art), AI-writing quest prompt packs, fantasy-map aesthetics — always with silver shoes and no MGM elements.

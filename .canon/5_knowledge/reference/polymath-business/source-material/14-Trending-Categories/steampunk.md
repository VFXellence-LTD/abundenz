# Steampunk

## Description
The retro-futurist aesthetic that imagines an advanced technology powered by steam, brass, and clockwork — Victorian-era industrial design fused with fantastical invention. Airships, mechanical automata, brass goggles, exposed gears, pressure gauges, and the figure of the gentleman-inventor. Rooted in public-domain source material (Jules Verne, H. G. Wells, Mary Shelley) and the real Victorian industrial age. The *aesthetic itself* is free; the traps are specific franchise designs. Adjacent flavors: dieselpunk (1920s–40s machine age), clockpunk, gaslamp fantasy.

## Why It Resonates (Audience Psychology)
Steampunk sells **romantic ingenuity** — a world where machines are beautiful, hand-crafted, and comprehensible, the opposite of today's sealed black-box electronics. It serves a maker/tinkerer identity (the buyer who values craft and mechanism), a cosplay-and-convention subculture that spends heavily on aesthetic, and a broad audience drawn to the adventure-and-wonder mood (airship voyages, mad inventions). The Victorian-elegance layer adds a sophisticated, decorative appeal that works on home decor and stationery as well as apparel. It is a complete, self-consistent visual world, which makes it unusually generative.

## Design / Store / POD Opportunities
- **POD/t-shirt:** gear-and-cog typographic designs; "inventor/airship-captain" identity tees; brass-goggles iconography; mechanical-anatomy (clockwork heart) art.
- **Poster:** blueprint-style contraption schematics; airship-voyage travel-poster art; exploded-clockwork prints; Victorian-filigree-meets-machine decor.
- **Children's book:** an inventor-kid-builds-machines series; airship-adventure stories; "how gears work" gentle STEM.
- **Podcast/video:** maker/craft content; steampunk-worldbuilding; Verne/Wells retellings (PD source).
- **Brand:** an original steampunk inventor world (Z-named) spanning books, prints, and merch.

## Visual Motifs
Brass and copper with patina, exposed clockwork and interlocking gears, riveted metal plating, blueprint and technical-drawing linework, Victorian filigree, sepia and aged-leather textures. See [[13]].

## Symbols
Gear/cog (mechanism/industry), brass goggles (the inventor), airship (adventure/freedom), pocket watch (time/precision), key (mystery/access), pressure gauge (steam power), top hat (Victorian gentility), clockwork (intricate design). See [[09]].

## Palette Pairings
Brass + copper + deep brown + cream for classic steampunk; aged teal + rust + gold for weathered industrial; black + brass + oxblood for refined neo-Victorian; muted sepia + amber for vintage-warm. See [[08]].

## Remix Combinations
- **Steampunk × Fantasy** → gaslamp fantasy (magic meets machine).
- **Steampunk × Space** → clockwork-cosmos / aetheric airships among the planets.
- **Steampunk × Nature** → botanical automata, mechanical insects (beautiful and distinctive).
- **Steampunk × Cute** → adorable tiny inventors and clockwork creatures.
See [[15]].

## Evergreen vs Trend
**Evergreen-leaning — 4/5.** Steampunk has sustained a devoted subculture for decades and doesn't seem to fade so much as ebb and flow. It is more durable than a fad but more niche than mythology — a stable, loyal mid-size category.

## Saturation / Competition Note
**Moderate.** Generic "gears on everything" is crowded, but the subculture rewards **detail and authenticity**, and several lanes stay open: **steampunk × nature** (mechanical botanicals/insects, under-served and striking), **dieselpunk** (less saturated than steampunk proper), and **coherent original-world art** with a consistent inventor cast. Quality and worldbuilding beat scattered gear-clutter.

## Legal / Execution Notes
The aesthetic and its Victorian source texts are free. Per [[01]]: avoid specific franchise designs (the Wild Wild West film, Bioshock's Big Daddy, named game/anime contraptions) and don't copy a recognizable artist's signature work. The generic gears-goggles-airship vocabulary is yours to use.

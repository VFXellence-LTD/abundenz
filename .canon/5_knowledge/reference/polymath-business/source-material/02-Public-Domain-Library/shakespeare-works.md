# The Works of William Shakespeare

> The most quotable body of work in English — totally free in the original text, with one trap: a *publisher's annotated edition* is its own copyright, even though the play is not.

## Overview
The plays and poems of William Shakespeare (c. 1590–1613) — tragedies (*Hamlet*, *Macbeth*, *Othello*, *King Lear*, *Romeo and Juliet*), comedies (*A Midsummer Night's Dream*, *Twelfth Night*, *Much Ado*), histories, and the Sonnets. The original texts are ancient and fully public domain worldwide. **The caution is editorial copyright**: modern critical editions (Arden, Oxford, Folger, Norton) own their footnotes, glossaries, introductions, and editorial choices — the *play* is free, a *specific annotated edition* is not. Quote from a clean PD text.

## Themes
- **Love and tragedy** — *Romeo and Juliet*; passion and doom.
- **Ambition and power** — *Macbeth*; the corrupting climb.
- **Jealousy** — *Othello*; trust destroyed.
- **Fate, madness, and revenge** — *Hamlet*; the haunted, hesitating mind.
- **Mistaken identity / disguise** — the comedies; gender play.
- **Mortality** — "to be or not to be"; Yorick's skull; memento mori.

## Symbols
- **Yorick's skull** — memento mori; the universal "alas, poor Yorick" image.
- **The dagger / the bloodied hands** — guilt and ambition (*Macbeth*).
- **The crown** — power, usurpation, its burden.
- **The balcony** — forbidden love (*Romeo and Juliet*).
- **The storm / the heath** — inner turmoil externalized (*Lear*).
- **The mask** — disguise, performance, "all the world's a stage."

## Character archetypes
- **Romeo & Juliet** — the Tragic Lovers.
- **Macbeth (and Lady Macbeth)** — the Ambitious Usurpers.
- **Hamlet** — the Melancholy, Indecisive Prince.
- **Othello / Iago** — the Jealous Husband / the Scheming Villain.
- **The Fool** — the Wise Fool who speaks truth.
- **Viola / Rosalind** — the Cross-Dressed Heroine.

## Visual motifs
- The Globe Theatre; Elizabethan ruffs and doublets.
- Skull-and-bone (Hamlet); dagger and crown (Macbeth); the balcony (Romeo and Juliet).
- Stormy heath (Lear); masquerade masks (the comedies).

## Risks
- **Copyright (text):** none — ancient, PD worldwide.
- **Editorial trap (primary risk):** modern annotated editions (Arden/Oxford/Folger/Norton) own their NOTES and editorial apparatus. Quote from a clean PD text; do not copy a publisher's footnotes/glossary/intro.
- **Adaptation trap:** specific film and stage PRODUCTION designs and actor likenesses are RESTRICTED.
- **Font trap:** specific decorative/blackletter font FILES may be licensed — use PD/openly-licensed fonts.

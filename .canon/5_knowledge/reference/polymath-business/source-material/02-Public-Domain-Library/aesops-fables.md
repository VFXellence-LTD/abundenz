# Aesop's Fables

> The most quotable, lowest-risk source in the entire Bible — hundreds of one-line morals everyone already knows, free for the taking.

## Overview
A body of short moral tales attributed to Aesop, a storyteller of ancient Greece (c. 620–564 BCE), passed down orally and collected/retold for millennia. Each fable is a tiny anthropomorphic-animal story ending in an explicit moral. The fables and their morals are ancient folk material with no copyright. This is the safest PD source in the Bible — the only caveat is that *specific modern translations and illustrated editions* are copyrighted, so use a PD translation/illustrator or create fresh.

## Themes
- **Moral lessons** — every fable ends in a portable maxim.
- **Cleverness vs. strength** — the small outwit the large.
- **Pride before a fall** — vanity punished (the Hare, the Crow).
- **Slow and steady** — diligence over flash (the Tortoise).
- **Consequences and foresight** — the Ant vs. the Grasshopper.
- **Deception** — the Wolf in sheep's clothing; the boy who cried wolf.

## Symbols
- **The Tortoise and the Hare** — persistence vs. complacency.
- **The Fox and the Grapes** — "sour grapes," self-deception.
- **The Lion and the Mouse** — small kindnesses repaid.
- **The Ant and the Grasshopper** — work vs. play, foresight.
- **The Wolf in Sheep's Clothing** — hidden danger.
- **The Crow and the Pitcher** — ingenuity.

## Character archetypes
- **The Trickster Fox** — clever, sometimes too clever.
- **The Proud Loser** (the Hare) and the **Humble Victor** (the Tortoise).
- **The Small-but-Mighty** (the Mouse).
- **The Deceiver** (the Wolf).
- **The Foolish Vain** (the Crow who drops the cheese).

## Visual motifs
- Anthropomorphic animals in simple, iconic poses.
- Tortoise-and-hare race, fox leaping at grapes, ant carrying grain, lion with a thorn, crow dropping pebbles in a pitcher.

## Reusable concepts
- The **fable structure** (tiny story → explicit moral) — endlessly remixable as modern parable.
- Animal-as-archetype mascot system (fox = cunning, tortoise = persistence).
- Single-line aphorisms ideal for typography.

## Inspirational quotes
> "Slow and steady wins the race." — *The Tortoise and the Hare* (traditional moral)

> "It is easy to despise what you cannot get." — *The Fox and the Grapes* (Townsend translation, PD)

> "No act of kindness, no matter how small, is ever wasted." — *The Lion and the Mouse* (MODERN paraphrase of the moral; the traditional moral is "Little friends may prove great friends." The phrasing itself is a 20th-c. summation, not an ancient line — it carries no real IP risk as a short proverb, but do not present it as a verbatim PD translation.)

> "A liar will not be believed, even when he speaks the truth." — *The Boy Who Cried Wolf* (traditional)

(Use PD translations — Townsend 1867, Vernon Jones 1912 — or rephrase.)

## Risks
- **Copyright:** none in the fables/morals — ancient.
- **Translation/edition trap:** modern translations and recent illustrated editions are copyrighted — use PD translations/illustrators or create fresh.
- **Trademark/publicity:** none for the work; avoid copying a specific living illustrator's distinctive style.

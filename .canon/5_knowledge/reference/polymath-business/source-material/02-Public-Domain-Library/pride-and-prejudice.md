# Pride and Prejudice

> The original enemies-to-lovers, and one of the most *merch-friendly* PD works in existence — its opening line alone has sold a million tote bags.

## Overview
Jane Austen's 1813 comedy of manners follows Elizabeth Bennet, second of five sisters, as she navigates marriage, class, and her sparring attraction to the proud Mr. Darcy. Sharp social satire wrapped in a love story. The text is fully public domain with an unusually clean rights profile — Austen's prose is endlessly quotable and the Regency aesthetic is broadly available in PD references. The only real caution is avoiding *specific film/TV adaptation* imagery and the actors' likenesses.

## Themes
- **Pride and prejudice** — both leads must shed both; the title is the thesis.
- **First impressions** (Austen's working title) — misjudgment and revision.
- **Marriage, money, and class** — economic reality under the romance.
- **Female agency within constraint** — Elizabeth's wit as power.
- **Reputation** — how it is built, risked, and repaired.

## Symbols
- **The ballroom** — the social marketplace; the dance as courtship choreography.
- **The letter** — Darcy's letter as the hinge of the plot; private truth.
- **The country estate (Pemberley)** — character revealed through place.
- **The walk** — Elizabeth's muddy hems; movement as independence.

## Character archetypes
- **Elizabeth Bennet** — the Witty Heroine / sharp-tongued independent.
- **Mr. Darcy** — the Proud Suitor / aloof-but-changing love interest.
- **Mrs. Bennet** — the Foolish Matchmaker (comic).
- **Mr. Wickham** — the Charming Cad.
- **Mr. Collins** — the Obsequious Comic Clergyman.

## Visual motifs
- Regency ballrooms, candlelit drawing rooms, empire-waist gowns.
- English country estates, rolling hills, garden paths, sealed letters.

## Reusable concepts
- **Enemies-to-lovers** — the foundational romance engine.
- The "comedy of manners" structure — social satire through courtship.
- The five-sisters ensemble — found-family / sibling dynamics.

## Inspirational quotes
> "It is a truth universally acknowledged, that a single man in possession of a good fortune, must be in want of a wife." — *Pride and Prejudice* (1813)

> "I could easily forgive his pride, if he had not mortified mine." — Elizabeth Bennet, *Pride and Prejudice* (1813)

> "You must allow me to tell you how ardently I admire and love you." — Mr. Darcy, *Pride and Prejudice* (1813)

(PD 1813 English text; no translation concern.)

## Risks
- **Copyright (text):** none — PD.
- **Adaptation trap:** do not use 1995 BBC or 2005 film stills, posters, or specific costume designs; those are copyrighted.
- **Right of publicity:** do not use Colin Firth / Keira Knightley / Matthew Macfadyen likenesses.
- **Trademark:** clean for the work itself.

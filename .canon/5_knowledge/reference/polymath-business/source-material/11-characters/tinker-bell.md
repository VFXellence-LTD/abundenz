# Tinker Bell

## Snapshot

The small fairy companion of Peter Pan from J. M. Barrie's 1904 play and 1911 novel — a "tinker" fairy (she mends pots and kettles) whose mood swings between devotion and jealousy, and whose life depends on children believing in fairies.

## Copyright status (the text/expression)

The **character as Barrie wrote her** is public domain in the US (1904 play / 1911 novel, both pre-1930) and out of ordinary UK copyright since 2007 (with the same GOSH royalty caveat as [[peter-pan]]). Barrie's Tinker Bell is barely described visually — often just "a ball of light" — leaving the concept (a tiny, glowing, jealous tinker-fairy) free to use.

## What is RESTRICTED (the modern design / trademark / publicity)

This is the most trademark-loaded entry in the section:

- **The Disney design is RESTRICTED copyright.** Disney's Tinker Bell — blonde hair in a top-knot bun, a strapless green mini-dress with a jagged hem, white pom-pom slippers, translucent wings, and a curvy figure — was designed for Disney's 1953 *Peter Pan* and is a Disney copyright. Do not copy it.
- **"Tinker Bell" is a live, heavily-enforced Disney TRADEMARK.** She is a Disney corporate mascot (the sparkle-trailing fairy in Disney's logo idents) and the lead of the *Disney Fairies* / *Pixie Hollow* franchise. Disney polices this hard.
- **Practical over-reach risk.** Even an original fairy that strongly *reads* as "Tinker Bell" (green dress + blonde bun + pixie dust) can attract a Disney complaint regardless of copyright technicalities.

## Risk level and why

**Caution, bordering on avoid for any commercial fairy that resembles Disney's.** The underlying character is PD, but Tinker Bell is functionally a *brand*, and Disney's combined copyright + trademark + mascot status makes her one of the riskiest "public domain" characters to touch. The conservative call: use Barrie's *concept* with a clearly distinct original design and your own name, or skip her entirely.

## Safe-use guidance

- **Do:** Use Barrie's *idea* of a tiny jealous tinker-fairy in a wholly original design, ideally with an original Z-name, especially for any standalone product.
- **Don't:** Reproduce the Disney design (green mini-dress + blonde bun + pom-pom shoes). Don't use the name "Tinker Bell" as branding or a product line. Don't make a "generic" fairy that obviously evokes the Disney mascot. Don't use the Disney sparkle-trail logo motif.

## Creative opportunities

### Design / POD
A folklore-style tinker-fairy (mending pots, glowing) in an original look; generic "believe in fairies" sentiment products that don't reference Disney's design or name; sprite/glow motif patterns.

### Story / writing
Barrie's fairy concept reimagined as an original sprite companion (renamed), folklore-fairy reframes, audio fairy tales. Keep the design and name your own.

### Other applications
A Z-named original fairy-companion character for a children's brand or merch line — this is the recommended path, since it captures the "magical helper sprite" appeal with zero Disney exposure.

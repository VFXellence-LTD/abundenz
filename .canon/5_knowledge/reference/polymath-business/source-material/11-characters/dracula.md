# Count Dracula

## Snapshot

The Transylvanian vampire nobleman of Bram Stoker's 1897 epistolary novel *Dracula* — the archetype that defined modern vampire fiction. A centuries-old aristocrat who relocates to England to spread his undead curse and is hunted by Van Helsing and a band of allies.

## Copyright status (the text/expression)

Fully public domain everywhere. *Dracula* was published in 1897 (US PD as a pre-1930 work; UK PD since 1982). The novel, the Count, Van Helsing, Mina, Jonathan Harker, Renfield, Lucy, and all plot elements are free to use.

## What is RESTRICTED (the modern design / trademark / publicity)

- **The Bela Lugosi / Universal 1931 design.** The slicked-back black hair with widow's peak, the white tie and tails, the high-collared satin opera cape, the hypnotic stare, the medallion — this is the *Universal film* Dracula, not Stoker's. It is protected by Universal's copyright in the film and, critically, by **right of publicity in Bela Lugosi's likeness** (the landmark case *Lugosi v. Universal Pictures* arose precisely over Dracula merchandising). Do not reproduce Lugosi's face or the exact 1931 costume design as a product.
- **Other film designs.** Christopher Lee's Hammer Horror Dracula, Gary Oldman's Coppola design, the *Hotel Transylvania* design (Sony), and *Castlevania* designs are each separately copyrighted.
- **Stoker's own description is PD and is different:** a tall old man clad in black, with a long white moustache, pointed ears, sharp teeth, bushy eyebrows, and hairy palms — who grows younger as he feeds. This is safe to depict.

## Risk level and why

**Caution.** The novel is bulletproof, but the public's mental image of "Dracula" is almost entirely the restricted Lugosi/Universal design, so creators drift into it by default. The Lugosi publicity angle adds a second restriction layer on top of Universal's film copyright. Stay with the novel's description and you are safe.

## Safe-use guidance

- **Do:** Depict the *novel's* Dracula — elderly aristocrat in black, white moustache, the Carpathian castle, transformation into bat/wolf/mist, blood, crucifix, stake, garlic. Use the full cast and plot.
- **Do:** Invent your own modern vampire look distinct from any film.
- **Don't:** Reproduce the Lugosi widow's-peak-and-cape design or Lugosi's facial likeness. Don't copy Hammer/Coppola/Sony designs. Don't market merch implying the Universal Monsters brand.

## Creative opportunities

### Design / POD
Gothic castle silhouette posters, "blood is the life" quote tees (PD text), bat-and-moon line art, Carpathian travel-poster pastiche. Faithful-to-novel old-count illustrations read as fresh precisely because they avoid the overused Lugosi look.

### Story / writing
Retellings, sequels, the Count's origin, an audio-drama of the epistolary letters/diaries, cosmic-horror or folk-strigoi reframes, a female Dracula. Public-domain text means you can even republish/remix the original prose.

### Other applications
Halloween POD ranges, vampire-aesthetic social packs, AI-writing gothic prompt sets, a Z-named original vampire-lord brand for merch lines that need their own trademark.

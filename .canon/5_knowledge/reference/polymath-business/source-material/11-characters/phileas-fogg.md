# Phileas Fogg

## Snapshot

The supremely punctual, unflappable English gentleman of Jules Verne's *Around the World in Eighty Days* (1872), who wagers half his fortune at the Reform Club that he can circumnavigate the globe in eighty days, setting off with his French valet Passepartout.

## Copyright status (the text/expression)

Fully public domain worldwide. *Around the World in Eighty Days* was published in 1872 (serialized) / 1873 (book); Verne died in 1905 (life+70 long expired). Fogg, Passepartout, Detective Fix, Aouda, the Reform Club wager, and the whole itinerary are free to use, as are the original 1870s illustrations.

## What is RESTRICTED (the modern design / trademark / publicity)

Very little — another clean character:

- **No dominant "official" design.** Fogg is simply a reserved, methodical Victorian gentleman; there's no iconic studio costume that owns the image the way film designs own Dracula.
- **A notable myth:** the famous **hot-air balloon** associated with the story is **not in Verne's novel** — it was introduced by the 1956 film. A balloon is a generic motif (no one owns "hot-air balloon"), so depicting one is fine, but know it isn't canonical.
- **Specific modern adaptations are restricted:** the 1956 *Around the World in 80 Days* film, the 2004 Jackie Chan version, the BBC/David Tennant series — their designs and actor likenesses are individually protected. Don't copy a specific adaptation.

## Risk level and why

**Safe.** PD text, full cast, no locked-in restricted design. The only caution is to avoid copying a particular film/series look or actor likeness — trivially easy to do.

## Safe-use guidance

- **Do:** Use the novel and characters freely; design your own Fogg (top hat, frock coat — generic Victorian); use the eighty-day wager, the global route, the Reform Club, Passepartout, modes of period transport, and (if you like) the non-canonical balloon as a generic motif.
- **Don't:** Copy a specific film/series design or reproduce an actor's likeness.

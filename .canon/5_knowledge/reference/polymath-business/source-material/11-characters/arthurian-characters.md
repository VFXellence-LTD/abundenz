# Arthurian Characters (King Arthur, Merlin, Lancelot, Guinevere)

## Snapshot

The legendary cast of the Matter of Britain: King Arthur, who draws the sword from the stone and wields Excalibur; Merlin the wizard-mentor; the knights of the Round Table (Lancelot, Gawain, Galahad, Percival, Kay, Bedivere); Queen Guinevere; the traitor Mordred; the sorceress Morgan le Fay; and the quest for the Holy Grail at Camelot.

## Copyright status (the text/expression)

Fully public domain by age. The legend was assembled over centuries — Geoffrey of Monmouth (c. 1136), Chrétien de Troyes (12th century), Sir Thomas Malory's *Le Morte d'Arthur* (1485), and Alfred Tennyson's *Idylls of the King* (1859–85) are all long PD. The characters, Excalibur, the Round Table, the Grail, Camelot, the Lady of the Lake — all free to use. No author, no estate.

## What is RESTRICTED (the modern design / trademark / publicity)

Only specific modern works built *on top of* the legend:

- **T. H. White's *The Once and Future King* (1958)** is still in copyright. Its *specific* characterizations (e.g. its particular comedic young-Arthur "Wart" and Merlyn) are NOT free — and Disney's *The Sword in the Stone* (1963) is based on White's book, so the **Disney designs are doubly restricted** (Disney copyright + based on a still-copyrighted novel). Avoid both.
- **Film/series designs and likenesses.** John Boorman's *Excalibur*, the BBC *Merlin* series, *King Arthur: Legend of the Sword*, and similar each carry their own copyrights and actor likenesses.
- **Specific copyrighted scripts/gags.** *Monty Python and the Holy Grail* — Arthur is PD, but the film's specific jokes, dialogue, and "Knights Who Say Ni" type material are copyrighted. Don't reproduce them.
- **Lerner & Loewe's *Camelot* musical** is also under copyright as a specific work.

## Risk level and why

**Safe.** The legend is authorless and ancient — about as free as Robin Hood. The only pitfalls are accidentally drawing on a *modern* retelling (especially T. H. White / Disney's *Sword in the Stone*, or Monty Python gags) rather than the medieval sources. Work from Malory/Tennyson (PD) and you are clear.

## Safe-use guidance

- **Do:** Use Arthur, Merlin, the knights, Excalibur, the Round Table, the Grail, Camelot freely; draw on Malory and Tennyson PD text; create your own armor/wizard designs.
- **Don't:** Copy Disney's *Sword in the Stone* designs or anything traceable to T. H. White's still-copyrighted novel. Don't reproduce a specific film/series look or actor likeness. Don't reuse Monty Python's copyrighted gags or the *Camelot* musical's material.

## Creative opportunities

### Design / POD
Sword-in-the-stone and Excalibur minimalist art, Round Table geometric designs, "the once and future king" (careful — use the *phrase* concept generically, not White's text styling), grail-quest poster series, heraldic knight illustrations, Camelot-castle aesthetics.

### Story / writing
Faithful Malory retellings, female-knight reframes, sci-fi/space Camelot, grail-quest mysteries, audio-dramas, an original Z-named questing knight in an Arthurian-flavored world. Vast remixable corpus.

### Other applications
Children's chivalry books, fantasy-brand identities (Z-named for a clean trademark), AI-writing epic-quest prompt packs, full-range merch.

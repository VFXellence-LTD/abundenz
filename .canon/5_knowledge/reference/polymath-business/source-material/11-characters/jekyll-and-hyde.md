# Dr Jekyll & Mr Hyde

## Snapshot

The respectable London physician Dr Henry Jekyll and his depraved alter ego Edward Hyde, from Robert Louis Stevenson's 1886 novella *Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde*. Jekyll's potion lets him split off and indulge his darker self as the brutish Hyde — the definitive parable of human duality.

## Copyright status (the text/expression)

Fully public domain worldwide. The novella was published in 1886; Stevenson died in 1894 (life+70 long expired). Jekyll, Hyde, the transformation potion, Mr Utterson, and the whole story are free to use.

## What is RESTRICTED (the modern design / trademark / publicity)

Relatively little, which makes this an unusually clean character:

- **No single "official" Hyde design exists to infringe.** Stevenson deliberately described Hyde only vaguely — smaller and younger than Jekyll, with an indefinable deformity that makes everyone who sees him recoil. There is no canonical costume or face, so there's no iconic studio design dominating the public imagination the way Lugosi's Dracula or Karloff's Frankenstein do.
- **Specific modern adaptations are restricted:** individual film designs (e.g. the various Hollywood transformations) and their actor likenesses; the Frank Wildhorn **Broadway musical *Jekyll & Hyde*** (its songs and book are copyrighted); the Hyde depicted in Alan Moore's *The League of Extraordinary Gentlemen* (that specific version is the creators'/publisher's). Don't copy a particular adaptation's design or script.

## Risk level and why

**Safe.** PD text, a full cast, and — crucially — no dominant restricted design to accidentally reproduce. The only way to slip is to lift a *specific* film/stage adaptation's look or material, which is easy to avoid by working from Stevenson's text.

## Safe-use guidance

- **Do:** Use the novella and characters freely; invent your own Hyde (Stevenson invites it — the deformity is "indefinable"); use the duality theme, the potion, the transformation, gaslit-London atmosphere.
- **Don't:** Copy a specific film's Hyde design or an actor's likeness; don't reuse the Broadway musical's songs/book or the *League of Extraordinary Gentlemen* version.

Split-face "two natures" graphic designs, "Man is not truly one, but truly two" quote products (PD text), beaker/potion gothic art, Victorian-gentleman-vs-brute silhouette pairs, dual-tone psychological poster series.

### Story / writing
Faithful retellings, Hyde-as-protagonist, an addiction or AI/alter-ego allegory, modern reframes, audio-dramas. The "respectable person with a hidden monstrous self" engine is endlessly reusable.

### Other applications
Psychological-horror brand identities, duality-themed merch, AI-writing "the self divided" prompt packs, a Z-named original divided-man character. The lack of a locked-in iconic design means your version *is* the design.

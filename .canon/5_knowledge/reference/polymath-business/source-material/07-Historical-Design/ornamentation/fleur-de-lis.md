# Fleur-de-lis

> The stylized three-part lily (or iris) bound at the waist — Europe's emblem of royalty, purity, and faith, and one of heraldry's most enduring charges.

## Defining Features
A symmetrical, stylized flower with three upright "petals" — a tall central spire flanked by two outward-curling side leaves — gathered and bound by a horizontal band near the base, below which a short foot or additional leaflets appear. Strictly bilaterally symmetric. The form reads at any scale and is equally at home as a single emblem or repeated across a field.

## Visual Vocabulary
- **The charge** — a single fleur-de-lis as a standalone emblem.
- **Semé-de-lis (powdered field)** — many small fleurs scattered in regular rows across a ground (the old "France Ancient" arms).
- **Banded waist** — the cross-tie that defines a true fleur-de-lis (vs. a plain lily).
- **Crowned fleur-de-lis** — surmounted by a coronet for extra regalia.
- **Diaper repeat** — used as a quiet textured ground behind a coat of arms.

## Shapes
Symmetric, vertical, lance-like center with paired returning curls; the bottom is anchored by a horizontal bar. Outline-driven; reads as a strong single silhouette. The negative space between petals is part of the recognizable form.

## Textures
Embroidered banner cloth, gilded carving, stamped leather, illuminated gold-leaf, cast metal. In flat use: bold solid silhouette or fine heraldic line. Beautiful as gold-foil or metallic emboss.

## Palette(s)
Heraldic "or on azure" (gold on blue) is the classic French royal lockup; also gold on oxblood/black, white on navy, or single-color silhouette. High contrast favored.

## Modern Remix
- **POD / merch:** fleur-de-lis monogram crests, "old money" prep aesthetics, gothic and metal tee graphics.
- **Pattern:** semé fleur-de-lis as a refined seamless ground for gift wrap, journals, phone cases.
- **Character / brand:** invented Polymath crest (remember the Z-naming convention for any invented brand name).
- **AI prompt:** *"single heraldic fleur-de-lis emblem, gold on royal blue, symmetric, banded waist, vector crest, no team logos."*

## Legal & Risk
The ornamental/heraldic device is ancient and free. Care is needed because specific living organizations (a major American football team, scouting movements, certain cities and Quebec) use particular fleur-de-lis stylizations as registered marks. Use a generic, freshly-drawn fleur-de-lis; never clone a specific brand's exact logo, color, and surrounding lockup.

# Rocaille (Rococo Shell Ornament)

> Frothy, asymmetric scrollwork built from scallop shells, rocks, and water-foam — the lightest, most playful and intentionally *off-balance* of the classical ornament families.

## Defining Features
Where Baroque is heavy and symmetric, Rococo *rocaille* is light, airy, and deliberately **asymmetric**. The core unit is the scallop/cockle shell with its fan of ridges and ragged "rocky" edge, combined with C- and S-scrolls, frothy water/foam shapes, and delicate sprays of naturalistic flowers. Compositions tilt and swirl off-axis, never settling into mirror symmetry, and are typically gilded. The feeling is of melting, growing, frothing movement.

## Visual Vocabulary
- **Coquille (scallop shell)** — the signature fan-ridged shell.
- **Rocaille / rockwork** — encrusted rocky, foamy, watery edges.
- **Asymmetric cartouche** — an off-balance frame around a mirror or panel.
- **Floral spray** — loose naturalistic flowers springing from scrolls.
- **C- and S-scroll** — the curling skeletal lines.

## Shapes
Asymmetric, swirling C/S curves; ragged shell fans; flame-like foam; loose botanical sprays. The whole composition leans and flows — balance is dynamic, not mirrored.

## Textures
Carved-and-gilded wood (the gilt mirror frame), stucco, porcelain (Meissen/Sèvres), painted boiserie. Implies soft gilt highlights, rocky encrusted edges, and delicate painted flowers.

## Palette(s)
Pastels with gold: powder blue, rose pink, mint, cream, ivory — all lifted by gilt. Also white-and-gold (the classic Rococo interior).

# Neoclassical Art

> A sober return to antiquity and reason: clean contour lines, statue-like figures, classical settings, and moral seriousness — a deliberate rejection of Rococo frivolity.

## Overview
Neoclassicism revived the restraint, line, and moral gravity of Greek and Roman art as a reaction against Rococo excess, coinciding with the Enlightenment and the rediscovery of Pompeii. Its clarity, clean contour, and dignified antiquity make it ideal for refined, intellectual, heritage-leaning design. All original works are public domain.

## Defining Features
- Clear, controlled contour line over loose brushwork.
- Statue-like, idealized figures arranged with calm geometry.
- Classical settings: columns, togas, antique furniture.
- Moral and civic themes — duty, sacrifice, stoic virtue.
- Subdued, sober color and even, rational lighting.

## Visual Vocabulary
Crisp outlines, smooth modeling, friezelike horizontal arrangements, restrained gesture, cameo profiles, engraving-style line work, classical architecture as backdrop, muted earthy color.

## Shapes
The clean contour, the column and pediment, the horizontal frieze band, the cameo oval, the laurel circle, the stable rectangular composition.

## Textures
Smooth matte paint, fine engraved line, marble, plaster relief, cool even surface.

## Palette
The "antique sober" family: stone grey, terracotta, muted olive, ivory, slate blue, and restrained gold. Refined and serious. See [[08-Color-Palettes/README]].

## Key Motifs
The cameo profile, classical column and laurel, the toga'd hero, the urn, the oath/gesture of resolve, the fasces and civic emblems, the engraved-line illustration.

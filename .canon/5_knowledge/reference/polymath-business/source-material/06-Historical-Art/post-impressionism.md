# Post-Impressionism

> Beyond capturing light: a cluster of artists who pushed color and form toward emotion and structure — swirling impasto skies, pointillist dots, and bold symbolic color.

## Overview
Post-Impressionism is an umbrella for artists who built on Impressionism but pushed past it toward personal expression, structure, and symbolic color: expressive impasto (Van Gogh), geometric structure (Cézanne), pointillist dots (Seurat), and flattened symbolic color (Gauguin). These distinct sub-styles are public domain and supply some of the most recognizable and reproducible painting techniques available.

## Defining Features
- Color and form used expressively/symbolically, not just to record light.
- Strong personal vision and emotional charge.
- Several distinct techniques under one banner: impasto, pointillism, structured faceting, decorative flatness.
- Visible, deliberate mark-making.
- A bridge toward 20th-century abstraction.

## Visual Vocabulary
Thick swirling brushstrokes, dense dots of pure color (divisionism), simplified geometric volumes, bold flat color fields with dark outlines, heightened non-naturalistic color, energized rhythmic surfaces.

## Shapes
Swirling spirals and rhythmic strokes, the dot/point grid, faceted geometric planes (cylinder, sphere, cone), flattened decorative shapes, bold simplified forms.

## Textures
Heavy impasto ridges, granular pointillist stipple, structured patchy planes, matte flat color, energetic directional strokes.

## Palette
Vivid and varied: cobalt and gold swirls, complementary dot pairings (orange/blue, red/green), earthy structured ochres, and tropical saturated pinks and greens. Bold and emotionally charged. See [[08-Color-Palettes/README]].

## Key Motifs
Swirling starry/night skies, sunflowers and expressive florals, the recurring mountain/structured landscape, pointillist crowds and parks, tropical/exotic figures, the structured still life.

## Rights Note
The STYLE and the founding works are public domain — Van Gogh, Cézanne, Gauguin, and Seurat all died well over 70 years ago. The standard nuance applies to **photographic reproductions** (US: a faithful photo of a flat PD painting is PD; some institutions assert terms; other countries vary — prefer confirmed open-access scans). Critically: emulate the *style*, but **do not market a generation as a specific famous painting** (e.g. selling an image as "Van Gogh's Starry Night") — that risks misrepresentation even where the underlying work is PD.

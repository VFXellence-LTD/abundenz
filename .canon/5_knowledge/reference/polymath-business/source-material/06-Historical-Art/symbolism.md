# Symbolism

> Art of dream, myth, and the inner world: veiled allegories, femmes fatales, mystic moons, and jewel-toned decoration that suggests meaning rather than stating it.

## Overview
Symbolism rejected Realism and Impressionism's focus on the visible world to depict dreams, myths, emotions, and the spiritual through suggestive imagery and allegory. It overlaps with Art Nouveau's decorative richness and anticipates Surrealism's dream logic. Its mystical, sensual, jewel-toned aesthetic is potent — but it straddles the public-domain boundary, so treat specific works with caution.

## Defining Features
- Meaning suggested through symbol and allegory rather than depicted directly.
- Dream, myth, the occult, mortality, and sensuality as subjects.
- The femme fatale and other archetypal, charged figures.
- Decorative, often flattened, jewel-rich surfaces.
- A deliberately mysterious, ambiguous, melancholic mood.

## Visual Vocabulary
Dreamlike soft-focus atmosphere, ornate gold and pattern, veiled and elongated figures, mythic and occult symbols, jewel-toned color, decorative flattening, sensual and mysterious staging.

## Shapes
Sinuous figures, decorative pattern fields, the halo/disc, ornamental borders, the spiral, elongated organic forms, mosaic-like gold geometry.

## Textures
Gilded ornament, soft atmospheric haze, jewel-like glazes, decorative flat pattern, mosaic shimmer, velvety darks.

## Palette
The "jewel and gold" family: gold, deep emerald, sapphire, garnet, amethyst, ivory, and shadowy black. Rich, mystical, decadent. See [[08-Color-Palettes/README]].

## Key Motifs
The veiled/dreaming figure, femme fatale, sphinx, peacock, lily, the moon, serpent, severed head (mythic), mystic eye, gold ornamental pattern.

## Rights Note
The STYLE is free to emulate. **Caution on specific works:** Symbolism straddles the PD boundary — many founding works are public domain, but several signature figures cleared the life+70 term only recently (Gustav Klimt died 1918; Odilon Redon died 1916), and reproductions or derivatives can still carry encumbrances. Emulate the dreamlike, mythic, gold-and-pattern *style*; do **not** reproduce a specific named work (e.g. a particular Klimt "gold" painting) without confirming its status against [[01-Legal-Guidelines/us-public-domain-cutoff]].

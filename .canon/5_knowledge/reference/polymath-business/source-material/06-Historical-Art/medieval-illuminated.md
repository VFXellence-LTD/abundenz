# Medieval & Illuminated Manuscript Art

> Hand-painted books glowing with gold leaf: decorated initials, vine borders, and the famous marginalia where monsters, knights, and snail-fighting rabbits crowd the page edges.

## Overview
Medieval illumination is the art of the decorated book — gold-leafed initials, intricate borders, and the gloriously weird marginalia (drolleries) in the page margins. It pairs disciplined order (the text block, the geometric border) with anarchic whimsy (margin grotesques). The manuscripts are public domain and supply an enormous library of motifs, monsters, and decorative systems.

## Defining Features
- Historiated and decorated initials — a single letter expanded into a painting.
- Lavish borders of vines, acanthus, and gold.
- Marginalia: hybrid creatures, jokes, and dramas in the margins, unrelated to the text.
- Flat, jewel-toned color over gold leaf; no naturalistic depth.
- Knotwork and interlace (especially Insular/Celtic, e.g. Book of Kells).

## Visual Vocabulary
Dense, flat patterning; gold-leaf highlights; richly outlined figures; vine scrolls terminating in leaves and beasts; blackletter and uncial lettering; bestiary animals (real and imaginary) rendered with charming earnestness.

## Shapes
The decorated initial, the rectangular text block, vine and acanthus curls, knotwork loops, the rounded arch, the shield, the grotesque hybrid silhouette.

## Textures
Burnished gold leaf, vellum, flat tempera color, fine pen line, the matte grain of parchment.

## Palette
The "scriptorium" family: gold, vermilion, ultramarine blue, leaf green, deep red, and cream vellum. Jewel-toned and warm. See [[08-Color-Palettes/README]] and [[07-Historical-Design/illuminated-borders/README]].

## Key Motifs
Historiated initial, vine/acanthus border, marginal grotesques and drolleries, knotwork interlace, bestiary beasts (dragon, unicorn, basilisk), fleur-de-lis, the praying or jousting knight, the snail-fighting rabbit.

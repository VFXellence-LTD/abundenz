# Patriotic Niche — Operating Guide

The patriotic niche — Constitutional text, Declaration of Independence, Bill of Rights, and civic-themed designs — is the **second-highest-revenue, low-platform-risk** niche in section 20. Like the faith niche, it has strong buyer loyalty and a clear public-domain source material base that eliminates copyright risk.

The buyer base for patriotic content is broad: it spans political affiliations when content stays civic and historical rather than partisan.

## The three operating rules

### 1. No government seals or official insignia

This is the single most important constraint in the patriotic niche. See [[government-works/government-seals-warning|government-seals-warning]] for the full doctrine.

**Concisely:** The text of the Constitution is free. The Great Seal of the United States, the Presidential Seal, federal agency seals, and military branch insignia are restricted by federal criminal statute (18 U.S.C. §§ 506, 701, 713 and related). Do not reproduce them on any commercial product.

**The practical rule:** You can use the American eagle as a national symbol (it's an ancient symbol). You cannot reproduce the specific design of the Great Seal. You can print Constitutional text. You cannot surround it with the Presidential Seal as if it were an official government document.

### 2. No partisan slogans or party marks

Patriotic content's commercial breadth comes from its bipartisan appeal. Explicitly partisan slogans or party marks narrow the audience and raise platform-policy risk.

- Constitutional text appeals across the political spectrum.
- "We the People" has universal resonance.
- "MAGA," "RESIST," and equivalent partisan slogans are polarizing, may be trademarked, and are platform-risk items.

Keep patriotic-niche products civic and historical, not partisan.

### 3. No specific living politicians' names or likenesses

For right-of-publicity and platform-policy reasons, do not name or depict specific living politicians in patriotic-niche products, even approvingly.

**Exception:** Factual historical quotations from historical figures deceased long before 1955. Lincoln, Jefferson, Washington, Adams, Madison — all safe in factual historical use with clear attribution.

## High-performing product categories

### 1. "We the People" typography
The Preamble to the Constitution — especially the opening three words in a distinctive typeface — is the single most recognizable patriotic text element. Consistently strong in poster, t-shirt, and canvas print formats.

Variants: full preamble, "We the People" large with preamble small below, "We the People" in an American-flag color scheme.

### 2. Amendment-series prints
Individual amendments formatted as art prints. The First and Second Amendments have particularly intense dedicated buyer communities. A "Bill of Rights" full-set print is a reliable performer.

### 3. Founding-document parchment aesthetic
High-resolution text overlay on a parchment texture (AI-generated or PD historical parchment scans from National Archives). Works for the full Declaration, the Constitution, or selected passages.

### 4. Historical-figure quote typography
Verified PD quotations from the Founders (Jefferson, Madison, Franklin, Washington) in typographic format. Pair with [[12-Quotes/README|12-Quotes]] section for verified attribution.

Benjamin Franklin and Thomas Jefferson both produced extensive PD writing (Franklin d. 1790, Jefferson d. 1826). Cross-reference the Quotes section for verified attributions — many famous "Founder quotes" circulating online are misattributed.

### 5. Eagle and flag imagery
The American bald eagle as a national symbol (not the Great Seal design) and the American flag design are both free to use. Historical engravings and illustrations of the eagle (19th century and earlier) are PD. Original illustrations or AI-generated eagle imagery are also free.

The US flag design itself is not trademarked — the pattern of stars and stripes is public domain. Official flags of specific branches of the military may have restrictions; consult per branch.

## Seasonal peaks

- **Independence Day (July 4):** Largest sales spike for patriotic content. Start promoting 3-4 weeks early.
- **Constitution Day (September 17):** Smaller but real niche sales spike.
- **Presidents' Day (third Monday in February):** Founder-quote and historical-figure products.
- **Memorial Day / Veterans Day:** Military-themed patriotic content — extra caution on official insignia; keep it flag and founding-document focused.
- **Election years:** Patriotic civic content (non-partisan) sees broader interest but competition also increases.

# Islam — Quran Translation Rights & Cultural Guidance

The Quran's Arabic text is ancient and has never been subject to copyright. English translations, however, are each independent copyrighted works. This entry maps the rights situation and adds critical cultural-sensitivity guidance that applies regardless of copyright status.

---

## The Arabic text

The Arabic Quran is ancient — compiled in written form in the 7th century CE. It is entirely public domain as a text. Arabic calligraphy derived from Quranic verses is also a historic art form with no copyright issues in its traditional forms.

However: **the Arabic text is not in English.** For English-language products, you need a translation — and translation rights are where the complexity sits.

---

## English translation rights

### Pickthall — *The Meaning of the Glorious Quran* (1930)
- **Translator:** Mohammed Marmaduke Pickthall (d. 1936)
- **Publication year:** 1930 (first published in the UK; US publication needs verification)
- **PD claim:** Under US copyright rules, works published in **1930 are public domain** as of January 1, 2026. If the first US publication was 1930, this translation is PD.
- **Risk level:** Caution — treat as **likely PD** but confirm the 1930 US publication date before full production commitment. See [[_VERIFICATION|_VERIFICATION]] — this check is flagged as HIGH PRIORITY.
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH. The 1930 date is widely cited; the US edition year needs manual confirmation.
- **Character:** Careful, somewhat formal Victorian English. Preserves the sense of the original without being awkward. Widely used in academic contexts.
- **Available at:** Project Gutenberg (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2800) — verify this is the 1930 edition, not a later revision.

### Yusuf Ali — *The Holy Quran: Text, Translation and Commentary* (1934)
- **Translator:** Abdullah Yusuf Ali (d. 1953)
- **Publication year:** 1934
- **PD claim:** **NOT YET PUBLIC DOMAIN IN THE US.** Under the 95-year rule, a 1934 US publication enters PD on **January 1, 2030**. Do not use before that date.
- **Risk level:** **Avoid until 2030.**
- **Note:** This is a common mistake. Yusuf Ali's translation is widely available online, but "available on the internet" does not mean public domain. It remains under copyright until 2030 in the US.
- **Update action:** Flag this entry for review in January 2030. When the term expires, update `pd_status` to `public-domain`, `risk_level` to `safe`, and `verified` to `true`.

## The Prophet Muhammad — critical cultural guidance

**Do not depict the Prophet Muhammad in any image, illustration, or AI-generated artwork for any commercial product.**

This is not primarily a legal question — it is a cultural one with significant practical business consequences:

- Depictions of the Prophet (whether reverent, neutral, or satirical) are considered by the vast majority of Muslims to be deeply offensive and forbidden. This is not a fringe view — it is a mainstream religious position across Sunni and Shia traditions.
- Publishing such depictions on commercial products will generate severe negative reactions, platform complaints, organized boycotts, and in some cases real-world threats and violence (as major media organizations have discovered at considerable cost).
- The commercial upside of such depictions is zero. There is no buyer segment for whom "the Prophet on a t-shirt" is a desirable product; there is a very large segment for whom it is an unforgivable offense.

**Hard avoid. No exceptions.**

This restriction applies to AI-generated images as well as traditional illustration. Do not include "Prophet Muhammad" or "Muhammad" as a subject in any image generation prompt.

## What IS commercially viable for an Islamic-themed product line

The following are well-established, culturally respected commercial content categories:

- **Arabic calligraphy** of Quranic verses (using the PD Arabic text, or a PD English translation). Arabic calligraphy is a centuries-old sacred art form; beautiful calligraphic renderings of bismillah, short surahs, or the 99 names of God (Asma ul-Husna) are popular and respected.
- **Geometric Islamic pattern art** — the mathematical, non-figurative pattern tradition in Islamic art. Ancient, beautiful, no depiction concerns, and commercially strong.
- **Typography posters** using Pickthall translation text (once the 1930 US edition is confirmed).
- **The 99 Names of Allah** (Asma ul-Husna) rendered typographically — a popular devotional product format.

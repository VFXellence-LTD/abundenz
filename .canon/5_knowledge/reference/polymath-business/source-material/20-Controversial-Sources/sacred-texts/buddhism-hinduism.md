# Buddhism & Hinduism — Sacred Text Rights & Cultural Guidance

Both Buddhist and Hindu sacred texts present a dual challenge: translation rights (the same ancient-PD-but-translation-copyrighted pattern from every faith in this section) and **living-faith cultural sensitivity** that is particularly pronounced in these traditions. Both are addressed here.

---

## Buddhist texts

### The ancient originals

The Pali Canon (Tipitaka), Sanskrit Buddhist texts, and the Chinese Buddhist canon are ancient, entirely public domain. Specific texts of high commercial interest include:

- **Dhammapada** — collected sayings of the Buddha; aphorisms on ethics, mind, and the path. Rich source for typographic/quote products.
- **Majjhima Nikaya** — "Middle-Length Discourses," core Theravada teaching texts.
- **Lotus Sutra (Saddharmapundarika)** — foundational Mahayana text.
- **Diamond Sutra (Vajracchedika)** — short, highly quoted Prajnaparamita text.

### Safe translations (public domain in US)

**T.W. Rhys Davids translations** (Pali Text Society, 1881–1921):
- Thomas William Rhys Davids (d. 1922); his translations published 1881–1921 are public domain.
- The Pali Text Society (PTS) produced many early scholarly translations; those published before 1931 are PD by date.
- Available via sacred-texts.com and CCEL.

**F.L. Woodward / C.A.F. Rhys Davids** (early 20th century PTS editions):
- Various Pali text translations published 1900s–1920s; verify per volume.

**Müller-era translations:**
- Max Müller's *Sacred Books of the East* (1879–1910) includes Buddhist texts from the Pali, Sanskrit, and Chinese traditions. All PD.

**Dhammapada — specific recommended PD translation:**
- F. Max Müller, *Dhammapada*, Volume X of Sacred Books of the East (1881). PD. Available at Project Gutenberg.

### DO NOT USE — active copyright

| Author / edition | Status |
|---|---|
| Bhikkhu Bodhi translations (Wisdom Publications) | Active copyright |
| Thich Nhat Hanh translations and commentaries | Active copyright; also active estate concerns |
| The Dalai Lama's writings and translations | Active copyright |
| Shambhala Publications editions (most post-1970 translations) | Active copyright |
| Pema Chödrön | Active copyright |
| Gil Fronsdal's Dhammapada translation | Modern; check license — may be CC but verify |

## Hindu texts

### The ancient originals

The Vedas, Upanishads, Bhagavad Gita, Ramayana, and Mahabharata are among the oldest surviving literature in human history. All are ancient and public domain in their Sanskrit originals.

### Safe translations (public domain in US)

**Max Müller's *Sacred Books of the East*** (Oxford University Press, 1879–1910):
- Includes Vedas (Rigveda, Sama-Veda, etc.), Upanishads, and other Hindu texts.
- Müller d. 1900; all volumes published in the series are PD.
- Available at sacred-texts.com and Internet Archive.

**Edwin Arnold, *The Song Celestial*** (Bhagavad Gita, 1885):
- Edwin Arnold (d. 1904); published 1885. Poetic translation. PD.
- Available at Project Gutenberg (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2388).
- Character: Beautiful Victorian verse rendering; has its own aesthetic appeal for poetic/inspirational products.

**Monier Williams, various Vedic translations** (19th century):
- Published 1875 and prior. PD. Available via archive.org.

**Charles Johnston, Upanishads translations** (1895–1931):
- Check per volume; those published 1930 or earlier are PD.

### DO NOT USE — active copyright

| Author / edition | Status |
|---|---|
| Swami Prabhupada, *Bhagavad-gita As It Is* (ISKCON) | Active copyright; 1968 and later. Also involves active trademark and religious community claims. |
| Barbara Stoler Miller translation of the Gita | Active copyright (Columbia University Press, 1986) |
| Georg Feuerstein translations | Active copyright |
| Any Chinmaya Mission or Ramakrishna Mission modern editions | Active copyright |
| Graham Schweig, Winthrop Sargeant modern Gita editions | Active copyright |

## The manji / Buddhist swastika

The swastika is a sacred symbol in Buddhism (the *manji* in Japanese Buddhist tradition) and Hinduism. It appears on temples, in religious art, and in traditional contexts throughout Asia.

**For Western commercial products, do not use this symbol regardless of the context you intend.**

The Nazi appropriation of the swastika means it is read as a hate symbol by most Western consumers. The sacred Buddhist/Hindu meaning does not overcome this in a commercial Western context. See [[09-Symbols/_restricted/swastika-warning|swastika-warning]] for the full doctrine.

## Cultural appropriation — elevated risk in both traditions

Buddhism and Hinduism are **living religions of billions of people worldwide**. Sacred imagery, symbols, and texts carry active devotional meaning to practitioners. Using them as casual graphic decoration — without substantive knowledge or context — is broadly considered disrespectful and risks:

- Significant negative audience reaction (particularly in diaspora communities with a strong online presence).
- Platform complaints and content takedowns.
- Reputational damage to the brand.

This is a separate concern from copyright. The Dhammapada aphorisms used in an inspiring typography poster with genuine design quality and attribution to the Buddhist tradition are a very different product from slapping an Om symbol on a cheap novelty item as an "exotic" decorative pattern. The former can be respectful and commercially successful; the latter invites backlash.

See [[cultural-sensitivity|cultural-sensitivity]] for the fuller framework.

## High-potential product concepts (within safe lane)

- **Dhammapada aphorisms (Müller translation):** "All that we are is the result of what we have thought" and similar widely-known sayings work as typographic posters and apparel. Verify the specific verse against the Müller translation for exact wording.
- **Om (ॐ) symbol typography:** The Om symbol is universally recognized, aesthetically strong, and has broad secular/spiritual crossover appeal. Not trademarked. Appropriate in quality design context.
- **Lotus imagery:** The lotus (representing purity and enlightenment) is a powerful, flexible visual motif with no rights issues. Connects to both Buddhist and Hindu traditions.
- **Dharma wheel (Dharmachakra):** The Buddhist wheel of dharma is an ancient symbol (not trademarked); appropriate in a respectful design context.
- **Edwin Arnold's Gita verse quotations:** The *Song Celestial*'s verse translations of Gita passages are PD, poetic, and suitable for inspirational products.

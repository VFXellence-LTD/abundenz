# Jack and the Beanstalk

> A poor boy trades the family cow for a handful of magic beans, climbs the giant beanstalk that grows overnight, and robs a sky-dwelling giant of his treasures.

## Origin & Tradition
An English folk tale type (ATU 328, "the boy steals the ogre's treasure"). Benjamin Tabart printed a moralized version in 1807; Joseph Jacobs published the now-standard *English Fairy Tales* version in 1890. Both are public domain. The giant's chant "Fee-fi-fo-fum" predates these, appearing in variant form in Shakespeare's *King Lear* (early 17th c.) and other old sources.

## The Story / The Figure
Jack, a poor boy, sells the family's last cow for "magic" beans, enraging his mother, who throws them out. Overnight they grow into a colossal beanstalk reaching into the clouds. Jack climbs it to a giant's castle in the sky and, over repeated trips, steals bags of gold, a goose that lays golden eggs, and a magic singing harp. The giant pursues; Jack chops down the beanstalk, and the giant falls to his death.

## Visual Identity
Free, traditional motifs: a beanstalk twisting up into the clouds; a giant's castle floating above; a goose laying golden eggs; a self-playing golden harp; the looming giant chanting "Fee-fi-fo-fum." All public domain and freshly renderable.

## Symbolism & Themes
Daring and luck lifting the poor out of poverty; the unknown world above the clouds; the morally ambiguous "heroic theft" (Jack is, after all, a burglar); cleverness besting brute size.

# Hansel and Gretel

> Two abandoned children, a trail of crumbs eaten by birds, a house made of candy, and a witch who fattens children for her oven — outwitted by the very children she meant to eat.

## Origin & Tradition
A German folk tale fixed by the Brothers Grimm in 1812 (*Hänsel und Gretel*), likely sharpened by memories of famine. The Grimm text is public domain. (Engelbert Humperdinck's 1893 opera and various films are separate, later works — the opera libretto is also old enough to be PD; specific modern stagings/recordings are not.)

## The Story / The Figure
A poor woodcutter, pressed by his wife (or stepmother), abandons his two children in the forest. Hansel first marks the way with pebbles, then with breadcrumbs that birds eat. Lost, the children find a house built of bread, cake, and sugar. Its owner, a cannibal witch, cages Hansel to fatten him and sets Gretel to work. Gretel tricks the near-blind witch into the oven, frees her brother, and they return home with the witch's treasure.

## Visual Identity
Free, traditional motifs: a cottage of gingerbread, candy, and icing; a dark forest; a trail of breadcrumbs or pebbles; a child poking a bone through a cage bar to fake thinness; a great oven. All public domain and freshly renderable.

## Symbolism & Themes
Hunger and scarcity; abandonment and self-reliance; temptation (the irresistible candy house) masking danger; children's wit triumphing over adult malice. A core "danger dressed as delight" lesson.

# Rumpelstiltskin

> A miller's daughter must spin straw into gold or die; a strange imp does it for a price — her firstborn child — unless she can guess his hidden name.

## Origin & Tradition
A European "name of the helper" tale type (ATU 500), fixed by the Brothers Grimm in 1812 (*Rumpelstilzchen*). The Grimm text is public domain. The story turns on the ancient folk belief that knowing a being's true name gives power over it.

## The Story / The Figure
A miller boasts that his daughter can spin straw into gold. A greedy king locks her in a room of straw and demands gold on pain of death. A little imp appears and spins it for her, taking her ring, then her necklace, and finally a promise of her first child. When the child is born and the imp comes to collect, he offers a reprieve: if she can guess his name in three days, she keeps the child. She overhears him gloating his name by his fire and wins.

## Visual Identity
Free, traditional motifs: gleaming golden thread spilling from a spinning wheel; a locked room piled with straw; a small, gnarled imp dancing and cackling by a fire in the woods; the dramatic name-guessing. All public domain.

## Symbolism & Themes
The desperate bargain and its hidden cost; the magic power of a true name; greed (the king's) versus cunning (the imp's, then the queen's); the trickster who is himself out-tricked.

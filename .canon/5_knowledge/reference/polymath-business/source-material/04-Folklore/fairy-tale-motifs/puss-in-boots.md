# Puss in Boots

> A penniless youth inherits only a cat — but the cat, in fine boots and a feathered hat, schemes his master into a marquis and a royal marriage.

## Origin & Tradition
A European "helpful animal" tale type (ATU 545B). Giovanni Francesco Straparola printed an early form in the 1550s; Charles Perrault's *Le Maître chat ou le Chat botté* (1697) is the famous fixation. Both source texts are public domain. DreamWorks' *Puss in Boots* character (from the *Shrek* franchise) is a separate copyrighted modern creation.

## The Story / The Figure
A miller dies, leaving his youngest son nothing but a cat. The cat asks only for a pair of boots and a sack, then sets to work. Through a series of clever cons — presenting his master to the king as the wealthy "Marquis of Carabas," tricking an ogre into turning into a mouse so he can be eaten and his castle seized — the cat elevates the poor youth to nobility and marries him to the princess.

## Visual Identity
Free, traditional motifs: a confident cat standing upright in tall leather boots; a plumed musketeer-style hat; the cat carrying a sack; a swallowed ogre's castle; the cat presenting itself at court. **Restricted:** the DreamWorks character's specific design.

## Symbolism & Themes
Cleverness and audacity trumping birth and wealth; the loyal trickster who serves a master's rise; performance and confident bluff as social currency; the underdog elevated by wit.

## Legal & Cultural Status
Tale type and Straparola/Perrault texts: **public domain.** Restricted: DreamWorks' specific Puss character. Defers to [[../../01-Legal-Guidelines/pd-source-vs-modern-adaptation|PD source vs modern adaptation]].

# The Lady of the Lake

> The veiled water-spirit who gives Arthur the sword Excalibur and, at the end, reclaims it — the otherworldly hand at both ends of the legend.

## Origin & Tradition
The Lady of the Lake is a composite figure (Nimue, Viviane, Niniane, and others) from French Arthurian romance (13th c.) and Malory's *Le Morte d'Arthur* (1485). She is an otherworldly enchantress of the waters who bestows Excalibur, fosters Lancelot, and in some strands entraps Merlin. The legendary figure and source texts are public domain.

## The Story / The Figure
Dwelling in or beneath a lake, she is the source of Arthur's true sword: an arm "clothed in white samite" rises from the water holding Excalibur, which Merlin instructs Arthur to take. She raises the young Lancelot as her ward ("Lancelot of the Lake"). At Arthur's death, when the sword is thrown back into the water, her hand rises to catch it and draw it under, closing the cycle.

## Visual Identity
Free, traditional motifs: an arm in white silk rising from still water holding a sword aloft; a serene, mist-shrouded lake; a veiled female figure standing or gliding on the water; ripples and reflections. All public domain and iconic.

## Symbolism & Themes
The otherworld as a giver and reclaimer of power; water as a threshold between worlds; the mystical feminine that legitimizes and then collects the king's power; the gift that must one day be returned.

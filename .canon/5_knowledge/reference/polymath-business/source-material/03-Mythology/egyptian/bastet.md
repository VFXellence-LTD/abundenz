# Bastet (Bast)

> The graceful cat-goddess of home, joy, and protection: gentle guardian of the household and fierce defender of those she loves — and the patron saint of every cat-lover in history.

## Who / What
Bastet (or Bast) is the ancient Egyptian goddess of the home, domesticity, fertility, joy, music, and the protection of the household — and, above all, the goddess of cats. Cats were sacred in Egypt (killing one was a capital crime, and beloved cats were mummified), and Bastet was their divine embodiment. Originally a fierce lioness war-goddess, she softened over time into a gentler, protective cat-goddess (her fierce aspect lived on in Sekhmet). She is depicted as an elegant woman with the head of a cat, or as a sleek seated cat adorned with gold, often holding a sistrum (a sacred rattle) and surrounded by kittens. She combines warmth, grace, joy, and the fierce protectiveness of a mother cat. She is one of the most commercially beloved figures in all of mythology, thanks to the vast cat-loving audience.

## Archetype
The Protector of Home / Cat-Goddess / Joyful Guardian / Gentle-but-Fierce Mother. The warm, graceful guardian of the household — and the divine cat.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
An elegant, poised woman with the head of a cat; or a sleek, dignified seated cat wearing gold collars and earrings (in the style of the famous ancient bronze cat statues); holding a sistrum (rattle); surrounded by kittens; graceful, alert, and serene. Gold and profile-art style.

## Symbols
- **The cat** — her sacred animal and embodiment.
- **The cat-headed woman** — her divine form.
- **The sistrum** — the sacred rattle of music and joy.
- **Kittens** — fertility and motherhood.
- **The protective amulet** — cats were worn for protection.

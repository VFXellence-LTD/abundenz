# Eye of Horus (Wedjat)

> The stylized falcon-eye that was torn out and made whole again: the ancient Egyptian symbol of protection, healing, and restoration to wholeness.

## Who / What
The Eye of Horus (the *wedjat* or *udjat*, "the sound/whole one") is a stylized human eye marked with the distinctive cheek-line and spiral of a falcon's eye. Its meaning comes from the myth in which Horus lost his eye in his battle with Set, after which it was healed and restored by the god Thoth (or Hathor) — making it the supreme symbol of healing, restoration, protection, and being made whole again. Egyptians wore it as a protective amulet against evil and harm, and it was painted on coffins, ships, and walls. Its six parts were even used as fractions in measuring. It is one of the most recognizable and widely-reproduced symbols in the world. **Note: it is often confused with the separate "Eye of Providence" (the eye-in-a-triangle on the US dollar), which is a Masonic/Christian symbol, not Egyptian.**

## Archetype
The Protective Symbol / All-Seeing Eye / Emblem of Healing. The mark of protection, restoration, and watchful wholeness.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A stylized human eye with a bold eyebrow above, the distinctive falcon cheek-marking (a teardrop shape descending from the inner corner) and a curling spiral below; rendered in strong lines, usually in blue and gold; as an amulet, a wall motif, or a tattoo. (Distinct from the triangle-eye of Providence.)

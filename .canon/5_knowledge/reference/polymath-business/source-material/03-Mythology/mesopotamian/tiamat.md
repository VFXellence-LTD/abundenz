# Tiamat

> The primordial chaos: the vast salt sea given monstrous form, mother of the first gods and of an army of monsters — slain by Marduk and split in two to become the sky and the earth.

## Who / What
Tiamat is the primordial Mesopotamian goddess of the salt sea and the embodiment of primal chaos. In the *Enuma Elish*, she and her consort Apsu (the fresh water) are the first beings, from whom the gods are born. When the younger gods grow noisy and kill Apsu, the enraged Tiamat creates an army of eleven monsters and a new champion to destroy them. The young god Marduk faces her, catches her in his net, and splits her vast body in two — using one half to form the sky and the other the earth, her eyes becoming the sources of the Tigris and Euphrates. Tiamat is the ultimate "primordial chaos / cosmic ocean" figure: vast, formless, terrible, and the raw material from which the ordered world is made. **Crucial caution: she is famous in modern times as a five-headed dragon in Dungeons & Dragons — that specific design is owned by Wizards of the Coast and must be avoided; the ancient Tiamat is oceanic and formless.**

## Archetype
The Primordial Chaos / Chaos-Dragon / Devouring Mother / Cosmic Monster. The formless deep from which all order is wrenched — chaos as the mother of creation.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw — your own design)
A colossal, oceanic chaos-entity: a vast sea-serpent or dragon embodying the primordial salt ocean; formless yet terrible; surrounded by her brood of monsters; her body being split to form sky and sea. Render her as primordial ocean and storm — NOT as the D&D five-headed chromatic dragon. Deep-sea blues, blacks, and the churning abyss.

- **The eleven monsters** — her created brood.
- **The split body becoming the world** — creation from destruction.
- **The primordial waters** — the cosmic deep.

# Gilgamesh

> The hero-king of humanity's oldest story: two-thirds god and one-third man, who sought to defeat death itself — and learned, instead, what it means to be mortal.

## Who / What
Gilgamesh is the hero of the *Epic of Gilgamesh*, the oldest surviving great work of literature (Mesopotamian, written on clay tablets over four thousand years ago). He is the proud, tyrannical king of Uruk, two-thirds divine and one-third human. The gods create the wild man Enkidu to humble him; the two become inseparable friends and adventure together, slaying the forest-guardian Humbaba and the Bull of Heaven. When Enkidu dies, Gilgamesh is shattered by grief and terrified of his own mortality, and sets out on a desperate quest for the secret of eternal life — meeting Utnapishtim, the survivor of a great flood (the original of the Noah story), and briefly obtaining a plant of immortality, only to lose it to a serpent. He returns home having learned that immortality lies not in escaping death but in the lasting works (the great walls of Uruk) and the friendships one leaves behind.

## Archetype
The Hero-King / Seeker of Immortality / Transformed Tyrant / Grieving Friend. The original hero's journey — pride humbled, friendship found and lost, and the hard wisdom of accepting mortality.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A powerful, muscular bearded king with a lush, curled Mesopotamian beard; grasping a lion under one arm (a famous relief pose); the ziggurat-crowned city of Uruk and its great walls; the towering cedar forest; rendered in the ancient Mesopotamian relief / cylinder-seal art style (free and distinctive). Lapis blue and gold.

## Symbols
- **The walls of Uruk** — his lasting legacy; "immortality through works."
- **The cedar forest** — the great adventure.
- **The Bull of Heaven** — the slain divine beast.
- **The plant of immortality** — the prize lost to a serpent.
- **The clay tablet** — the oldest story, written.

## Themes
Heroism and kingship, friendship and its loss, mortality and the fear of death, the quest for immortality, hubris transformed into wisdom, legacy, the original flood story.

# Hera (Juno)

> The queen of Olympus: crowned, proud, peacock at her side, and merciless to her unfaithful husband's lovers.

## Who / What
Hera is the wife and sister of Zeus, queen of the gods, and goddess of marriage, women, and the family. The Romans called her Juno. She is dignified and sovereign, but her defining trait in myth is relentless jealousy toward Zeus's many lovers and their children — most famously her lifelong persecution of Heracles.

## Archetype
The Queen / Matriarch / Jealous Wife. The protector of the marriage bond who becomes its avenging fury when it is broken. A powerful study in wounded sovereignty.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
Regal, mature, crowned woman; a peacock with its eyed tail fanned; a diadem (the *polos* crown); a scepter often topped with a lotus or cuckoo; seated on a throne; veiled as a matron. Rich robes, gold, and royal bearing.

## Symbols
- **Peacock** — her sacred bird; the eyes of Argus set in its tail.
- **Diadem / crown** — her queenship.
- **Cuckoo** — how Zeus first wooed her.
- **Pomegranate** — fertility and marriage.
- **Cow** — her sacred animal ("cow-eyed Hera").

## Themes
Marriage and its sanctity, jealousy and vengeance, queenship and sovereignty, the dignity and the rage of the wronged woman, family and legitimacy.

## Reusable Concepts
The powerful spouse who avenges betrayal. The matriarch who guards the family's honor. The cycle of jealousy that punishes the innocent (Heracles never wronged her). The peacock as a symbol of watchful, all-seeing pride.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
Her persecution of Heracles (sending serpents to his cradle, driving him to madness). The setting of hundred-eyed Argus to guard Io. The judgment of Paris (she loses to Aphrodite). Her schemes against Zeus's lovers and offspring.

# Apollo

> The radiant god of the sun, music, and prophecy: a golden lyre in his hands, a laurel crown on his brow, and arrows that bring both healing and plague.

## Who / What
Apollo is the god of the sun and light, music and poetry, prophecy, archery, and healing — and, by the same hand, of plague. He is the twin brother of Artemis, son of Zeus and Leto. He presides over the Oracle at Delphi (which he won by slaying the serpent Python), leads the Muses, and embodies the Greek ideal of harmonious, rational beauty. The Romans kept his name. He carries a striking duality: the bringer of healing is also the sender of disease.

## Archetype
The Radiant Youth / Artist / Oracle. The idealized creative-rational god, but with a dark mirror (the same arrows heal and infect). A figure of light, order, and the arts.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A beautiful, beardless, athletic young man; a golden lyre or kithara; a laurel wreath; a radiant sun or halo; a silver bow and quiver; the tripod and the Delphic oracle setting; the slain serpent Python.

## Symbols
- **Lyre / kithara** — music and the Muses.
- **Laurel** — Daphne's transformation; victory and poetry.
- **Sun** — light and the day.
- **Bow and arrow** — far-shooting; healing and plague.
- **Raven** and **tripod** — prophecy and Delphi.

## Themes
Light and the sun, music and the arts, prophecy and fate, healing and disease, youth and ideal beauty, harmony and order, the duality of a gift that can also harm.

## Reusable Concepts
The artist-god whose creativity is also a weapon. The oracle who speaks fate in riddles. The light that both nurtures and burns. The unrequited pursuit (Daphne, who becomes the laurel). The contest of music (Apollo vs. Marsyas / Pan).

## Story / Narrative Hooks
The slaying of Python and the founding of the Delphic oracle. The pursuit of Daphne, who turns into a laurel tree. The flaying of Marsyas after a music contest. The plague he sends on the Greek camp in the Iliad. The story of Phaethon and the sun chariot (often linked).

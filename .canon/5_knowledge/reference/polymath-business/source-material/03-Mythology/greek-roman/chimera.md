# Chimera

> A lion with a goat's head sprouting from its back and a serpent for a tail, breathing fire — the impossible fusion that gave its name to every wild hybrid since.

## Who / What
The Chimera is a fire-breathing monster composed of three creatures fused into one: a lion's body and head, a goat's head rising from its back, and a serpent for a tail. Offspring of the monsters Typhon and Echidna, it terrorized the region of Lycia until the hero Bellerophon, riding Pegasus, killed it from the air (in one version by lodging a lump of lead in its throat, which the creature's own fiery breath melted). Its very name has become a word for any impossible or fantastical fusion — in biology, a "chimera" is an organism with cells of different genetic origins; in speech, a "chimera" is a wild, unattainable dream.

## Archetype
The Monster / Unnatural Hybrid / Fire-Breather / Impossible Thing. The creature that should not exist — a fusion of incompatible parts. The root metaphor for all mashups and hybrids.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A lion's powerful body and snarling head; a goat's head emerging from the middle of its back; a serpent or snake-headed tail lashing behind; gouts of fire from its mouth; an unsettling, impossible composite. Fiery oranges and a sense of wrongness.

## Symbols
- **Lion head and body** — strength and ferocity.
- **Goat head (from the back)** — the unnatural fusion.
- **Serpent tail** — venom and the chthonic.
- **Fire breath** — its deadly weapon.
- **Three-in-one** — the impossible composite.

## Themes
The impossible hybrid, fire and destruction, the unnatural and the monstrous, fusion of incompatible things, the fantastical, dreams that cannot be (the "chimera" of speech).

## Reusable Concepts
"A chimera" — the perfect word/concept for any improbable fusion, mashup, or unattainable dream (great for creative-brand branding). The monster of incompatible parts. The fire-breather defeated by cleverness, not strength (the lead lump). The thing that defies nature. Genetic/scientific chimerism for biotech themes.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
Its monstrous parentage (Typhon and Echidna, alongside Cerberus and the Hydra). Its slaying by Bellerophon from the back of Pegasus. The clever lead-melting kill. Its enduring life as a word for the impossible.

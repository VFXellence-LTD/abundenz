# Poseidon (Neptune)

> Lord of the sea: a trident raised over the waves, sea-foam horses at the gallop, and the power to shake the earth itself.

## Who / What
Poseidon is the god of the sea, earthquakes, storms, and horses, brother to Zeus and Hades. The Romans called him Neptune. He rules the deep with a three-pronged trident, and his moods drive the weather of the ocean. He is proud, easily slighted, and capable of vast destruction — he hounded Odysseus across the sea for a decade.

## Archetype
The Storm-Lord / Sea-King / Untamed Elder. The personification of nature that cannot be governed, only weathered. A brooding power who answers slights with floods and quakes.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A powerful bearded man rising from the waves; a three-pronged trident; a chariot drawn by hippocampi (horse-fish hybrids) or by foam-white horses; dolphins; a crown of coral or seaweed; the storming, white-capped sea as setting.

## Symbols
- **Trident** — his signature weapon and an iconic standalone symbol.
- **Dolphin** — his sacred and friendly creature.
- **Horse** — he created and is god of horses.
- **Hippocampus** — the mythical sea-horse that pulls his chariot.
- **Wave / conch shell** — the sea and its summons.

## Themes
The vast, untamed power of nature; wrath and grudges; freedom and the deep; the sea as both giver and destroyer; storms and upheaval.

## Reusable Concepts
The elemental force that can be neither bargained with nor controlled. The grudge that pursues a hero across the world. The contest for a city (Poseidon vs. Athena for Athens). The creation of the horse as a gift to humanity.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
His decade-long persecution of Odysseus. The contest with Athena for the patronage of Athens (his salt-spring vs. her olive tree). The creation of Pegasus from Medusa's blood. The sending of the sea-monster against Andromeda / against Troy.

# Meditations

> The private notebook of a Roman emperor — and the most-quoted, most-accidentally-infringed Stoic text on the internet.

## Public-domain status
Marcus Aurelius wrote his *Meditations* (originally untitled personal notes, in Greek) around 170–180 CE. The original is permanently free. **The critical trap here is the translation.** The version that dominates online quote graphics is **Gregory Hays's 2002 translation — which is under copyright.** Quoting Hays's modern phrasing on merchandise is a real infringement risk. Use a public-domain translation — **George Long** (Victorian, the classic free version), Meric Casaubon (1634), or C. R. Haines (Loeb, older) — or your own rendering. See [[traps/translations-and-editions]]. This entry is flagged because the Stoic-quote POD niche is enormous and the Hays-translation trap is the single most common mistake in it.

## What it is
A series of short, unconnected personal reflections the emperor wrote to himself, on campaign, as exercises in Stoic philosophy: reminders to accept what he cannot control, to do his duty, to remember that he will die, to treat others with patience, and to keep his mind ("the inner citadel") free of disturbance regardless of external chaos.

## Themes & symbols
- **Themes:** Stoic acceptance; *memento mori* (remember death); self-discipline; virtue as the only good; impermanence; duty; the serenity of the governed mind.
- **Symbols:** the inner citadel, the flowing river of time, the flame, the philosopher-emperor.
- **Archetypes:** the sage, the philosopher-king, the stoic.

## Visual & emotional palette
A Roman marble bust, a single candle flame, a calm mountain, laurel and stone. Calm, resolute, grounding. Palette: marble white, deep ink, gold accent, slate, laurel green.

## The trap (if any)
**The translation is the trap, and it is a serious one.** Use George Long or another public-domain translation, never Hays's 2002 wording. Verify the exact wording you put on a product traces to a pre-cutoff translation.

- Social: a "daily Stoic" series.
- Podcast/blog: Stoic practice for modern life.

# Metamorphoses

> Rome's grand catalogue of transformation myths — the source for half the Greek and Roman myths people actually know.

## Public-domain status
Ovid's *Metamorphoses* was completed around 8 CE; the original Latin is permanently free. Use a public-domain translation — Arthur Golding's Elizabethan version (the one Shakespeare read), Brookes More, or the Dryden/Garth collaborative version — or your own. Avoid copying a recent in-copyright translation (e.g. Stephanie McCarter's 2022, Charles Martin's, Allen Mandelbaum's). See [[traps/translations-and-editions]].

## What it is
A 15-book Latin narrative poem stringing together over 250 myths, all linked by the theme of transformation — gods and mortals turning into animals, plants, stars, and stone. It is the single richest source for the famous transformation myths: Daphne turning into a laurel tree to escape Apollo; Narcissus wasting away at his own reflection and becoming a flower; Echo fading to a voice; Icarus's wax wings melting; Pygmalion's statue coming to life; King Midas's golden touch; Arachne the weaver turned into a spider; Phaethon's catastrophic ride of the sun-chariot.

## Themes & symbols
- **Themes:** transformation as the universe's only constant; desire and its consequences; divine cruelty and caprice; the power of art; love in all its forms; punishment and metamorphosis as fate.
- **Symbols:** the laurel tree (Daphne), the narcissus flower, the golden touch (Midas), wax wings (Icarus), the rival tapestries (Arachne), the awakened statue (Pygmalion).
- **Archetypes:** the shapeshifter, the doomed lover, the punished mortal, the jealous god, the over-reaching artist.

## Visual & emotional palette
Bodies caught mid-transformation — fingers becoming twigs, skin becoming bark, a figure falling from the sky. Reflections in still water, statues awakening, gods in disguise. Sensual, uncanny, lyrical, often tragic. Palette: marble white, laurel green, water-silver, gold, sky-blue with a falling figure.

## The trap (if any)
No trap in the source. Translation trap only. Note that many famous *paintings* of these myths (Bernini's *Apollo and Daphne*, Waterhouse's *Echo and Narcissus*) are themselves public domain — see Section 06 — and make excellent reference, but verify each artwork's date.

## Applications / remix hooks
- Poster/AI image: a "transformation" series — Daphne/laurel, Narcissus/reflection, Icarus/fall.
- POD: minimal myth icons with the myth's name.
- Podcast/story: one transformation myth per episode.
- Blog: myth explainers tied to modern themes (vanity = Narcissus, overreach = Icarus).

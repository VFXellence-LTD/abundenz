# The Canterbury Tales

> A cross-section of medieval society on the road — and a storytelling contest that invented the English narrative frame.

## Public-domain status
Chaucer's *Canterbury Tales* was written c. 1387–1400; the original Middle English is permanently free. **Watch the modernization trap:** a modern English rendering such as Nevill Coghill's popular 1951 Penguin verse modernization is **copyrighted.** Use the original Middle English (free), a public-domain modernization, or your own. The Ellesmere Manuscript's famous portraits of the pilgrims are public domain. See [[traps/translations-and-editions]].

## What it is
A group of pilgrims travelling from London to the shrine of Thomas Becket at Canterbury agree to a storytelling contest to pass the journey, with the host of the Tabard Inn as judge. The frame lets Chaucer parade the entire spectrum of medieval society — a noble Knight, a bawdy Miller, a worldly Wife of Bath, a corrupt Pardoner, a gentle Parson, a Prioress, a Merchant, a Cook — each telling a tale that reflects their character. The result is by turns courtly, filthy, pious, and savagely satirical, and it is the foundational work of English literature.

## Themes & symbols
- **Themes:** the pilgrimage of life; the whole of human society in one company; storytelling as competition and self-revelation; satire of corruption and hypocrisy (especially the Church); love and lust; folly and wisdom.
- **Symbols:** the pilgrim road, the Tabard Inn, the Canterbury shrine, the assembled company of estates.
- **Archetypes:** the pilgrim, the rogue, the pious fraud (the Pardoner), the bold woman (the Wife of Bath), the ideal Knight.

## Visual & emotional palette
A line of mounted medieval travelers on a spring road; a busy roadside inn; illuminated-manuscript portraits. Earthy, comic, humane, vivid. Palette: illuminated-manuscript gold and vermilion, spring green, road-dust brown.

## The trap (if any)
No trap in the source; the modernization trap is the concern. Use the Middle English original or your own modern rendering; never lift Coghill's or another copyrighted modernization.

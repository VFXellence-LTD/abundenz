# Lion Rampant (Heraldic)

> The rearing lion of courage, royalty, and martial valor.

## Meaning
The lion is the most common charge in European heraldry, symbolizing courage, royalty, strength, nobility, valor, and dominion ("the king of beasts" as the king's emblem). The "rampant" posture — rearing up on the hind legs with forepaws raised and claws bared, shown in profile — is the most iconic, conveying readiness for battle and fierce nobility. Other postures (passant, statant, sejant) carry subtler meanings.

## Origin
A staple of medieval heraldry across Europe from the 12th century. The generic charge is PD. **Caution**: specific royal and national arms (the Royal Banner of Scotland's red lion rampant, the three lions of England) and countless corporate lion logos are protected — design your own generic lion, never reproduce a specific coat of arms or live brand.

## Visual description
A lion shown in profile, rearing on one hind leg with the other raised, forepaws extended and claws spread, head in profile, tongue and tail flourishing. Render in vintage-engraving, woodcut, gold-on-crest, or bold modern crest styling.

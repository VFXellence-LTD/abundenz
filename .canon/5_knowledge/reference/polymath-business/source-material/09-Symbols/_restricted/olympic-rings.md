# Olympic Rings — DO NOT USE

> Protected far beyond ordinary trademark. Statute + treaty. No commercial, decorative, or "inspired-by" use.

## Why it is NOT free

- **Not PD by age** — the rings were designed by Pierre de Coubertin in 1913; this is a 20th-century work, not an ancient symbol.
- **US statute** — the **Ted Stevens Olympic and Amateur Sports Act** (36 U.S.C. § 220506) grants the USOPC exclusive rights to the symbol of five interlocking rings and to the words "Olympic," "Paralympic," etc. Crucially, the USOPC can enforce **without proving likelihood of consumer confusion** — a far stronger right than ordinary trademark.
- **International treaty** — the **1981 Nairobi Treaty on the Protection of the Olympic Symbol** obliges member states to refuse/invalidate marks consisting of the Olympic symbol and to prohibit unauthorized commercial use.

## Doctrine

**Never reproduce, imitate, or evoke** the five rings — including stylized "inspired-by" versions or a laurel/torch/podium lockup that reads as Olympic. The generic [[../botanical/laurel-wreath]] is fine on its own; an Olympic-evoking composition is not.

## Bottom line

**Recognize and reject. Statute + treaty protected. No commercial, decorative, or evocative use.**

# Swastika — DO NOT USE COMMERCIALLY

> Ancient and sacred in Asia; in the West it reads as the Nazi Hakenkreuz. The canonical "benign origin, never-use-commercially" symbol.

## The dual nature (do not flatten either side)

- **Sacred / living**: The swastika is among the oldest symbols on Earth and remains an **auspicious, sacred living symbol** in Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism (Sanskrit *svastika*, "well-being"; Japanese *manji*). It appears on temples, thresholds, and in religious art across Asia today. Disrespecting it as inherently "evil" erases a living faith tradition.
- **Hate-appropriated**: The Nazis adopted a 45°-canted black swastika (the *Hakenkreuz*) as the emblem of the Third Reich. In Western and most commercial contexts the symbol is **overwhelmingly read as a hate symbol** and is illegal to display in Germany, Austria, and several other countries.

## Doctrine

**Never use commercially or decoratively in Western markets.** Permissible only in genuine editorial, historical, or religious depiction with explicit, unambiguous context (e.g. documenting Hindu temple art, or teaching history). The pipeline's job is to **recognize and reject** it for product use.

## Bottom line

**Recognize and reject for any commercial/decorative use. Editorial/historical/religious context only, never as merch or branding.**

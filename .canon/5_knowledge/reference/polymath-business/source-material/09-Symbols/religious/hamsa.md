# Hamsa (Hand of Fatima / Hand of Miriam)

> The protective open hand that wards off the evil eye — shared across faiths. Treat with respect.

## Meaning
The hamsa (Arabic *khamsa*, "five") is an open-hand amulet that wards off the evil eye and brings protection, blessing, and good fortune. It is shared across Middle Eastern and North African cultures and three Abrahamic faiths: in Islam it is the "Hand of Fatima" (daughter of the Prophet); in Judaism the "Hand of Miriam" (sister of Moses). Often an eye is set in the palm to repel the malevolent gaze.

## Origin
Ancient, predating the Abrahamic faiths (linked to earlier Phoenician/Mesopotamian protective hands), later absorbed into Jewish, Muslim, and regional folk practice. Fully PD. It carries **religious significance** to living communities — respect required, though it is widely used in jewelry and decor.

## Visual description
A stylized, often symmetrical open right hand — typically with the thumb and little finger of equal length splayed outward, mirroring each other. Frequently decorated with an eye in the palm, florals, filigree, or the [[evil-eye-nazar]]. Render in boho/spiritual decor styling, ornate filigree, or jewelry form.

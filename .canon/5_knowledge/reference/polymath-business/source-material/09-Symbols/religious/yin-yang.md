# Yin-Yang (Taijitu)

> The flowing circle of complementary opposites — balance, harmony, and the Tao.

## Meaning
The yin-yang (Taijitu, "diagram of the supreme ultimate") represents the Taoist principle that all things arise from and contain their opposites — dark/light, feminine/masculine, passive/active, cold/hot — held in dynamic, flowing balance. Each half contains a seed of the other (the small dots), signifying that nothing is purely one thing and that opposites are interdependent and ever-transforming. It is the emblem of harmony, balance, and wholeness.

## Origin
Rooted in ancient Chinese Taoist philosophy; the familiar circular Taijitu was formalized by the Song-dynasty philosopher Zhou Dunyi (11th c.) building on much older concepts. Fully PD. It stems from a **living philosophy/religion (Taoism)** but is broadly used decoratively worldwide with little friction; still, frame it thoughtfully.

## Visual description
A circle divided by an S-shaped curve into two equal teardrop halves — one black (yin), one white (yang) — each containing a small dot of the opposite color. The S-curve gives a sense of rotation/flow. Render as crisp monochrome, with custom dual-element fills (sun/moon, fire/water, two koi), or minimalist.

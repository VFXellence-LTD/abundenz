# Scarab Beetle

> The dung beetle that rolls the sun — Egypt's emblem of rebirth and transformation.

## Meaning
The scarab (dung beetle) was sacred in ancient Egypt as a symbol of rebirth, transformation, the rising sun, regeneration, and protection. The Egyptians observed the beetle rolling a ball of dung and associated it with the god Khepri, who rolled the sun across the sky each dawn — making the scarab an emblem of the cyclical renewal of life and the sun's daily rebirth. Heart scarabs were placed with the dead to ensure rebirth in the afterlife.

## Origin
Ancient Egyptian, ubiquitous as amulets, seals, and jewelry from the Old Kingdom onward (the god Khepri appears in the earliest periods). Fully PD. Egyptian-revival styles (Art Deco) later popularized the jeweled scarab. Respect Kemetic spiritual use.

A beetle seen from above — oval body with a segmented carapace, a triangular/notched head, and six legs. Often depicted rolling or flanking a solar disc, or with outstretched wings (winged scarab). Render in gold-and-lapis jeweled style, hieroglyphic line, or Art Deco Egyptian-revival.

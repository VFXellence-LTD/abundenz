# PD Source vs Modern Adaptation

> The most important practical table in this section. A public-domain *story* is free. A later studio's specific *design* of that story is its own, still-protected work. **Use the original. Draw your own.**

## Summary

When an old, public-domain work gets re-made by a studio, the studio adds **new, original, protectable expression** — a specific costume, makeup, color scheme, character design, added characters, new dialogue, a distinctive look. That new layer gets its **own** copyright (and often trademark). The underlying old tale stays free; the modern additions do not.

So the rule for every classic property is the same two-step:

1. **Go to the public-domain original** (the old book / ancient myth / traditional tale).
2. **Create your own fresh expression** from it — your own art, your own design, your own added details.

Never copy the **distinctive elements that the modern adaptation invented**. Those are the restricted parts.

## SAFE vs RESTRICTED — side by side

| Property | SAFE — public-domain original | RESTRICTED — modern adaptation (do not copy) |
|---|---|---|
| **Fairy tales (Cinderella, Snow White, Little Mermaid, Sleeping Beauty, Beauty & the Beast)** | Grimm / Perrault / Andersen original texts (early 1800s); your own illustrations; the plot and characters as described in the old tale | **Disney's specific designs** — Cinderella's blue ball gown & glass-slipper look, Ariel's red hair/green-tail/purple-shell look, Snow White's exact dress, the Disney castle; Disney character names where trademarked; added Disney songs |
| **Sherlock Holmes** | Conan Doyle stories **published 1930 or earlier** (the bulk of the canon); the characters Holmes & Watson as written in those stories; 221B Baker Street | The **estate-asserted late-story character traits**; **BBC *Sherlock*** look & characterization; **Robert Downey Jr. film** designs; specific film/TV scripts; later illustrations' distinctive "look" |
| **Dracula** | Bram Stoker's 1897 novel text; Count Dracula as **described in the novel** (older aristocrat, hairy palms, moustache); Transylvania setting; your own vampire design | **Universal's Bela Lugosi Dracula** (slicked hair, cape, medallion, accent) and **Lugosi's likeness/persona**; the Hammer **Christopher Lee** Dracula; any specific film design |
| **Frankenstein** | Mary Shelley's 1818 novel text; the Creature as **described by Shelley** (yellow skin, watery eyes, black lips, articulate and tall — NOT bolt-necked); Victor Frankenstein the character | **Universal's 1931 monster** — flat head, neck bolts, green skin, Karloff makeup (a copyrighted design + Karloff likeness); the "IT'S ALIVE" specific staging |
| **Wizard of Oz** | L. Frank Baum's 1900 book text; Dorothy, Scarecrow, Tin Man, Cowardly Lion, the Wizard; **silver** shoes (book); Emerald City as described | **MGM's 1939 film** — **ruby** slippers, Judy-Garland Dorothy's specific look, the green Wicked Witch makeup, Munchkin designs, the film's distinctive Technicolor styling, the song "Over the Rainbow" |
| **Greek/Norse myth** | The myths themselves (Zeus, Athena, Thor, Odin, Medusa, Hercules/Heracles); ancient depictions; your own designs | **Marvel's Thor/Loki/Odin** designs & characterizations; **God of War's Kratos/Atreus and its specific gods**; **Disney's *Hercules*** designs; **Rick Riordan's Percy Jackson** characters |
| **Pinocchio** | Carlo Collodi's 1883 *The Adventures of Pinocchio* text; the puppet who lies & whose nose grows; Geppetto, the Blue Fairy as written | **Disney's 1940 Pinocchio** — the specific yellow-hat/red-shorts/bow-tie design, Jiminy Cricket (a Disney addition), the Disney visual styling; the 2022 Disney remake |
| **Peter Pan** | J.M. Barrie's story (note: UK has a special perpetual royalty for the hospital, and US PD status of specific versions varies — treat as caution) | **Disney's Peter Pan** design (green outfit specifics, the Disney Tinker Bell look — Tinker Bell is a Disney trademark/logo); film scripts |
| **Alice in Wonderland** | Lewis Carroll's 1865/1871 text; **Tenniel's original 1865 illustrations** (PD); your own designs | **Disney's 1951 Alice** (blue-dress/white-apron specific design), the Tim Burton film designs, modern illustrated editions |
| **A Christmas Carol** | Dickens' 1843 text; Scrooge, Marley, the Ghosts as written; your own illustrations | Specific **film/stage adaptations'** scripts, designs, added scenes (e.g. the Muppets' versions, specific movie ghost designs) |
| **Robin Hood / King Arthur** | The medieval legends; characters and stories as in old ballads/Malory's *Le Morte d'Arthur* | **Disney's *Robin Hood*** (the fox design), **specific film/TV Arthurs** (e.g. *Excalibur*, BBC *Merlin* designs), the Marvel/DC takes |

## The principle behind the table

| What you copy | Status |
|---|---|
| The old PD **story, plot, characters-as-written, setting** | **SAFE** — it's public domain |
| Your **own new visual design** built from the PD description | **SAFE** — it's your original work |
| The modern adaptation's **specific invented design / look / makeup / costume / added characters / added songs / distinctive styling** | **RESTRICTED** — that's the studio's own copyright |
| A **trademarked character name or logo** used commercially | **RESTRICTED** — trademark, regardless of copyright |
| A real **actor's likeness** from the film | **RESTRICTED** — right of publicity |

## How an AI should apply this

When a prompt references a classic property:
1. **Split** the request into the PD core (free) and any modern-adaptation specifics (restricted).
2. If the prompt asks for the **modern design** (ruby slippers, Universal's bolt-neck monster, Disney's Ariel) → `avoid`; steer to the PD original detail (silver slippers, Shelley's described Creature, a generic mermaid).
3. If the prompt asks for the **PD original + an original design** → `safe`.
4. Always **prefer the original text's described details** over the famous film's details when they differ (silver not ruby; articulate not grunting monster).
5. Run the trademark/publicity checks from [[copyright-vs-trademark-vs-likeness]] on character names and any real actors.

## Examples

- **SAFE:** "A vampire count in a Transylvanian castle, my own design, based on Stoker's 1897 description" → `safe`.
- **AVOID:** "Bela Lugosi-style Dracula with cape and medallion" → `avoid` (Universal design + Lugosi likeness).
- **SAFE:** "Dorothy in silver shoes following a yellow brick road, original art" → `safe` (Baum's book).
- **AVOID:** "Dorothy in ruby slippers, MGM style" → `avoid` (MGM's added design).
- **SAFE:** "Thor the Norse thunder god, ancient-styled, my own design" → `safe`.
- **AVOID:** "Marvel's Thor" → `avoid`.

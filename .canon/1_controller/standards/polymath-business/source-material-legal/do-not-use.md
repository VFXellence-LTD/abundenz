# Do Not Use

> The red-light bucket. No realistic safe commercial use exists without a license. There is no clever loophole. If a candidate lands here, the correct action is to **stop and pick a public-domain or original alternative.**

## Summary

Everything in this list will, if used commercially without a license, expose VFXellence to takedowns, platform/account termination (POD platforms ban repeat infringers fast), statutory copyright damages (which can be large), trademark claims, publicity claims, or — for hate symbols — brand destruction and ethical harm. These are not "risky but tempting" — they are simply **off the table**. Redirect to [[safe-to-use]].

---

## 1. Copyrighted characters (still in copyright)

**Why no:** Characters from works still under copyright are protected by copyright and usually trademark too. Using their name, likeness, or distinctive traits on products infringes.

**Examples:** Mickey Mouse (modern design), Harry Potter, Spider-Man / Batman / all Marvel & DC heroes, Pikachu and all Pokémon, Mario / Zelda / Nintendo characters, the Minions, Elsa/Anna and all modern Disney/Pixar characters, SpongeBob, Hello Kitty, Baby Yoda/Grogu, anime characters (Naruto, Goku, etc.), Peppa Pig, Paw Patrol.

**No workaround:** Renaming, "fan art for sale," slight stylistic changes, or "tribute" framing do not cure infringement on commercial products.

---

## 2. Modern film/studio designs of public-domain characters

**Why no:** The *character* may be PD (from old text), but the **specific 20th/21st-century visual design** is its own protected work. This is the single most common trap. See [[pd-source-vs-modern-adaptation]].

**Examples:**
- **Frankenstein:** Shelley's text is PD; **Universal's 1931 flat-head, neck-bolt, green monster design** (Boris Karloff makeup) is restricted.
- **Dracula:** Stoker's novel is PD; **Bela Lugosi's specific Universal Dracula look** and Lugosi's likeness are restricted.
- **Wizard of Oz:** Baum's 1900 book is PD; **MGM's 1939 ruby slippers, the specific Judy-Garland Dorothy look, the green Wicked Witch makeup, the Munchkin designs** are restricted (the book's slippers were *silver*).
- **Sherlock Holmes:** early stories PD; the **deerstalker-and-Inverness "look"** is largely from illustrations/films and the **modern BBC/RDJ designs** are restricted.
- **Peter Pan, Pinocchio, Cinderella, Snow White, The Little Mermaid, Alice, The Jungle Book:** the tales are PD; **Disney's specific character designs** are restricted.

**Safe alternative:** Use the **PD original text/description** and create your **own** visual design that does not copy the studio version. Make a monster, not *the* Universal monster.

---

## 3. Protected logos & brand marks

**Why no:** Logos are core trademarks; commercial use creates source confusion and dilution claims.

**Examples:** Nike swoosh, Apple logo, the Coca-Cola script, McDonald's golden arches, Adidas trefoil/three-stripes, sports-team logos and uniforms (NFL/NBA/MLB/FIFA/clubs), car-brand badges, the Olympic rings (specially protected by statute in many countries), university logos and mascots.

**No workaround:** "Inspired by," recoloring, or partial reproduction still infringes when it evokes the mark.

---

## 4. Corporate mascots

**Why no:** Mascots are trademarked brand characters, often also copyrighted.

**Examples:** Tony the Tiger, the Michelin Man, Ronald McDonald, the Geico Gecko, the M&M's characters, Mr. Clean, the Pillsbury Doughboy, the Kool-Aid Man, Duracell Bunny / Energizer Bunny, brand "esports/streamer" mascots.

**Safe alternative:** Invent your **own** mascot (Polymath uses the Z-naming convention for invented brand characters — see [[README]]).

---

## 5. Celebrity & real-person likenesses (right of publicity)

**Why no:** Recognizable living people (and many deceased) have publicity rights; commercial use without a release infringes. Photos additionally carry the photographer's copyright.

**Examples:** Any living celebrity, musician, athlete, influencer, politician's face on merch; actively-managed deceased icons (Elvis, Marilyn Monroe, Einstein, Bruce Lee, Audrey Hepburn — their estates license aggressively); AI-generated images/voices that clearly depict or imitate a real person.

**Safe alternative:** Use long-dead historical figures with no active estate, used factually (Lincoln, Cleopatra, Beethoven), or fully fictional original characters. See [[use-with-caution]] (right of publicity) for the narrow editorial exceptions.

---

## 6. Copyrighted artwork & photography (in copyright)

**Why no:** Modern artworks, illustrations, and photographs are protected for the artist's life + 70 years (or 95 years for older corporate works). Copying or close imitation infringes.

**Examples:** Most 20th/21st-century paintings and illustrations still in term; specific famous photographs (the *Afghan Girl*, most iconic press photos); a living illustrator's signature characters/style applied to copy their actual work; stock images used outside their license; **AI outputs prompted "in the style of [named living artist]"** that reproduce that artist's distinctive expression.

**Safe alternative:** PD artworks (Old Masters, pre-1931 art), CC0 imagery, or original/AI work prompted from generic genres and PD references. See [[safe-to-use]].

---

## 7. Franchise assets

**Why no:** Franchises bundle copyright + trademark and are policed by dedicated legal teams; there is no unlicensed commercial lane.

**Examples:** Star Wars (ships, droids, lightsabers, names, "May the Force be with you" as merch), the Marvel/DC universes, *Lord of the Rings*/Middle-earth names and designs, *Harry Potter* spells/houses/crests, *Game of Thrones* sigils, Pokémon, Star Trek insignia, *Stranger Things* logo/typeface, Nintendo worlds.

**Safe alternative:** Build **original** worlds and genres (space opera, high fantasy, etc. are *genres* and free; specific franchise assets are not).

---

## 8. Hate symbols & prohibited imagery

**Why no:** Beyond legal exposure (illegal in several countries), these cause irreversible **brand and ethical** damage and instant platform bans. This is a hard ethical line independent of copyright.

**Examples:** The Nazi swastika and SS insignia, other neo-Nazi/white-supremacist symbols and codes, terrorist-organization imagery, symbols on recognized hate-symbol databases (e.g. ADL's), and content promoting violence or hatred toward protected groups.

**Important nuance:** The **swastika** has an ancient, benign religious meaning in Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism (manji/sauvastika) that long predates its Nazi appropriation. For a Western commercial content business, **avoid the symbol entirely** to prevent misreading and harm — the ancient meaning does not neutralize modern perception in our markets. There is no commercial upside that justifies the risk. Hard avoid.

**No workaround:** No "ironic," "historical," or "reclaimed" framing makes these acceptable for Polymath's commercial output.

---

## How an AI should apply this

If a candidate matches **any** category above, the verdict is immediately `risk_level: avoid`. Do **not** attempt a workaround, a rename, a recolor, or a "tribute." Instead:

1. Emit `avoid` with the specific category cited in `risk_notes`.
2. Offer a **safe substitution** from [[safe-to-use]] (PD original, ancient myth, folklore, original concept, invented Z-named character).
3. For modern designs of PD characters, route the user to the **PD original** and an **original visual design** (see [[pd-source-vs-modern-adaptation]]).
4. For hate symbols, refuse outright — no substitution discussion of the symbol itself.

## Examples

- **AVOID → substitute:** "Pikachu on a t-shirt" → `avoid`; suggest an original "electric forest creature" design.
- **AVOID → substitute:** "Universal's Frankenstein monster poster" → `avoid`; suggest your own monster built from Shelley's PD 1818 *description*.
- **AVOID → substitute:** "MGM ruby slippers art" → `avoid`; suggest **silver** slippers from Baum's PD 1900 book.
- **AVOID:** "Taylor Swift face on merch" → `avoid` (right of publicity).
- **AVOID:** "Nike swoosh remix" → `avoid` (trademark).
- **HARD AVOID:** any hate symbol → refuse.

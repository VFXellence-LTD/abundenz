# Regaliz Jewel Tones

> Amethyst purple, emerald green, and sapphire blue, lifted by gold — a deep, saturated jewel-tone system for premium and brand work.

## Swatches & roles

| Role | Hex | Name | Notes |
|------|-----|------|-------|
| Primary | `#4B1248` | Amethyst | Deep royal purple; the dominant jewel |
| Secondary | `#0F5C4A` | Emerald | Rich green; second jewel |
| Tertiary | `#1B3A8C` | Sapphire | Deep blue; third jewel / cool balance |
| Accent | `#C9A227` | Antique Gold | The unifier; line-work, type, ornament |
| Background (light) | `#F3ECDD` | Champagne | Warm off-white ground for light layouts |

## Structure

A **tri-tone** of three saturated, equally-weighted jewel hues (amethyst, emerald, sapphire) tied together by a single gold accent. The three jewels are roughly evenly spaced around the wheel (a loose triadic), so they're vivid yet balanced; gold harmonizes all three and signals luxury. Deep and rich rather than bright.

## Mood & emotional read

Luxurious, rich, regal, opulent, confident. Reads as gemstones, royalty, velvet, heraldry, and premium quality. The library's go-to for "expensive", "magical", and "high-end" without going cold or minimal. Warm gold keeps it inviting.

## Usage notes

Pick **one** jewel as the dominant ground per piece (e.g. amethyst), use the other two as supporting feature colors, and let gold do the fine line-work, ornament, and type. Gold is precious — line, not fill. Champagne is the alternative light ground when you want a brighter luxury feel. The palette loves ornament, damask, and symmetrical decorative framing. Proportion: one jewel dominant (~60%), two jewels supporting (~30%), gold ~10%.

## POD / print considerations

- **CMYK**: `partial` — saturated jewel tones, especially **emerald and bright sapphire, can shift or dull** in CMYK (greens and deep blues are gamut-edge cases). Build emerald with strong cyan + yellow + some black to hold depth; proof if the product is premium. Amethyst (purple) is also gamut-sensitive — expect a slight shift and lean it toward the magenta side to keep it rich. Gold prints as flat ochre; use **gold foil** for true metallic on premium products.
- **Apparel**: Excellent on **black and dark garments** (jewels glow on dark fabric, gold pops). On white, the deep jewels read rich but lose the "velvet" depth; consider gold + one jewel as line-art for light tees. Foil or metallic-gold detailing elevates apparel toward the premium band.
- **Accessibility**: Gold or champagne on any of the deep jewels passes large-text contrast; use champagne for small body text (gold is borderline at small sizes). Jewel-on-jewel for text is too low-contrast — avoid.

## Remix hooks

- Heraldic luxury: add gold ornament/line-art framing for a crest/coat-of-arms feel.
- Per-line gemstone: swap one jewel for `#7A1028` ruby or `#B8860B` topaz to differentiate product lines.
- Ultra-premium: darken grounds toward near-black so jewels read as backlit gems.

## Legal / risk

Saturated jewel hues are generic and unownable — safe across the use cases here. One narrow trademark note: **Cadbury/Milka's purple is protected only as packaging for chocolate** in some jurisdictions — do not use a near-identical purple as packaging for a chocolate product. Outside that specific category, jewel purple (and our amethyst, which is deeper/redder) is free to use. Keep logos and wordmarks original.

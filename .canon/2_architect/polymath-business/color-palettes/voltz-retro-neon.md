# Voltz Retro Neon

> Midnight purple lit by hot-pink and electric-cyan — the 1980s synthwave glow for retro-tech, arcade, and night-city work.

## Swatches & roles

| Role | Hex | Name | Notes |
|------|-----|------|-------|
| Primary | `#1A0B2E` | Midnight Violet | Deep night ground; everything glows against it |
| Accent 1 | `#FF2A8D` | Hot Magenta | The headline neon; type, glow, sunset |
| Accent 2 | `#00E5FF` | Electric Cyan | Cool neon; grid lines, edges, secondary glow |
| Accent 3 | `#9D4DFF` | Laser Violet | Mid purple; gradients between magenta and cyan |
| Highlight | `#FFD166` | Sun Gold | Warm pop; retro sun, stars, contrast point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Structure

**Thematic with a triadic core** — hot magenta, electric cyan, and laser violet form the glowing trio against a near-black violet ground. Sun gold is the single warm note that keeps the cool neons from feeling cold. The palette is built around *luminosity*: bright saturated hues on a dark field so everything appears to emit light.

## Mood & emotional read

Electric, nostalgic, futuristic-yet-retro, nocturnal, fun. Reads instantly as "1980s / synthwave / arcade / retro-future". High energy and dreamy at once. Strong for music, gaming, tech, and nostalgia-driven content.

## Usage notes

Midnight violet is *always* the ground — neon only works on dark. Build glows by layering magenta/cyan with soft outer blur. Use sun gold sparingly as the single warm focal point (a sun, a star, a highlight). Cyan for the classic perspective-grid; magenta for the headline. Add subtle scanlines/chrome type for full effect. Proportion: dark ground dominant, neons as light sources, not fills.

## POD / print considerations

- **CMYK**: `cmyk_safe: false` — these are **RGB neon colors that CMYK fundamentally cannot reproduce.** On a standard press, hot magenta and electric cyan will look noticeably duller and the "glow" flattens. This palette is at its best on **screens (social, video, AI-video)**. For print: use fluorescent screen-print inks, neon vinyl, or accept a muted version and design accordingly; warn buyers that printed neons won't match the screen preview.
- **Apparel**: Strong on **black garments** with fluorescent/neon plastisol inks. Standard DTG will mute the neons — manage expectations. Never attempt on white shirts (the dark-ground concept collapses).
- **Accessibility**: High contrast against the dark ground (good). But neon-on-neon (e.g. cyan text touching magenta) causes visual vibration/strobing — keep text on the dark ground only, never neon-on-neon.

## Remix hooks

- Minimal: two-color reduction, hot magenta + electric cyan only.
- Calmer night: swap midnight violet for deep navy `#0A1A3F`.
- Chrome type: add a silver gradient (`#E8E8E8` → `#9AA3AD`) for classic 80s chrome lettering.

## Legal / risk

Neon color combinations are not copyrightable, and "synthwave/retro-80s" is a broad genre aesthetic, not an owned property — safe. Avoid copying a *specific* album cover, video-game key art, or film logo (those individual artworks are copyrighted). Build original grids, suns, and type. Main practical caveat is print accuracy (CMYK), not legality.

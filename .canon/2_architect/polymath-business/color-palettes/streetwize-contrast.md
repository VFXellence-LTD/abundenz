# Streetwize High-Contrast

> Pure black, one screaming accent, and bright white — the maximum-contrast duotone that streetwear graphics live on.

## Swatches & roles

| Role | Hex | Name | Notes |
|------|-----|------|-------|
| Primary | `#0A0A0A` | True Black | The ground; carries everything |
| Accent | `#E8FF00` | Hazard Lime | The "hit" — one loud color does all the shouting (swappable) |
| Highlight / Text | `#FAFAFA` | Paper White | Clean white for type and negative space |

## Structure

A **duotone** plus white: one near-black, one high-saturation accent. The whole system runs on extreme value contrast so graphics read from across a room and survive being shrunk to a phone thumbnail. The accent is intentionally swappable per "drop".

## Mood & emotional read

Loud, confident, youthful, a little aggressive. Reads as hype/streetwear/skate/gym culture. Designed to grab attention in a feed and on a rack. High energy, low subtlety — that's the point.

## Usage notes

Black is the field; **one** accent only (resist adding a second loud color — it kills the impact). White handles type and breathing room. Lean on oversized type, tight letter-spacing, and bold shapes. Proportion ~75% black, ~15% white, ~10% accent. Rotate the accent color per collection (lime, hot-pink `#FF2D78`, electric-orange `#FF5A1F`, cyan `#00E5FF`) to refresh without redesigning.

## Pairs with (sections / categories)

Streetwear and merch lines, gym/fitness apparel, music-adjacent graphics, bold slogan tees. Pairs with [[13-Visual-Motifs/bold-type]] and [[13-Visual-Motifs/blackletter]]. Complements [[voltz-retro-neon]] for digital/social graphics.

## POD / print considerations

- **CMYK**: `cmyk_safe: false` — the whole point is **RGB-bright neon accents that CMYK cannot reproduce.** Hazard lime, electric cyan, and hot-pink will print noticeably duller on standard 4-color presses. For true vividness use **screen-printing with a spot/fluorescent ink** or DTG on dark fabric where the accent is laid thick. If the product is CMYK-only (most posters/mugs), warn the buyer or pick a less neon accent.
- **Apparel**: Built for **black garments** — neon-on-black is the strongest, cheapest-looking-expensive combo in POD. On white shirts, swap to black + accent with the accent doing the heavy lifting; white-on-white is impossible so never rely on the white role on light fabric.
- **Accessibility**: Maximum contrast by design — white-on-black and most accents-on-black pass AAA easily. Accent-on-white (e.g. lime on white) fails contrast — never put text in the neon on a light ground.

## Remix hooks

- Drop rotation: lime → hot-pink → electric-orange → cyan, one per release.
- Three-tone: add `#6B6B6B` mid-grey for depth on larger graphics.
- Off-duty: invert to white ground with black graphic + small accent.

## Legal / risk

Color combinations are not copyrightable and black+neon is generic streetwear — safe. The real risk in this category is **graphic execution**: do not reproduce or closely imitate any existing brand's logo, mascot, slogan, or signature graphic. Keep type and artwork original. Avoid using this palette to dress up anything that reads as a knockoff of a known label.

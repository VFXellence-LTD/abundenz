# Sherlock Holmes

## Snapshot

The world's most famous consulting detective, created by Arthur Conan Doyle, debuting in *A Study in Scarlet* (1887). Solves crimes through observation and deductive reasoning from his Baker Street rooms, accompanied by Dr John Watson.

## Copyright status (the text/expression)

Fully public domain in the US. The Holmes canon is 56 short stories and 4 novels published 1887–1927. The earlier works entered the public domain over the preceding decades; the final collection, *The Case-Book of Sherlock Holmes* (1927), entered the US public domain on **January 1, 2023**. Every Holmes story and every character trait Doyle wrote is now free to use. In the UK and most life+70 countries, Holmes has been PD since 2001 (Doyle died 1930).

This closes the door on the Estate's long-running argument that the "later, more emotional" Holmes of the final stories remained protected — that copyright has now expired too.

## What is RESTRICTED (the modern design / trademark / publicity)

- **The "SHERLOCK HOLMES" trademark.** The Conan Doyle Estate and other parties hold registered trademarks for Holmes-related names in various classes (entertainment, merchandise). Copyright expiry does NOT erase trademark. You can write Holmes stories; you cannot necessarily run a merch *brand* called "Sherlock Holmes" without trademark exposure.
- **Specific modern designs.** The BBC *Sherlock* (Benedict Cumberbatch) look, Robert Downey Jr.'s film Holmes, the CBS *Elementary* design, and the *Enola Holmes* Netflix designs are each their own copyright. Do not copy them.
- **The deerstalker-and-pipe image is itself PD** — it derives from Sidney Paget's 1890s *Strand Magazine* illustrations and William Gillette's stage portrayal (the curved calabash pipe was Gillette's invention), all long expired. Drawing a generic deerstalkered detective is fine.

## Risk level and why

**Caution.** Not because of copyright — that's clean — but because the Conan Doyle Estate is one of the most aggressive enforcers in the PD world. It sued the editor Leslie Klinger (and lost: *Klinger v. Conan Doyle Estate*, 2014) and sued Netflix over *Enola Holmes* in 2020. Even meritless suits cost money to defend. Treat Holmes as usable but keep branding generic and avoid anything that implies Estate authorisation.

## Safe-use guidance

- **Do:** Write original Holmes stories, podcasts, comics, and illustrations. Depict the Victorian deerstalker/pipe/Inverness-cape look (Paget/Gillette derived). Reference 221B Baker Street, Watson, Moriarty, Mrs Hudson, the Baker Street Irregulars — all PD.
- **Do:** For a *branded product line*, prefer an original Z-named consulting-detective character to sidestep trademark entirely.
- **Don't:** Copy any modern screen/film design. Don't register or market a product under a "Sherlock Holmes" trademark in a contested class. Don't imply Estate or studio endorsement. Don't reproduce a specific living actor's likeness (right of publicity).

## Creative opportunities

### Design / POD
Deerstalker silhouette + magnifying glass + fog typography poster; "consulting detective" minimalist line art; deduction-themed quote tees (quotes from PD stories). Keep titling generic ("The Consulting Detective") rather than trademarked branding.

### Story / writing
Pastiche mysteries, gender-swapped or far-future reframes, "lost case" short fiction, an AI-narrated deduction podcast. All canon characters and the 60 stories are fair game.

### Other applications
AI-writing prompt packs for "deduce the culprit" interactive fiction; noir poster series; a Z-named original detective brand that *evokes* Holmes without using the name.

# Aztec & Maya (Mesoamerican) Design

> The bold, blocky geometry of Mesoamerican civilizations — stepped frets, glyph cartouches, feathered serpents, and calendar roundels — powerful and instantly recognizable, and requiring real cultural care.

## Defining Features
A strongly **geometric, modular, high-contrast** visual language. Signature is the **xicalcoliuhqui** — the stepped-fret (a Greek-key cousin built from stacked steps and a hooked spiral) — run as bands and friezes. Figural elements (serpents, jaguars, eagles, deities) are stylized into bold blocky silhouettes packed with internal geometric detail. Maya adds dense glyph cartouches; Aztec adds the famous circular calendar/"sun stone" composition. Everything reads flat, frontal, and emphatic.

## Visual Vocabulary
- **Stepped fret (xicalcoliuhqui)** — the defining stepped-and-hooked band (safest, most generic motif).
- **Glyph cartouche** — boxed Maya logograms (decorative use only; not a real writing system to copy verbatim).
- **Feathered serpent** — Quetzalcoatl/Kukulcan motif (note: religious significance — use with care).
- **Calendar roundel** — concentric ringed composition.
- **Stylized animal silhouettes** — jaguar, eagle, serpent in blocky geometric form.

## Shapes
Right angles, steps, terraces, hooked spirals, concentric circles, bold block silhouettes, dense internal geometry. Modular, grid-based, high-contrast.

## Textures
Carved stone relief, painted stucco, woven and embroidered textile, painted ceramic, codex bark-paper painting. Implies flat bold color blocks and heavy outline; weathered carved stone for an "ancient" read.

## Palette(s)
Earth-and-jewel — terracotta/oxblood red, turquoise, ochre/gold, deep black, jade green, cream. High-contrast black-on-bright is common in textile and graphic use.

## Modern Remix
- **Safest path — geometric only:** step-fret border bands, monochrome blackwork repeats, turquoise-and-terracotta geometric all-over prints.
- **POD:** bold step-fret tees, geometric tote/blanket patterns.
- **AI prompt (safe):** *"bold Mesoamerican stepped-fret geometric border, black on terracotta, modular repeat, no deity figures, no codex pages."*

## Legal & Risk
**This entry carries the section's highest cultural-sensitivity flag.** The ancient motifs are not copyrightable, but this is *living Indigenous heritage* with sacred religious content. Steer toward the abstract **geometric step-fret** (least sacred, most generic). Avoid: deity depictions, sacrifice imagery, specific codex pages, and the "generic exotic tribal" flattening that erases distinct cultures. Do not reproduce protected museum photographs of artifacts. When uncertain, default to abstract geometry or choose another system.

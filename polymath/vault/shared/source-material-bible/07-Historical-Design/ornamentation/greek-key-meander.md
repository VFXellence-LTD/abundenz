# Greek Key / Meander

> The unbroken right-angled fret — a single line that turns back on itself forever — the most recognizable border in Western design, named for the winding Maeander River of Anatolia.

## Defining Features
A continuous band built from a single line that makes a series of right-angled turns, folding into interlocking square or rectangular spirals. The pure meander is one unbroken path (symbolizing eternity and unity); variants stack rows, double the line, or break it into discrete repeating "keys." Strictly rectilinear — no curves — which gives it a crisp, architectural, mechanical rhythm.

## Visual Vocabulary
- **Single meander** — one continuous fret line.
- **Double / triple meander** — parallel interlocking lines.
- **Running key border** — discrete repeating square-spiral units.
- **Meander corner** — the L-turn that lets the band frame a rectangle.
- **Wave meander** — a curvilinear cousin where the turns are rounded (the "running dog" / Vitruvian scroll lives nearby).

## Shapes
Pure orthogonal geometry: squares, right angles, nested square spirals, parallel lines at fixed pitch. Everything is on a grid. The negative space is as deliberate as the positive line.

## Textures
Painted pottery (black/red figure ware), mosaic tesserae, carved stone string-courses, woven textile borders, inlaid floors. In flat design: clean vector line, equal stroke weight; in mosaic mode, the line is built from little square tiles.

## Palette(s)
Classic Greek black-on-terracotta or terracotta-on-black; also marble white with black/gold key; navy-and-gold; or any high-contrast two-color. The motif is fundamentally graphic and reads in a single color.

## Modern Remix
- **POD:** Greek-key border tees, hems, and tote trims; seamless key as all-over print.
- **Brand:** premium "classical" framing for logos, hangtags, and packaging trim.
- **Motion / video:** animated continuous-line key as a transition or lower-third.
- **AI prompt:** *"seamless continuous Greek key meander border, single line, black on terracotta, vector, no curves."*

## Legal & Risk
The fret itself is ancient and free. The only caution: certain luxury fashion houses use a Greek-key pattern as a signature trade dress, paired with their logo and exact colorway. Avoid cloning *that specific lockup*; the generic key is everyone's.

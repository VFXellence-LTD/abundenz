# Egg-and-Dart (Egg-and-Tongue)

> The classic Ionic running molding: a row of rounded "eggs" each cradled in a cup, alternating with downward-pointing "darts." A quiet, rhythmic architectural border that signals classical refinement.

## Defining Features
A horizontal molding (the ovolo profile) carved with an alternating rhythm: a convex egg-shape enclosed in a shell/cup, then a slim arrowhead or tongue between each pair of eggs. The repeat is even and metronomic. It is a *trim* motif — it frames and edges rather than fills — and is often paired with bead-and-reel below it on classical entablatures.

## Visual Vocabulary
- **Egg + shell** — the convex oval in its cup.
- **Dart / tongue / anchor** — the pointed element between eggs.
- **Bead-and-reel** — companion molding (alternating beads and spools), often runs alongside.
- **Running border** — the band itself, used to edge panels and frames.

## Shapes
Alternating convex ovals and slim triangular darts; strict horizontal repeat; symmetrical units. Reads as a beaded rhythm along an edge.

## Textures
Carved stone/plaster relief, cast plaster cornice, carved wood picture-frame, embossed metal. Strong light-and-shadow modeling. In flat design, render with a soft inner shadow to imply relief, or as crisp two-tone silhouette.

## Palette(s)
Architectural neutrals (white, cream, grey, gilt). Gold-on-color for luxe frames; single-color for graphic borders.

## Modern Remix

# The Inventor

> One-line essence: the problem-solver who builds what has never existed.

## Description
The Inventor is the *technologist* facet of the making impulse — the one who looks at a problem and answers it with a machine, a device, a clever mechanism. Daedalus, Archimedes, the mad scientist, the gadgeteer, the garage tinkerer, the engineer-founder — all run this pattern. Where the [[creator]] expresses a vision and the [[craftsman]] perfects a traditional form, the Inventor *solves and builds*: the drama is the problem, the breakthrough, and the consequences of unleashing something new.

The Inventor pattern carries an optimism about progress and a built-in danger: the invention that escapes its maker's control. This is the engine behind both the heroic-inventor story (cleverness saves the day) and the cautionary one (the creation turns monstrous). The Inventor sits between the [[creator]]'s art and the [[magician]]'s transformation — grounded in mechanism and logic rather than aesthetics or the numinous.

In branding, the Inventor is the innovation-and-ingenuity voice: tech and gadgets, engineering, "build the future," problem-solving products, STEM, maker/DIY, and steampunk aesthetics. It promises cleverness and the next breakthrough.

## Core values
- Ingenuity — the elegant solution.
- Curiosity and relentless experimentation.
- Progress and the betterment of life through technology.
- Function and how-things-work.
- Persistence through failed prototypes.

## Motivations
- **Deep want:** to solve the unsolved and build something that changes how things work.
- **Deep fear:** that their creation fails, is useless, or causes harm they didn't foresee.

## Shadow
The shadow Inventor is the **mad scientist** or the **reckless tinkerer** — building because they *can*, not because they *should*, blind to consequences, willing to risk others to prove a point. The breakthrough becomes a catastrophe.

# Achilles

> The greatest warrior of the Trojan War: near-invincible but for a single heel — and undone less by that flaw than by his own towering rage.

## Who / What
Achilles is the central hero of Homer's *Iliad*, the mightiest Greek warrior at Troy. Dipped as an infant in the river Styx by his mother Thetis, he was made invulnerable everywhere except the heel by which she held him. The *Iliad* is, at its core, about his rage — his withdrawal from battle over a slight, the death of his beloved companion Patroclus, and his terrible vengeance on the Trojan prince Hector. He knew he was fated to die young at Troy and chose glory over a long, quiet life. He is finally killed by an arrow to the heel.

## Archetype
The Warrior / Doomed Hero / Rager / Near-Invincible. The supreme fighter with a single fatal weakness, whose greatest enemy is his own pride and fury. The origin of the "Achilles heel" — the one vulnerability in an otherwise invincible thing.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A magnificent, armored warrior in his prime; the symbolically vulnerable heel; the elaborate shield forged by Hephaestus; the duel with and dragging of Hector; the Greek war camp on the beach at Troy; the funeral pyre. Bronze-age armor, plumes, and spears.

## Symbols
- **The heel** — the single point of vulnerability; "Achilles heel."
- **Spear and shield** — the warrior; his shield is a famous ekphrasis.
- **Armor** — the divine armor lost with Patroclus and reforged.
- **The Myrmidons** — his fierce warrior band.

## Themes
Glory and its price, rage and its consequences, mortality and the choice of a short brilliant life, the fatal flaw, friendship and grief, fate accepted, the warrior's code.

## Reusable Concepts
The "Achilles heel" — the one weakness in an otherwise unbeatable thing (an endlessly reusable concept). The hero whose true enemy is his own anger. The choice between long life and lasting glory. Grief turned to terrible vengeance. The invincible undone by the smallest thing.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
The dipping in the Styx and the held heel. The wrath of Achilles and his withdrawal from battle (the *Iliad*'s opening). The death of Patroclus in Achilles's armor. The vengeful slaying and dragging of Hector. His own death by an arrow to the heel.

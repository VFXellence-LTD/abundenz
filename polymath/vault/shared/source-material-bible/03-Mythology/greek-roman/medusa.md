# Medusa

> The Gorgon whose gaze turns flesh to stone: serpents for hair, sorrow behind the snarl — monster, victim, and protective talisman all at once.

## Who / What
Medusa is the most famous of the three Gorgons, a creature whose gaze petrifies anyone who looks at her, and whose hair is a writhing mass of living snakes. In the most retold version (Ovid's), she was once a beautiful priestess of Athena, violated in the goddess's temple by Poseidon, and then punished — transformed into a monster — by Athena. She is slain by Perseus, who uses a mirror-shield to avoid her gaze; her severed head retains its power and becomes a weapon and, later, a protective emblem (the *gorgoneion*) placed on shields and doorways to ward off evil.

## Archetype
The Monster / Cursed Victim / Protective Talisman / Femme Monstrueuse. A uniquely layered figure: terrifying predator, tragic victim, and apotropaic guardian. Modern readings reclaim her as a symbol of female rage and survival.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A woman with living serpents for hair; intense, stone-turning eyes; in the archaic style, a grotesque snarling face with tusks and wings; in the classical style, tragic beauty with a sorrowful or defiant expression; the severed head held aloft or rendered as a frontal emblem (the gorgoneion).

## Symbols
- **Snake hair** — her defining, instantly readable feature.
- **The petrifying gaze** — turning the viewer to stone.
- **The gorgoneion** — her frontal-face head used as a protective ward.
- **Severed head** — Perseus's trophy and weapon.
- **Wings and claws** — archaic monstrous attributes.

## Themes
The dangerous gaze, monstrousness and victimhood intertwined, transformation as punishment, protection through fear (the apotropaic head), female rage and reclamation, beauty and horror.

## Reusable Concepts
The monster who is also a victim (a powerful reframing). The thing you cannot look at directly. The severed head that keeps its power. The protective emblem made from a fearsome face. The gaze as weapon. Reclamation: the wronged woman whose monstrousness is her armor.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
Her transformation from priestess to monster (the Ovid version). Her slaying by Perseus via the mirror-shield. The birth of Pegasus and Chrysaor from her blood / severed neck. Her head set into Athena's aegis. The use of her image as a protective ward.

# Athena (Minerva)

> The grey-eyed goddess of wisdom and strategic war: helmeted, owl on her shoulder, born fully armored from the head of Zeus.

## Who / What
Athena is the goddess of wisdom, strategic warfare, crafts, and civilization, sprung fully grown and armored from the head of Zeus. The Romans called her Minerva. She is the patron of Athens (she won the city with the gift of the olive tree), the favored helper of heroes like Perseus and Odysseus, and the embodiment of disciplined, intelligent power as opposed to Ares's blind bloodlust.

## Archetype
The Wise Warrior / Strategist / Patron. The mind that wins where brute force fails. The mentor-goddess who aids the worthy hero. A clean, dignified study in intelligence-as-power.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
An armored woman in a long robe with a crested Corinthian helmet; an owl perched nearby; an olive branch; a spear and round shield (the *aegis*) sometimes bearing the Gorgon's head; calm, level gaze. The Parthenon and classical statuary as setting.

## Symbols
- **Owl** — wisdom and watchfulness; her sacred bird.
- **Olive tree / branch** — her gift to Athens; peace and prosperity.
- **Aegis** — her protective goatskin shield, often with the gorgoneion.
- **Helmet and spear** — strategic warfare.

## Themes
Wisdom over force, strategy and craft, justice and civilization, the patronage of heroes, disciplined power, the dignity of the unmarried independent woman.

## Reusable Concepts
The strategist who out-thinks stronger opponents. The wise patron who tests and rewards merit. Intelligence vs. brute force (Athena vs. Ares). The gift that wins a contest (olive vs. salt spring). The mentor who gives the hero exactly the tool they need (the mirror-shield for Perseus).

## Story / Narrative Hooks
Her birth from Zeus's head. The contest with Poseidon for Athens. Her aid to Perseus against Medusa and to Odysseus throughout his journey. The weaving contest with Arachne. Her role in the Trojan War.

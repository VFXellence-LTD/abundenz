# Dionysus (Bacchus)

> The god of wine and ecstasy: ivy-crowned, thyrsus in hand, leading the wild dance between joy and madness, civilization and the untamed.

## Who / What
Dionysus is the god of wine, fertility, ritual madness, ecstasy, and theatre. The Romans called him Bacchus. Twice-born (rescued from his dying mortal mother and sewn into Zeus's thigh), he is the only Olympian with a mortal parent, which makes him the great boundary-crosser — between divine and human, sober and drunk, order and chaos. His followers, the maenads, dance themselves into frenzy; his gift is liberation, and his danger is madness.

## Archetype
The Reveler / Liberator / God of Ecstasy / Dual-Natured Outsider. The force that loosens inhibition — joyous in measure, destructive in excess. The patron of theatre and the original "wild card."

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A youthful (or sometimes mature, bearded) figure crowned with ivy and grape leaves; a thyrsus (a pinecone-tipped fennel staff); a wine cup or drinking horn; clusters of grapes and trailing vines; leopards or panthers (his mount); dancing maenads; theatrical comedy and tragedy masks.

## Symbols
- **Grapevine and clusters** — wine and fertility.
- **Thyrsus** — his ivy-and-pinecone staff.
- **Ivy crown** — his wreath.
- **Wine cup / drinking horn** — revelry.
- **Leopard / panther** — his wild mount.
- **Theatrical masks** — patron of drama.

## Themes
Wine and intoxication, ecstasy and liberation, the thin line between joy and madness, theatre and performance, fertility and rebirth, duality and boundary-crossing, the civilizing and the savage at once.

## Reusable Concepts
The liberator who frees you from yourself — for better and worse. The twice-born who belongs nowhere and everywhere. Ecstasy as both gift and curse. The patron of masks and performance. The outsider god who must be welcomed or he brings ruin.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
His twice-born origin (Zeus's thigh). The destruction of King Pentheus, who refused his worship (the Bacchae). The transformation of pirates into dolphins. The gift of the golden touch to Midas. His rescue and marriage of Ariadne.

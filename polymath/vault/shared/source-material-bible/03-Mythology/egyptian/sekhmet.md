# Sekhmet

> The lioness-headed goddess of war and destruction whose breath formed the desert and whose rage nearly wiped out humanity — and who is, paradoxically, also a goddess of healing.

## Who / What
Sekhmet ("the powerful one") is the ancient Egyptian goddess of war, destruction, divine wrath, and — paradoxically — healing. She is depicted as a woman with the head of a lioness, crowned with the solar disk and uraeus cobra, marking her as a fierce "eye of Ra." In her most famous myth, Ra sends her to punish rebellious humanity, but she becomes so drunk on slaughter that she threatens to destroy the entire human race; the gods trick her into stopping by flooding the fields with beer dyed red to look like blood — she drinks it, falls into a stupor, and humanity is saved. She is the embodiment of overwhelming, fierce power, yet she was also invoked to ward off plague and was patron of physicians. She represents the dual nature of fierce power: the same force that destroys can heal and protect.

## Archetype
The War Goddess / Fierce Lioness / Destroyer-and-Healer / Divine Wrath. The terrifying, overwhelming feminine power that protects as fiercely as it destroys — strength in its most awesome form.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A powerful, regal woman with the head of a lioness; crowned with a solar disk and rearing cobra; sometimes rendered in fierce red; holding an ankh and a papyrus scepter; an aura of overwhelming power. Many ancient Sekhmet statues survive (free to reference). Gold, red, and profile-art style.

- **The sun disk and uraeus** — the "eye of Ra."
- **Red linen / blood** — her bloodlust (and the beer trick).
- **The arrows of pestilence** — plague she sends and wards off.
- **The ankh** — her healing aspect.

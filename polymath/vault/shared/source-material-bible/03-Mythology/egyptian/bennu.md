# Bennu (the Egyptian Phoenix)

> The sacred heron of the first sunrise: the self-renewing solar bird whose cry began creation and whose form gave the world the original phoenix.

## Who / What
The Bennu is the ancient Egyptian sacred bird associated with the sun, creation, rebirth, and the Nile flood — and the likely original inspiration for the Greek phoenix. Unlike the eagle-like Greek phoenix, the Bennu is depicted as a tall grey or golden **heron** with a long beak and a two-feathered crest. In Egyptian myth, the Bennu was the *ba* (soul) of the sun god Ra, and in some accounts it was the first living thing to appear at creation, alighting on the primordial mound (the *benben* stone) and giving the cry that began time. It is self-renewing and self-created, linked to the daily rebirth of the sun and the annual rebirth of the land through the Nile flood. It embodies the first dawn, renewal, and the cycle of creation.

## Archetype
The Rebirth-Bird / Creation Symbol / Solar Bird / Self-Renewing. The original phoenix — but in heron form, tied to the very first sunrise and the soul of the sun.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A tall, elegant grey or golden heron with a long beak and a distinctive two-long-feather crest; perched on the sacred benben stone or pillar; haloed in the radiant light of the first sunrise; sometimes wearing the atef crown of Osiris; the Nile and the dawn behind it. (The heron form distinguishes it from the eagle-like Greek phoenix.)

## Symbols
- **The heron** — its distinctive form (vs. the Greek eagle-phoenix).
- **The sun / first dawn** — creation and renewal.
- **The benben stone** — the primordial mound where it first landed.
- **The two-feathered crest** — its identifying mark.
- **The flame / radiance** — its solar nature.

## Themes
Rebirth and renewal, the sun and creation, the first dawn and new beginnings, the soul (it is Ra's ba), the cycle of life, resurrection, the primordial.

# Sphinx (Egyptian)

> The recumbent lion with a pharaoh's face: a colossal, serene guardian that has watched over the Egyptian desert for thousands of years — the very image of eternal, monumental power.

## Who / What
The Egyptian sphinx is a creature with the body of a lion and the head of a human (usually a pharaoh) or sometimes a ram or falcon. Unlike the riddling, winged, female Greek Sphinx, the Egyptian sphinx is typically **male, wingless, recumbent (lying down), and benevolent** — a guardian figure representing royal power and the protection of sacred sites. The most famous is the Great Sphinx of Giza, a single colossal limestone statue (with a pharaoh's face and a lion's body) that has guarded the pyramids for over 4,500 years. Sphinxes lined the avenues to temples as protective sentinels. The Egyptian sphinx embodies serene, monumental, eternal guardianship and the fusion of human intelligence with leonine strength.

## Archetype
The Guardian / Monument / Royal Protector / Eternal Watcher. The serene, immovable sentinel of sacred and royal places — power and protection rendered monumental.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A recumbent (lying) lion with the head of a pharaoh wearing the striped *nemes* headdress; monumental scale; a serene, forward-facing expression; guarding a temple avenue or tomb; the desert and pyramids in the background. Sandstone and gold tones. (Distinct from the Greek winged female sphinx.)

## Symbols
- **Lion body with pharaoh head** — strength joined to royal intelligence.
- **The nemes headdress** — royalty.
- **The recumbent (lying) pose** — serene, eternal watchfulness.
- **The monumental statue** — permanence and scale.
- **The desert guardian** — protection of sacred sites.

## Themes
Guardianship and protection, royal power, the eternal watch, mystery and ancient secrets, the monumental and the permanent, kingship, serenity.

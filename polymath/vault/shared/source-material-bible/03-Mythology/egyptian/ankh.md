# Ankh (the Key of Life)

> The looped cross the gods hold to the lips of kings — the ancient Egyptian symbol of life itself, eternity, and the divine breath that animates all things.

## Who / What
The ankh is one of the most recognizable symbols of ancient Egypt: a cross topped with a teardrop-shaped loop. It is the hieroglyph for "life" and, by extension, eternal life. In Egyptian art, the gods are shown holding the ankh by its loop and offering it to the nose of a pharaoh — giving the literal "breath of life." It appears constantly in tombs, temples, and amulets as a promise of life and immortality. Sometimes called the "key of life" or "crux ansata" (cross with a handle), it has been adopted in modern times by spiritual movements and is especially iconic in goth and alternative subcultures. It is a clean, elegant, life-affirming symbol.

## Archetype
The Symbol of Life / Key of Eternity / Divine Gift. The pure visual shorthand for life, vitality, and the eternal.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A cross with a teardrop/oval loop at the top; held by the loop in a god's hand; offered to the nose of a pharaoh (the breath of life); rendered in gold, lapis, or carved stone; clean and symmetrical. Highly adaptable as jewelry, tattoo, and minimalist design.

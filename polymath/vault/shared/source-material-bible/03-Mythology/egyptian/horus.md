# Horus (Heru)

> The falcon-headed sky god and rightful king of Egypt, who avenged his murdered father and won the throne from his usurping uncle — the divine pattern of every pharaoh.

## Who / What
Horus is the ancient Egyptian god of the sky, kingship, and protection, depicted as a falcon or a falcon-headed man. The son of Osiris and Isis, he was hidden and raised in secret after Set murdered his father, then grew up to challenge Set in a long series of contests and battles to reclaim the throne of Egypt. He ultimately won, becoming the model of legitimate kingship — every living pharaoh was considered the living Horus. In the conflict he lost an eye, which was healed (becoming the Eye of Horus, a symbol of protection and wholeness). His eyes are the sun and the moon; his outstretched falcon wings shelter the king. He embodies rightful rule, vengeance for wrong, protection, and victory.

## Archetype
The Rightful Heir / Avenger / Sky-King / Falcon Lord. The legitimate successor who reclaims what was stolen — the divine template of kingship and the avenging son.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A falcon-headed man wearing the double crown (the *pschent*) of Upper and Lower Egypt; or a full falcon with outstretched protective wings; the Eye of Horus (the *wedjat*); the sky as his domain; perched protectively behind the pharaoh's head. Gold, blue, and profile-art style.

## Symbols
- **The falcon head** — the sky god.
- **The Eye of Horus (wedjat)** — protection, healing, wholeness (see [[eye-of-horus]]).
- **The double crown** — kingship over all Egypt.
- **Outstretched falcon wings** — protection of the king.
- **The sun and moon** — his two eyes.

## Themes
Kingship and legitimacy, the sky and the falcon's flight, vengeance for a wronged father, protection, divine right, victory after long struggle, the rightful heir reclaiming his own.

## Reusable Concepts
The rightful heir who reclaims a stolen throne (a classic, powerful narrative). The avenging son. "Claim your throne" (empowerment, ambition). The protective eye/wing (shelter, watchfulness — see the Eye). The falcon's soaring, far-seeing vantage. Victory through perseverance against a usurper.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
His secret upbringing in the marshes (protected by Isis). His long contest with Set for the throne (including the famous "Contendings of Horus and Set"). The loss and healing of his eye. His coronation as king and the establishment of the pharaonic ideal.

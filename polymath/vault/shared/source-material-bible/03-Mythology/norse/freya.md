# Freya (Freyja)

> The goddess of love, beauty, war, and death, who rides a chariot drawn by two great cats and weeps tears of red gold — proof that the feminine divine can be both sensual and lethal.

## Who / What
Freya is the most prominent goddess of the Norse pantheon — goddess of love, beauty, sex, fertility, war, death, gold, and magic (*seiðr*). She is no gentle love-goddess: she rides into battle and claims half of all warriors slain in combat for her hall Fólkvangr (Odin takes the other half to Valhalla). She owns the dazzling necklace Brísingamen, a falcon-feather cloak that lets the wearer fly, and a chariot pulled by two large cats. When her husband Óðr wanders away, she weeps tears of red gold. She embodies the union of opposites: love and war, beauty and death, desire and sorcery.

## Archetype
The Love-and-War Goddess / Sorceress / Chooser of the Slain / Dual-Natured Feminine. A far more complex figure than a simple beauty-goddess — she holds love and death in the same hands.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A beautiful woman in a chariot drawn by two large cats (a wonderfully distinctive motif); a radiant golden necklace (Brísingamen); a falcon-feather cloak; weeping tears of gold; a boar companion (Hildisvíni); equally at home on the battlefield and in the bedchamber. Norse gold, fur, and a fierce-sensual blend.

## Symbols
- **Brísingamen** — her famous golden necklace.
- **The cat-drawn chariot** — uniquely hers; great for differentiation and whimsy.
- **The falcon cloak** — flight and transformation.
- **Tears of red gold** — her grief for her wandering husband.
- **The boar** — her battle-mount.

## Themes
Love and desire, beauty and war intertwined, death and the claiming of the slain, magic and sorcery, fertility and gold, grief and longing, the dual-natured feminine.

## Reusable Concepts
Love and war in one figure (a powerful, non-stereotyped goddess). The cat-drawn chariot (charming and distinctive). Tears of gold (a luxury/value metaphor). The sorceress with real power. Half the slain go to her — a striking afterlife idea. Beauty that is also lethal.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
The winning of Brísingamen. Her loan of the falcon cloak to other gods. Her grief and golden tears for Óðr. Her claiming of half the battle-slain. The giants' repeated demands for her hand (and the gods' refusals).

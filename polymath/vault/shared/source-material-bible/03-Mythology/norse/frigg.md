# Frigg

> The queen of the gods and Odin's wife, who knows the fate of all things but speaks of none — the all-knowing mother whose foreknowledge could not save her own beloved son.

## Who / What
Frigg is the queen of the Norse gods, wife of Odin, and goddess of marriage, motherhood, the household, and foresight. She alone shares Odin's gift of knowing the fates of all beings, yet she keeps her knowledge silent. She is the mother of the beloved Baldr, and her central, tragic myth is her desperate attempt to save him: foreseeing his death, she extracts an oath from every thing in the world not to harm him — overlooking only the mistletoe, which becomes the instrument of his death. Friday ("Frigg's day") is named for her. She is sometimes confused with Freya in the sources, but is a distinct figure: the dignified matriarch and silent knower of fate.

## Archetype
The Queen / All-Knowing Mother / Matriarch / Keeper of Fate's Secrets. The wise, dignified mother who knows what is coming and grieves it in advance — foresight as a burden, not a power.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A dignified, regal queen; a spinning distaff (she spins the clouds, and spinning is her craft); a ring of household keys (the matron's authority); a serene but knowing, faintly sorrowful expression; her marshy hall Fensalir; the protective mother shielding her son.

## Symbols
- **The distaff / spinning wheel** — her craft; fate spun like thread.
- **The household keys** — the matron's domestic authority.
- **The protective oath** — her attempt to save Baldr.
- **The falcon cloak** — which she lends out (shared with Freya lore).
- **Fensalir** — her hall in the marshes.

## Themes
Motherhood and protection, foreknowledge and its burden, marriage and the household, prophecy that cannot avert fate, grief foreseen, the silent knower, the matriarch's quiet authority.

## Reusable Concepts
"Knows the future but says nothing" — a poignant tragic-prophet theme. The mother who tries to protect against fate (and the one thing she overlooks). Foresight as sorrow. The dignified matriarch. Spinning fate (the distaff). The keeper of the home and its keys.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
Her sharing of Odin's foresight and her silence. The extraction of the oath from all things to protect Baldr, and the overlooked mistletoe. Her grief at Baldr's death. Her quiet, central role as queen and matriarch.

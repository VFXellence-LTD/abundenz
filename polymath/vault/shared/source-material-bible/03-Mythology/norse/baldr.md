# Baldr (Balder)

> The most beloved and radiant of the gods, made invulnerable to everything in the world — except one thing too small to bother asking. His death by that single overlooked weakness sets the doom of the gods in motion.

## Who / What
Baldr is the Norse god of light, purity, beauty, and innocence — the most beloved of all the gods, so radiant that light shines from him. When he is troubled by dreams of his own death, his mother Frigg extracts an oath from every thing in the world — fire, water, metal, stone, beasts, plants, sickness — never to harm him. She overlooks only the mistletoe, thinking it too young and small to ask. The gods amuse themselves throwing weapons at the now-invulnerable Baldr, until the trickster Loki, having learned of the one exception, fashions a dart of mistletoe and guides the hand of the blind god Höðr to throw it. Baldr dies, and his death — and the failure to ransom him back from Hel — is the beginning of Ragnarök. His funeral, on a burning ship pushed out to sea, is one of myth's great images.

## Archetype
The Beloved / Radiant Innocent / Doomed Good / Sacrificed Light. The pure, cherished figure whose loss devastates the world — and a perfect study in the single overlooked vulnerability.

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
A radiant, beautiful young god haloed in light; the small sprig of mistletoe (the unlikely killer); the burning funeral ship drifting out to sea at dusk; all of nature — gods, beasts, stones, trees — weeping for him. Light against gathering dark.

## Symbols
- **Light / radiance** — his defining quality.
- **The mistletoe** — the single overlooked weakness; the killer.
- **The funeral ship** — his iconic sea-burial pyre.
- **The weeping world** — universal grief.
- **The white flower** — purity.

## Themes
Light and purity, innocence and belovedness, the single overlooked weakness, prophecy and inevitable doom, grief and mourning, the loss that breaks the world, the fragility of the good.

## Reusable Concepts
"Everything was sworn except the one small thing" — a powerful metaphor for the overlooked vulnerability (security, planning, the detail you skip). The beloved whose death changes everything. The funeral ship (a striking memorial image). Universal grief (all the world weeps). Light extinguished as the start of the end.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
His ominous dreams. Frigg's oath from all things, and the overlooked mistletoe. The deadly game of throwing weapons at him. Loki's mistletoe dart and the blind Höðr. The failed attempt to ransom him from Hel (Loki, disguised, refuses to weep). The burning funeral ship.

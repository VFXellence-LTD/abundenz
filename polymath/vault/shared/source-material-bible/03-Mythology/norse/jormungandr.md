# Jörmungandr (the World Serpent)

> The serpent so vast it wraps around the entire world and grips its own tail in its jaws — and when it lets go, the world ends.

## Who / What
Jörmungandr, the Midgard Serpent or World Serpent, is a colossal sea-serpent and a child of Loki, cast by Odin into the great ocean that encircles the mortal world. There it grew so immense that it wraps all the way around the earth and bites its own tail — an image strikingly parallel to the pan-cultural **ouroboros** (the serpent eating its tail, a symbol of eternity, cycles, and continuity). It is the great enemy of Thor: the two are fated to kill each other at Ragnarök, when the serpent finally releases its tail and rises from the sea. Myths tell of Thor's fishing expedition where he nearly hauled the serpent from the deep.

## Archetype
The World-Encircler / Cosmic Serpent / Ouroboros / Fated Enemy. The boundary of the world made flesh, and a vivid emblem of cycles and eternity (when it lets go, time itself ends).

## Visual Motifs (safe to draw)
An enormous sea-serpent so vast it encircles the entire globe; the tail gripped in its own jaws (the ouroboros image); coiled in the ocean depths; rising titanically from the sea to face Thor; scales, water, and immensity. The ouroboros simplification (a single serpent biting its tail in a circle) is a clean, free, infinitely usable symbol.

## Symbols
- **The serpent encircling the world** — the boundary of all things.
- **The tail in the mouth (ouroboros)** — eternity, cycles, continuity, recycling.
- **The ocean deep** — its domain.
- **Coils** — vastness.

## Themes
The encircling boundary, the cosmic cycle, eternity and continuity, the fated enemy, vastness beyond comprehension, the held-together world (and its release/end).

## Reusable Concepts
The ouroboros — one of the most useful symbols in design: eternity, infinity, cycles, recycling, self-renewal, "what goes around comes around," sustainability. The thing that holds the world together (and whose release ends it). The vast, encircling boundary. The fated foe. The serpent as continuity rather than evil.

## Story / Narrative Hooks
Its casting into the ocean by Odin. Thor's fishing trip where he hooks and nearly lands it. The mutual doom of Thor and the serpent at Ragnarök (Thor kills it but dies of its venom). The ouroboros parallel across world cultures.

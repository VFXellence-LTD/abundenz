# Vintage-Label Layouts

## Description
A **vintage-label layout** is the dense, hierarchical typographic arrangement found on late-1800s-to-1940s product packaging, apothecary bottles, and advertising. Multiple type sizes and styles stack within a bordered panel — brand name large and ornate at top, product descriptor in the middle, fine print and "established" date at the bottom — punctuated by ornamental rules and small illustrations. The grammar screams authenticity, craft, and provenance.

## Visual vocabulary
- **Type hierarchy stack:** 4–7 distinct type sizes/styles top to bottom, each line a different "voice" (display serif, condensed sans, script, small caps).
- **Ornamental rules:** decorative dividers and "spencerian" flourishes separating sections.
- **Bordered panel:** the whole composition lives inside a frame (links to [[frames-and-borders]]).
- **Central illustration:** a small engraved emblem (wheat, bottle, gear, animal) — links to [[vintage-engraving-anatomical]].
- **"Fake-Latin" fine print & dosage-style copy:** filler lines that look like ingredient lists or directions.
- **Symmetry:** strong centered axis; everything balances left/right.
- **Aging:** subtle paper texture, ink spread, distress.

## Where it shines
- **Merch & packaging (best):** product labels, candle/soap/coffee packaging, apothecary tins, beer/soda labels (fictional brands).
- **Apparel:** "vintage product" tees — a whole fake-brand label printed on the chest.
- **Posters:** advertising-revival prints, kitchen/bar wall art.
- **Brand:** craft/heritage brand identities (coffee, spirits, grooming, hot sauce).
- **Struggles:** modern-minimal contexts; tiny scale (the dense hierarchy needs room to read).

## Pairs with
- **Categories / subjects:** craft food/beverage, apothecary/grooming, general-store/mercantile, tonic/elixir parody, coffee.
- **Palettes:** cream + black + single spot, sepia/aged, muted heritage. (link section 11)
- **Art movements:** Victorian advertising, sign-painting, woodtype/letterpress, Art Nouveau labels.
- **Other motifs:** wraps a [[banners-and-ribbons]] for the brand name, centers a [[retro-sunbursts]] burst, frames an engraving from [[vintage-engraving-anatomical]], can sit inside a [[badges-and-crests]].

## Remix hooks
- **Fictional apothecary/tonic brand** — invent "ZESTWELL'S MIRACLE ELIXIR · EST 1894" for parody tees and packaging (use the Z-naming convention for Polymath brands).
- **Modern product, old label:** an "energy drink" or "wifi router" dressed in 1890s apothecary typography = comedic contrast.
- **Type-only** version (no illustration) leans full vintage-advertising.
- Heavy **distress/aging** for a thrift-found feel; or clean it up for premium craft branding.

## AI prompt phrasing
- **Core phrase:** `vintage product label layout, ornate typographic hierarchy, multiple type styles stacked, decorative rule dividers, bordered panel, central engraved emblem, cream and black, aged paper`
- **Modifiers:** `apothecary bottle label`, `1890s advertising`, `letterpress`, `distressed`, `fictional brand name`, `spencerian flourishes`
- **Negative hints:** suppress `modern sans-serif minimal, photo, 3d, real brand name, copyrighted logo, coca-cola, jack daniels`.

## Legal note
The layout grammar is free; the fill is the risk. Never reproduce a real brand's actual label or packaging trade dress (Coca-Cola, Jack Daniel's, Levi's patch, Tabasco, etc.). Invent fictional brand names and copy. For commercial use, avoid implying real medicinal/alcohol/tobacco efficacy claims (advertising regulation, separate from copyright).

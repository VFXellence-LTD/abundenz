# Rococo Art

> Baroque drama turned light and sweet: pastel skies, frothy drapery, cherubs and roses, and endless asymmetric shell-and-scroll ornament made for pleasure and play.

## Overview
Rococo took the grandeur of Baroque and made it intimate, playful, and decorative — pastel palettes, asymmetric curves, cherubs, flowers, and scenes of flirtation and leisure. It is the definitive "pretty, soft, ornate" historical style and maps perfectly onto modern pastel, romantic, feminine, and "cottagecore"-adjacent product lines. All original works are public domain.

## Defining Features
- Light pastel palettes replacing Baroque's dark drama.
- Asymmetric C- and S-scroll ornament (rocaille).
- Shell, foliage, and floral decorative motifs.
- Themes of love, leisure, music, and pleasure.
- Frothy, airy drapery and cloud-filled skies.

## Visual Vocabulary
Soft pastel washes, gilded curlicues, cherubs floating in clouds, garlands of roses, asymmetric ornament curling off frames and panels, delicate brushwork, dreamy diffuse light.

## Shapes
The C-scroll and S-scroll, the rocaille shell, the garland loop, the asymmetric cartouche, the soft cloud, the gentle figure curve.

## Textures
Powdery pastel, silk and lace, gilded plaster ornament, soft feathered brushwork, porcelain glaze.

## Palette
The "pastel boudoir" family: rose pink, sky blue, mint, cream, lilac, peach, and soft gold. Light, sweet, romantic. See [[08-Color-Palettes/README]].

## Key Motifs
Cherub/putto, roses and floral garlands, the rocaille shell-scroll, ribbons and bows, songbirds, the swing/leisure scene, gilded curlicue frames, pastel clouds.

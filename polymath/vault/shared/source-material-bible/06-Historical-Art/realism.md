# Realism

> Art turns to ordinary life: real laborers, peasants, and everyday scenes rendered honestly, without idealization, granting dignity to the common and the unglamorous.

## Overview
Realism rejected both Neoclassical idealism and Romantic drama in favor of depicting contemporary life truthfully — working people, ordinary settings, the unromanticized everyday. Its grounded honesty and earthy palette suit "authentic," "handmade," "salt-of-the-earth," and documentary-leaning brands. The founding works are public domain.

## Defining Features
- Unidealized subjects: real workers, peasants, ordinary people.
- Contemporary, everyday scenes rather than myth or history.
- Honest observation over flattery or drama.
- Natural, often muted lighting and grounded composition.
- Implicit social awareness and empathy for the common person.

## Visual Vocabulary
Sober, naturalistic rendering, weathered faces and hands, plain clothing and settings, even diffuse daylight, grounded horizontal compositions, earthy restrained color, dignity granted to humble subjects.

## Shapes
Stable grounded forms, the bent working figure, plain rectangular interiors and fields, the solid central subject, natural irregular silhouettes.

## Textures
Matte naturalistic paint, rough cloth, weathered skin, soil and stone, plain wood, unpolished surfaces.

## Palette
The "earthy honest" family: umber, ochre, olive, slate grey, muted blue, off-white, and warm brown. Grounded, natural, sober. See [[08-Color-Palettes/README]].

## Key Motifs
The field worker / laborer, weathered hands, the plain interior, the gleaner/gatherer, bread and simple meals, the dignified ordinary portrait, the crowd of common people.

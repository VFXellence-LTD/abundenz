# Romanticism

> Emotion and the sublime over reason: a tiny human figure dwarfed by storm, mountain, and sea — nature as overwhelming, beautiful, and terrifying.

## Overview
Romanticism prized intense emotion, individual experience, and the sublime power of nature over Neoclassical order and reason. Its signature image — a lone figure overwhelmed by a vast, dramatic landscape — is one of the most reproduced and emotionally resonant compositions in Western art, fully public domain, and perfect for "awe," "wanderlust," and introspective product lines.

## Defining Features
- The sublime: nature as vast, powerful, and awe-inspiring (even terrifying).
- Emotional intensity and subjectivity over objective order.
- The lone individual confronting the immensity of nature.
- Dramatic weather, light, and atmosphere.
- Fascination with ruins, the exotic, the medieval, and the wild.

## Visual Vocabulary
Vast atmospheric landscapes, a small figure for scale, turbulent skies, moonlight and mist, dramatic chiaroscuro of weather, loose expressive brushwork, ruins overtaken by nature, the sea in motion.

## Shapes
The towering mountain triangle, the small lone figure, the sweeping diagonal of storm or wave, the receding atmospheric horizon, the broken ruin silhouette.

## Textures
Atmospheric haze, loose painterly brush, turbulent cloud, misty diffusion, rugged rock, foaming sea.

## Palette
The "sublime" family: stormy grey-blue, sunset gold and rose, misty white, deep forest green, twilight purple, and dramatic dark accents. Atmospheric and emotional. See [[08-Color-Palettes/README]].

## Key Motifs
The lone wanderer before a vast view, stormy/sublime skies, moonlit sea, mountain peak, shipwreck, ruin in landscape, the wild horse, the misty forest.

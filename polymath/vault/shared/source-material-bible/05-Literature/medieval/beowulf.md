# Beowulf

> The oldest English epic — a hero, three monsters, and the long dark of mortality.

## Public-domain status
*Beowulf* is an Old English heroic poem composed somewhere between roughly 700 and 1000 CE; the original is permanently free. **The translation trap is sharp:** Seamus Heaney's acclaimed 1999 translation — the one most modern readers know — is firmly under copyright, and so is J.R.R. Tolkien's. Use a public-domain translation: **Francis Barton Gummere**, William Morris, or John Lesslie Hall — or your own. See [[traps/translations-and-editions]].

## What it is
The Geatish hero Beowulf comes to the aid of the Danish king Hrothgar, whose mead-hall Heorot is being terrorized by the monster Grendel. Beowulf kills Grendel bare-handed by tearing off its arm; then he kills Grendel's vengeful mother in her underwater lair. Decades later, now an old king of the Geats, Beowulf fights a dragon roused by a stolen cup from its hoard. He slays the dragon but is mortally wounded, and the poem ends with his funeral and a foreboding of his people's doom.

## Themes & symbols
- **Themes:** heroism and glory (*lof*); the inevitability of death even for the greatest; loyalty between lord and thane; courage against the dark; the elegiac sense that all glory fades.
- **Symbols:** the mead-hall (light and fellowship against the dark), the dragon's hoard, the severed arm, the funeral pyre, the ancient sword-hilt.
- **Archetypes:** the hero, the monster, the dragon, the aged king, the loyal retainer (Wiglaf).

## Visual & emotional palette
A torchlit mead-hall against a black fen; a monstrous shape at the door; a dragon coiled on gold; a Viking-style funeral boat or pyre. Grim, heroic, elegiac. Palette: torch-gold against black, blood-red, cold fen-grey, dragon-gold.

## The trap (if any)
No trap in the source; the translation trap is the whole concern — Heaney and Tolkien are copyrighted. Use Gummere or your own. The 2007 film's CGI designs are separate live copyrights.

## Applications / remix hooks
- Poster/AI image: the dragon and its hoard; the mead-hall against the dark; Grendel at the door.
- POD: "I am Beowulf"-style heroic graphics; dark-age dragon art.
- Podcast/story: a retelling of the three monster-fights.

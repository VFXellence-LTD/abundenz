# The Iliad

> The oldest war story in the Western canon — fifty-one days of the Trojan War, told through one man's rage.

## Public-domain status
The *Iliad* is an ancient Greek epic, composed roughly the 8th century BCE and attributed to Homer. There is no copyright on the original text in any jurisdiction — it predates copyright by millennia, so it is permanently free. The single caveat is the **translation trap**: a specific modern English translation (Emily Wilson 2023, Robert Fagles, Richmond Lattimore) is its own copyrighted work. Use a public-domain translation — Alexander Pope's verse, Samuel Butler's prose, George Chapman's Elizabethan version — or produce your own. See [[traps/translations-and-editions]].

## What it is
The poem opens in the tenth year of the Greek siege of Troy. Achilles, the greatest Greek warrior, is insulted by King Agamemnon and withdraws from the fighting in fury. The Greeks suffer; Achilles' beloved companion Patroclus is killed by the Trojan prince Hector; Achilles returns in terrible grief and rage, kills Hector, and desecrates his body — until Hector's father, King Priam, comes to beg for the body, and the two enemies share a moment of shared mortal sorrow. The poem ends with Hector's funeral, before Troy itself falls.

## Themes & symbols
- **Themes:** the destructiveness of rage (*menis*, the poem's first word); the tension between glory (*kleos*) and life; the certainty of death even for heroes; honor and its price; grief; fate.
- **Symbols:** the Shield of Achilles (the whole world depicted on one shield), the heel of Achilles, the walls of Troy, the funeral pyre, the wine-dark sea.
- **Archetypes:** the doomed warrior (Achilles), the tragic defender (Hector), the proud king (Agamemnon), the wise old counselor (Nestor).

## Visual & emotional palette
Bronze and dust. Sun on bronze armor, ships drawn up on a wide beach, a walled city on a plain, single combat between champions, gods watching from cloud. The mood is monumental and grief-soaked — heroism shadowed by mortality. Color palette: bronze, ochre, oxblood, sea-blue, bone-white.

## The trap (if any)
No trap in the original. **Only** the translation trap — always use a public-domain translation or your own text. The 2004 film *Troy* and any video-game designs of these characters are separate live copyrights; draw from ancient/classical visual sources (Greek vase painting, neoclassical art) instead.

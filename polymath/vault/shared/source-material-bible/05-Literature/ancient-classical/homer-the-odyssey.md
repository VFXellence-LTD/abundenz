# The Odyssey

> The original journey-home story — ten years of monsters, gods, and longing between a war and a hearth.

## Public-domain status
The *Odyssey* is an ancient Greek epic (c. 8th century BCE, attributed to Homer). The original is permanently free worldwide; copyright cannot attach to it. The **only** thing to watch is the translation: Emily Wilson's celebrated 2018 verse translation is **copyrighted**, as are Fagles, Lattimore, and other modern versions. Use a public-domain translation — Samuel Butler (prose), Alexander Pope (verse), A. T. Murray (Loeb, older), or William Cowper — or produce your own. See [[traps/translations-and-editions]].

## What it is
After the fall of Troy, the Greek hero Odysseus spends ten years trying to get home to the island of Ithaca, his wife Penelope, and his son Telemachus. Along the way he blinds the Cyclops Polyphemus, resists the Sirens, navigates between the monsters Scylla and Charybdis, loses his entire crew, spends years with the nymph Calypso, and finally returns disguised as a beggar to reclaim his home from the suitors besieging his faithful wife — who has held them off by weaving and unweaving a shroud, and who tests his identity with the secret of their olive-tree bed.

## Themes & symbols
- **Themes:** homecoming (*nostos*), cunning intelligence (*metis*), perseverance, the pull of temptation, loyalty, hospitality, the cost of the long way home.
- **Symbols:** the great bow only Odysseus can string, the Cyclops's single eye, the Sirens' song, Penelope's loom, the immovable olive-tree bed.
- **Archetypes:** the trickster-hero, the faithful wife, the wanderer, the monster, the enchantress (Circe, Calypso).

## Visual & emotional palette
The wine-dark sea, a single-masted ship against open water, a monster's cave, an enchanted island, the hearth waiting at the end. The mood swings from peril and wonder to deep longing to triumphant return. Palette: sea-blue, sail-white, cave-black, hearth-gold, Mediterranean ochre.

## The trap (if any)
No trap in the original epic. The translation trap is the whole story — use a public-domain translation or your own. The 2024–2026 film and game adaptations of the Odyssey are separate live copyrights; build from ancient/classical visual sources.

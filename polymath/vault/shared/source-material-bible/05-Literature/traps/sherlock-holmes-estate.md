# Trap Dossier — Sherlock Holmes & the Conan Doyle Estate

> The whole Holmes canon is public domain now — but the estate spent a decade making people think otherwise.

## The headline (settled law)
Every Sherlock Holmes story Arthur Conan Doyle wrote is now in the US public domain. The four novels and the first stories were free long ago; the last protected stories were the twelve in *The Case-Book of Sherlock Holmes* (the stories first appeared in *The Strand* between 1921 and 1927; the collected book was published June 1927). The final batch entered the US public domain on **1 January 2023** when the 1927 collection's 95-year term expired. In the UK, the entire canon has been public domain since 2000 (Doyle died in 1930; life + 70).

So as of 2026, **all of Sherlock Holmes — text, characters, plots, names, the lot — is free to use.**

## Why it is still flagged as a trap
The Conan Doyle Estate Ltd built a licensing business on the gap years (roughly 2013–2022) when a handful of late stories were still under US copyright. Its tactic was to argue that *character traits* introduced in the still-copyrighted stories (a "warmer", more emotional Holmes; Watson's second marriage; Holmes's interest in dogs) were themselves protected, so any portrayal showing those traits needed a license.

- In *Klinger v. Conan Doyle Estate* (2014), the US Seventh Circuit rejected the estate's theory wholesale for the already-public-domain elements, and Judge Posner separately sanctioned the estate for what looked like a shakedown.
- In 2020 the estate sued Netflix over the *Enola Holmes* film on the "emotional Holmes" theory; the case settled.

The estate is **historically litigious and quick to send demand letters** even where the law is clearly against it. After 1 January 2023 there is no remaining copyright hook at all in the US.

## What is safe
- The name **Sherlock Holmes**, Dr. Watson, Mycroft, Mrs. Hudson, Professor Moriarty, Inspector Lestrade, Irene Adler.
- 221B Baker Street, the deerstalker cap, the calabash pipe (a stage invention, not Doyle's, but free), the magnifying glass, the violin.
- Any plot, line, or scene from the canonical stories.
- Building a Polymath detective brand inspired by Holmes (use a Z-name of your own, e.g. a "Zsherlock"-style original sleuth, to sidestep even the appearance of trading on the name).

## What to avoid
- **Specific modern screen designs.** Benedict Cumberbatch's coat-and-scarf silhouette, Robert Downey Jr.'s look, the *Enola Holmes* visual styling, *House*-as-Holmes — these are recent copyrighted/trademarked treatments. Draw from the *Victorian* Holmes (Sidney Paget's original 1890s *Strand Magazine* illustrations are public domain and are the safe visual reference).
- Implying any **official endorsement** or using estate logos.
- Reproducing a recent annotated or critical edition's added apparatus (see [[traps/translations-and-editions]]).

## The takeaway for the pipeline
Set `risk_level: caution` not because the use is unlawful — it is lawful — but because the estate generates friction. The conservative play: use the public-domain Victorian Holmes and Paget illustrations, avoid any post-1930 screen design, and consider an original Z-named detective for owned branding.

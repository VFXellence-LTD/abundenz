# Merlin

> The prophet-wizard who engineered Arthur's birth, mentored his kingship, and is the template for every robed, bearded sorcerer that followed.

## Origin & Tradition
Merlin (Welsh *Myrddin*) was assembled by Geoffrey of Monmouth (c. 1136) from Welsh prophetic-bard traditions and the figure of Ambrosius. He became the great wizard and counselor of the Arthurian cycle, his story expanded through French romance (the *Vulgate Cycle*). The legendary figure and the medieval source texts are public domain.

## The Story / The Figure
A wizard, prophet, and shape-shifter of half-mortal, half-otherworldly parentage, Merlin orchestrates Uther's union that produces Arthur, raises and counsels the young king, and engineers the sword-in-the-stone test. He is associated with prophecy (the warring red and white dragons beneath Vortigern's tower) and with the building of Stonehenge in some tellings. In many versions he is finally undone by love — entrapped by the enchantress Nimue/Viviane (sometimes conflated with the Lady of the Lake).

## Visual Identity
Free, traditional motifs: a long-bearded man in robes; a staff; the crystal cave; star-and-moon imagery; conjuring or prophesying over dragons. **Caution:** the generic wizard look overlaps with trademarked characters (Gandalf, Dumbledore) — render Merlin in a distinctly historical/Celtic mode to avoid evoking protected designs.

## Symbolism & Themes
Wisdom and foresight; the mentor who shapes the hero; magic in service of destiny; the prophet who can see all but his own downfall. The original "wise old wizard."

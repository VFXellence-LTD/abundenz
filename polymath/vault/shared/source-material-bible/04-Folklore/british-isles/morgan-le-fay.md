# Morgan le Fay

> Arthur's enchantress half-sister — healer and adversary, mistress of Avalon — the ambivalent sorceress at the dark edge of Camelot.

## Origin & Tradition
Morgan le Fay ("Morgan the Fairy") first appears in Geoffrey of Monmouth's *Vita Merlini* (c. 1150) as a benevolent healer of the Isle of Avalon. In later French romance and Malory's *Le Morte d'Arthur* (1485) she becomes a darker antagonist — Arthur's scheming half-sister, a powerful sorceress and shape-shifter — while still being one of the queens who bears the dying king to Avalon. The legendary figure and source texts are public domain.

## The Story / The Figure
A sorceress of great power and ambiguous loyalty, Morgan is variously Arthur's healer and his enemy. She plots against him and Guinevere, steals Excalibur's scabbard, and tests the court's virtue, yet she is also among the mystical women who carry the mortally-wounded Arthur to Avalon for healing. She embodies the otherworldly, fairy-touched magic that surrounds Camelot.

## Visual Identity
Free, traditional motifs: a dark-haired sorceress in flowing robes; the misty isle of Avalon; ravens; spell-casting imagery. **Restricted:** Marvel Comics' trademarked "Morgan le Fay" character and any specific copyrighted film/TV design; do not conflate with Disney's Maleficent.

## Symbolism & Themes
The ambivalent feminine power — both healer and destroyer; the enchantress who unsettles the male order of chivalry; the otherworld (Avalon, the fairy realm) as a source of magic and death-passage.

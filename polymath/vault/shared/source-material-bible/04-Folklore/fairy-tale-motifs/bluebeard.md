# Bluebeard

> A wealthy man with a sinister blue beard gives his new wife every key but forbids one door. Her curiosity reveals the murdered wives who came before.

## Origin & Tradition
A French folk tale type (ATU 312, "the forbidden chamber"), fixed by Charles Perrault in 1697 (*La Barbe bleue*). The Perrault text is public domain. The tale has been linked, probably wrongly, to the historical murderer Gilles de Rais, but its real lineage is folkloric.

## The Story / The Figure
A rich, repellently blue-bearded man marries a young woman and, leaving on a journey, gives her keys to the whole house but forbids one small room. Unable to resist, she unlocks it and finds the bodies of his former wives. The key is magically bloodstained and will not come clean, betraying her disobedience. Bluebeard returns and prepares to kill her; she stalls until her brothers arrive and slay him, leaving her his fortune.

## Visual Identity
Free, traditional motifs: a man with an unnatural blue beard; a single forbidden door and an ornate iron key; a key spotted with blood that cannot be washed away; a chamber of horrors; a gothic mansion. All public domain.

## Symbolism & Themes
Forbidden curiosity and its consequences; marriage as a site of hidden danger; the secret that the powerful conceal; the bloodstain of knowledge that cannot be undone. A foundational gothic-horror template.

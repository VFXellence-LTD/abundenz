# Beauty and the Beast

> A merchant's daughter, a cursed prince in monstrous form, and a love that sees past appearance to break the spell — the great fairy tale of inner beauty.

## Origin & Tradition
The tale type (ATU 425C) descends from ancient "animal bridegroom" stories, including the Greco-Roman *Cupid and Psyche*. The familiar version was written by Gabrielle-Suzanne de Villeneuve (1740) and shortened by Jeanne-Marie Leprince de Beaumont (1756), whose text became standard. Both are public domain. Disney's 1991 animated film and its 2017 live-action remake are copyrighted modern works.

## The Story / The Figure
A merchant, lost and desperate, plucks a rose from an enchanted castle's garden; its monstrous owner, the Beast, demands his life or a daughter in exchange. The youngest, Beauty, goes willingly. She comes to see kindness beneath the Beast's terrifying form. Her love (and her return when he is dying) breaks the curse, restoring him to a prince.

## Visual Identity
Free, traditional motifs: a single rose (often under glass or in an enchanted garden); a grand, gloomy cursed castle; a beast of mixed animal features (leonine, horned, boar-tusked — the folk descriptions vary). **Restricted:** Disney's 1991 specific Beast design, the named living objects, Belle's gold gown, and the iconic rose-under-glass as Disney rendered it.

## Symbolism & Themes
Love that transcends appearance; transformation through being truly seen; redemption of the monstrous; sacrifice and return. The withering enchanted rose is the central time-pressure symbol.

## Legal & Cultural Status
Tale type and Villeneuve/de Beaumont source texts: **public domain.** Restricted: Disney's films and designs. Defers to [[../../01-Legal-Guidelines/pd-source-vs-modern-adaptation|PD source vs modern adaptation]].

# Eight-Pointed Star

> The guiding star — hope, direction, and cosmic order.

## Meaning
The eight-pointed star signifies guidance, hope, divinity, balance, and cosmic order. As the Star of Ishtar (Inanna) it represented the goddess and the planet Venus. As the compass star or "star of the sea," it denotes navigation and the guiding North Star. Eight points often connote regeneration, abundance, and the cardinal + ordinal directions. In Islamic geometric art the eight-pointed form (and the Rub el Hizb) is widespread.

## Origin
Among the oldest stylized stars, the eight-pointed Star of Ishtar appears on Mesopotamian boundary stones and seals over four thousand years ago. The form recurs across Byzantine, Islamic, and European heraldic and decorative traditions. The geometry — two overlaid squares rotated 45°, or a radiant eight-rayed burst — is ancient and PD. Specific named configurations (Rub el Hizb) carry religious weight; specific corporate compass-star logos are protected.

## Visual description
Eight points radiating from a center. Two main constructions: (1) two squares overlaid and rotated 45° forming a star octagon; (2) a radiant burst of eight triangular rays, sometimes alternating long/short to read as a compass (N/E/S/W long, ordinals short). Often drawn with internal facets for a "jeweled" look.

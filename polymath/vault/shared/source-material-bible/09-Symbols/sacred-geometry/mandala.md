# Mandala

> The radial cosmic circle — wholeness, the self, and the center of all things.

## Meaning
A mandala ("circle" in Sanskrit) is a radially symmetric geometric design representing the cosmos, wholeness, the self, and the journey from periphery to sacred center. In Hindu and Buddhist traditions mandalas are spiritual and ritual diagrams; in psychology (Jung) they symbolize the integrated self. As a decorative form, the mandala is now a global motif for balance, meditation, and intricate beauty (henna, coloring books, tattoos).

## Origin
Ancient in Hindu and Buddhist practice (Tibetan sand mandalas, deity mandalas, yantra-mandalas). The general radial form is ancient and PD, and decorative mandalas are universal. **Specific religious mandalas** (a particular Tibetan deity mandala, a sacred yantra) carry living religious weight and should not be copied or trivialized.

## Visual description
Concentric rings of repeating motifs arranged with radial (often 4-, 8-, 12-, 16-fold) symmetry around a central point. Decorative versions use florals, geometry, dots, and lace-like detail; coloring-book versions are clean black line art. Render original patterns — do not trace a specific sacred mandala.

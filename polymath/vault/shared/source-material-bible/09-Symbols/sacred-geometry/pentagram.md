# Pentagram

> The five-pointed star — protection, the elements, and the human microcosm.

## Meaning
The pentagram is a five-pointed star drawn in a single continuous stroke. Across history it has meant protection and good fortune (a warding sign), the five classical elements (the upright form crowns [[../elemental/spirit-aether]] over fire, water, air, earth), the human microcosm (Leonardo's Vitruvian proportions; head + 4 limbs), and mathematical perfection (it embeds the golden ratio). Enclosed in a circle it becomes the **pentacle**. The **inverted** pentagram (two points up) carries a separate, often Satanic/occult, modern reading.

## Origin
Among the oldest symbols, the pentagram appears on Mesopotamian potsherds (c. 3500 BCE), in Pythagorean philosophy (the pentagram of health, hygieia), in Christian symbolism (the five wounds), and in Western esotericism. Ancient and PD. The plain five-pointed star is also the world's most common flag/heraldic star and a near-universal "rating" symbol — entirely free and benign.

## Visual description
A five-pointed star formed by five straight strokes crossing to leave a central pentagon. Upright = single point up. Inverted = two points up. The pentacle adds an enclosing circle. Built on golden-ratio proportions. Render as fine occult line, neon, or bold flat star.

## Emotional tags
Protection, mystery, balance, power, the arcane; inverted flips to transgressive/ominous.

## Design & story applications
- **POD / apparel**: the plain star for patriotic/rating/sports merch (safe); the upright pentacle for witchy/celestial lines (frame with care).
- **Posters**: occult/witchy art, five-elements diagrams, golden-ratio geometry.
- **Story / AI writing**: protective wards, magic circles, the elements; the inverted form as a villain/transgression marker.
- **Branding**: the plain star is everywhere (excellence, rating) — distinctive rendering needed.

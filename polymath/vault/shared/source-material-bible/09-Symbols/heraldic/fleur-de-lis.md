# Fleur-de-lis

> The stylized lily of royalty, purity, and French heritage.

## Meaning
The fleur-de-lis ("lily flower") is a stylized lily long associated with French royalty, nobility, purity, faith (linked to the Virgin Mary and the Holy Trinity via its three petals), and perfection. It is one of the most enduring heraldic charges, signifying refinement, tradition, and aristocratic heritage. It also marks North on many compass roses.

## Origin
Emerged as a French royal emblem in the medieval period (associated with the Capetian kings). The generic charge is fully PD. **Note**: it is a live identity/trademark element for specific modern entities (Scouting organizations, the New Orleans Saints, various hotels and brands) — design a generic version, never imitate a specific protected one. Be aware of the painful historical use of the fleur-de-lis as a branding mark on enslaved people in colonial Louisiana.

## Visual description
A stylized three-petaled lily: a tall central petal flanked by two outward-curving side petals, all bound near the base by a horizontal band, often with small flourishes below. Render in gold-on-royal-blue, ornate damask, French-vintage, or clean modern line.

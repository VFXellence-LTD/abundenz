# Djed Pillar

> The backbone of Osiris — Egypt's emblem of stability and endurance.

## Meaning
The djed is the ancient Egyptian symbol of stability, endurance, and permanence — often interpreted as the backbone/spine of the god Osiris. The "raising of the djed" ceremony symbolized the resurrection of Osiris and the triumph of order and stability over chaos. Together with the [[ankh]] (life) and [[was-scepter]] (power), it forms the classic Egyptian power triad.

## Origin
Ancient Egyptian, one of the oldest symbols, dating from the Predynastic/Early Dynastic period and central to the Osiris cult. Fully PD. A "deeper cut" Egyptian motif. Respect Kemetic use.

## Visual description
A vertical column or pillar topped by four (sometimes three) stacked horizontal crossbars near the upper end, suggesting a stylized spine or a bundled column with capitals. Render in hieroglyphic line, gold relief, or as architectural-column inspiration.

# The Remix Engine — Universal Formula for Generating Any Remix

> **META DOCUMENT.** This file is not a single remix. It is the algorithm that produces *all* remixes. The other files in this section are worked presets of this engine. An AI generation system can run this document directly to invent a brand-new pairing on demand.

The Polymath remix strategy rests on one principle: **collide a free source with an unrelated category, and the synthesis becomes new, ownable intellectual property.** The ingredients are old and unprotected; the combination is original and ours. This document specifies, step by step, how to perform that collision reliably and legally.

---

## Part 1 — The formula in one line

```
REMIX = (Source A from the Bible) × (Category B from the category bank)
        → find TENSION + HARMONY
        → bind with PALETTE + MOTIF + ARCHETYPE
        → emit CONCEPT / VISUAL / STORY / POD / BRAND
```

Originality is created in the binding step, not the ingredient step. Two themes laid side by side is a mood board. Two themes fused through a tension is a new work.

---

## Part 2 — The runnable algorithm (AI executes top to bottom)

### STEP 0 — Legal gate (mandatory, runs first)
1. Take the candidate **Source A**.
2. Resolve it to a Bible entry and read its `pd_status` and `risk_level`.
3. Run it through [[01-Legal-Guidelines/decision-tree]].
4. **Branch:**
   - `safe` → proceed.
   - `caution` → proceed only using the public-domain substrate; carry the constraint into `risk_notes` of the output.
   - `avoid` → **reject A.** Either swap to the public-domain ancestor of A (e.g. swap "the Universal bolt-neck Frankenstein" for "Shelley's 1818 textual creature") or pick a different source.
5. Never accept a live trademark, a studio's specific visual design, a logo, a mascot, or a real person's likeness as Source A. If asked to, refuse and substitute the free substrate.

> The engine is only as safe as its inputs. This gate is non-optional.

### STEP 1 — Pick Source A
Draw from any Bible source section (mythology, public-domain literature, historical design, symbols, flora/fauna, astronomy). Prefer entries marked `pd_status: public-domain`. Record: its core **symbols**, its dominant **palette tendency**, its native **archetype**.

### STEP 2 — Pick Category B
Draw from the **category bank** (Part 4). Category B is a context, activity, medium, technology, or subculture — *not* another protected IP. Record: its iconography, its rituals/verbs, its native audience.

### STEP 3 — Find the TENSION
Ask: *what about A does not naturally belong in B?* Write the single sharpest clash as one sentence. The clash is the engine's fuel — it is what makes a viewer do a double-take. (Egyptian eternity vs. the cold infinity of space; knightly honor vs. skater irreverence; Victorian handcraft vs. machine intelligence.)

### STEP 4 — Find the HARMONY
Ask: *what about A secretly rhymes with B?* Write the single strongest hidden fit as one sentence. Harmony is what stops the remix from being random — it gives the brain a reason to accept the fusion. (Pyramids and rockets both point at the stars; knights and skaters both armor up to fall and rise; ornament and algorithms both generate complexity from rules.)

### STEP 5 — Bind with the three fasteners
Choose exactly one of each so the two worlds read as a single world:
- **PALETTE** — one named color system (pull from a color-palette Bible entry, or pick a 4–6 swatch set with hex). The palette is usually inherited from A's era and pushed toward B's mood.
- **MOTIF** — one repeated visual device that physically merges A and B (a hieroglyph rendered as a circuit; a knight's heraldry on a deck; an ornamental frame made of code).
- **ARCHETYPE** — one character/role both worlds share (the psychopomp, the trickster, the explorer, the alchemist). The archetype carries the story.

### STEP 6 — Emit the five outputs
Generate, in this fixed order:
1. **CONCEPT** — a one-line pitch + 10–20 concept seeds.
2. **VISUAL** — palette + motif + composition + AI-image-prompt cues + the "avoid" line.
3. **STORY** — archetype + conflict template + setting + 3–6 loglines.
4. **POD** — product formats + slogan angles + layout angles + SKU list.
5. **BRAND** — Z-named brand candidates + series naming pattern + tone.

### STEP 7 — Write the legal note
Re-state A's PD substrate and name the specific protected modern adaptation of A that must be avoided. Set `risk_level` and `risk_notes` on the output accordingly.

## Part 3 — The tension/harmony heuristic (how to find the spark fast)

For any A × B, scan these axes for the sharpest clash and the strongest rhyme:

| Axis | Find the TENSION by asking | Find the HARMONY by asking |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | Which one is ancient, which is modern? | Do both deal with a kind of *permanence* or *cycle*? |
| **Scale** | Intimate vs. cosmic? Hand-sized vs. planetary? | Do both obsess over a single repeated unit? |
| **Material** | Organic vs. synthetic? Stone vs. light? | Do both grow complexity from simple rules? |
| **Motion** | Static/eternal vs. fast/disposable? | Do both involve a journey or a transformation? |
| **Role** | Sacred vs. profane? Noble vs. street? | Do both have a guardian, a trickster, an explorer? |
| **Emotion** | Reverence vs. play? Dread vs. wonder? | Do both reach for awe? |

Pick the row with the loudest clash for TENSION and the row with the quietest, most surprising fit for HARMONY. You rarely need more than one of each.

## Part 4 — The category bank (Source B options)

Category B should be a free *context*, never a protected property. Standing options:

- **Technology / future:** space, sci-fi, cyberpunk, AI, robotics, circuitry, blockchain, retro-tech, aviation, deep-sea tech.
- **Subculture / activity:** skateboarding, surfing, gaming, gym/fitness, coffee culture, tattoo, graffiti, climbing, biking, cosplay.
- **Sport:** football, basketball, boxing, racing, chess, esports, Olympics-style events.
- **Nature / science:** ocean creatures, dinosaurs, insects, botany, astronomy, weather, geology, fungi, anatomy.
- **Craft / medium:** music, dance, cooking, architecture, cartography, quilting, blueprinting, woodblock print, neon signage.
- **Mood / genre:** noir, horror, fairy tale, western, carnival, steampunk, vaporwave, cottagecore, brutalism.
- **Everyday life:** playground, office, kitchen, road trip, commute, gardening, parenting, pets.

Any source section of the Bible can also act as Category B if it is public domain — remixing two source domains (e.g. Norse myth × dinosaurs) is fully allowed.

## Part 6 — Worked micro-example (engine run, abbreviated)

**Input:** A = Greek Mythology (public domain, ancient/folk), B = Modern Sports.

- **Legal gate:** Greek myth is `public-domain` (ancient). Avoid: any *specific modern film/game design* of a Greek god (e.g. a particular video-game Kratos). Use the textual/classical-sculpture substrate. → `safe`.
- **Tension:** Olympian divinity vs. sweaty mortal competition.
- **Harmony:** Both worship physical excellence and crown a single champion.
- **Palette:** "Marble & Laurel" — warm white #F4EFE6, bronze #A6703B, olive #6E7B3D, blood red #8E2B27.
- **Motif:** athletes posed as broken classical statues, mid-action, with laurel and stadium light.
- **Archetype:** the Champion (hero who must be tested).
- **Concept seeds:** "Hermes as a track sprinter," "Atlas at the deadlift platform," "the Fates as referees," "Sisyphus on leg day," ...
- **POD:** gym tee "TRAIN LIKE A TITAN," poster "PANTHEON OF GAINS."
- **Brand:** **Zelarion Athletics**, series "The Zeus Cuts."

## Part 7 — Quality checks before shipping a remix

1. **Tension present?** If A × B reads as merely "matching," it is a theme, not a remix. Sharpen the clash.
2. **Harmony present?** If the fusion feels random, find the hidden rhyme or change B.
3. **One world, not two?** The palette + motif + archetype must make A and B read as a single coherent universe.
4. **Legally clean?** Source A is PD/open; no protected modern adaptation is being reproduced; brand name is Z-coined and clearable.
5. **Sells across lenses?** A strong remix produces output in at least three of the five lenses (concept/visual/story/POD/brand).

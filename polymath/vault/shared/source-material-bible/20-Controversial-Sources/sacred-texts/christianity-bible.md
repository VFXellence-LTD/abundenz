# Christianity — Bible Translation Rights & Merch Guidance

The Christian Bible is one of the highest-revenue source texts for faith-niche POD and apparel. Scripture typography — a KJV verse on a quality t-shirt or poster — is a proven, repeatable product format with a large and loyal buyer base. The rights question is straightforward once you know which editions are safe.

---

## The short answer

**Use the King James Version (KJV, 1611). It is public domain in the US. Do not use the NIV, ESV, NASB, NLT, The Message, or any other modern translation without a license.**

---

## Safe editions (public domain in the US)

### King James Version (KJV) — 1611
- **PD basis:** Published 1611; well beyond any copyright term. Universally acknowledged as public domain in the US.
- **Risk level:** Safe for US commercial use.
- **UK caveat:** The KJV is subject to **Crown Copyright** in the UK, administered by the King's Printer (Cambridge University Press and Collins have the UK publishing rights). If your products sell into the UK market or ship internationally to UK buyers, note this. For a US-centric storefront with no specific UK sales push, this is a low practical risk but should be disclosed to Boss before any UK-targeted launch.
- **Source:** Available at Project Gutenberg (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10) and hundreds of other PD repositories.
- **Practical note:** The KJV's archaic phrasing ("thee," "thou," "doth," "verily") is part of its aesthetic appeal for many buyers. It reads as authoritative and timeless — a significant design asset, not a liability.

### American Standard Version (ASV) — 1901
- **PD basis:** Published 1901; 95-year term expired 1996.
- **Risk level:** Safe.
- **Character:** More literal than KJV; less archaic phrasing. Useful where plain-language readability matters.
- **Source:** Project Gutenberg https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/9900

### Douay-Rheims Bible — 1582–1609 (OT 1609, NT 1582)
- **PD basis:** Original publication 1582–1610; Challoner revision 1749–1752. All terms expired.
- **Risk level:** Safe.
- **Character:** Catholic translation; traditional, formal register. Market fits: Catholic-audience products.
- **Source:** Project Gutenberg and CCEL (Christian Classics Ethereal Library).

### Young's Literal Translation (YLT) — 1862, revised 1898
- **PD basis:** Robert Young d. 1888; work published 1862/1898. All terms expired.
- **Risk level:** Safe.
- **Character:** Extremely literal, even to the point of awkwardness. Niche use for "word of God, literally" aesthetic.

### Webster's Bible Translation — 1833
- **PD basis:** Noah Webster d. 1843; published 1833. Expired.
- **Risk level:** Safe.

## DO NOT USE — active copyright (no license = infringement)

| Translation | Copyright holder | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| New International Version (NIV) | Biblica | First pub. 1978; active copyright |
| English Standard Version (ESV) | Crossway | First pub. 2001; active copyright |
| New American Standard Bible (NASB) | Lockman Foundation | First pub. 1971; active copyright |
| New Living Translation (NLT) | Tyndale House | First pub. 1996; active copyright |
| The Message | NavPress | First pub. 1993; active copyright |
| Christian Standard Bible (CSB) | LifeWay | First pub. 2017; active copyright |
| New King James Version (NKJV) | Thomas Nelson | First pub. 1982; active copyright |
| New Revised Standard Version (NRSV) | National Council of Churches | First pub. 1989; active copyright |
| Revised Standard Version (RSV) | National Council of Churches | First pub. 1952; active copyright |

**No modern study Bible edition** (ESV Study Bible, NIV Study Bible, etc.) is safe regardless of the base text — study notes and apparatus are independently copyrighted.

## Using KJV text on products — practical guidance

### Typography products (posters, t-shirts, cards)
- Use the verse text exactly as it appears in a verified PD source (Project Gutenberg edition is reliable).
- Do not copy the typeset layout of a modern printed KJV Bible edition — set the type yourself or commission original lettering.
- Cite the source: "KJV" or "King James Version" (not a specific modern publisher's edition).
- Do not imply endorsement by a specific church, denomination, or publisher.

### Popular high-demand verse categories
Verses that consistently perform well in faith-niche POD (all confirmed KJV PD):
- Philippians 4:13 — "I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me."
- Psalm 23 — "The Lord is my shepherd..."
- John 3:16 — "For God so loved the world..."
- Romans 8:28 — "And we know that all things work together for good..."
- Isaiah 40:31 — "But they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength..."
- Proverbs 3:5-6 — "Trust in the LORD with all thine heart..."
- Jeremiah 29:11 — "For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, saith the LORD..."

### What you cannot trademark
You cannot obtain trademark protection for a scripture verse, even in your specific typographic layout, if the verse is the primary element. The artistic design can potentially be protected but the words cannot be trademarked. This cuts both ways: your design is not protectable via trademark, but you are also free to use the PD text without fear of a competitor holding a trademark in the words.

### AI image generation with scripture
Feeding a KJV verse into an AI image-generation prompt as text overlay is safe. The verse text is PD. The generated image itself is your original work. Do not prompt AI tools to generate images "in the style of" a specific modern Bible illustrator — that raises separate issues.

- Faith-niche products perform well across Amazon Merch, Etsy, Redbubble, and TeePublic.
- Clearly KJV-attributed products avoid translation-rights risk and also signal authenticity to buyers familiar with the KJV's cultural authority.

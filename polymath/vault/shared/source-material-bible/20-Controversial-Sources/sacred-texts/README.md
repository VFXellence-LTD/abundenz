# Sacred Texts — Rights Overview

This subfolder covers the copyright status of major religious and sacred texts and their English translations. It exists to answer one practical question: **which specific edition of this text can I legally print on a product?**

This subfolder **defers to [[01-Legal-Guidelines/README|01 — Legal Guidelines]]** for all foundational doctrine. Section 01 wins if anything conflicts.

---

## The master rule

> **An ancient original text being public domain does not make the translation you are holding public domain.**

This is the single most important rule in this entire subfolder. The ancient Hebrew of the Torah, the Greek of the New Testament, the Arabic of the Quran, and the Pali of the Dhammapada are all ancient — uncopyrightable, belonging to humanity. But **every English translation is an independent creative work** with its own author, its own publication date, and its own copyright term.

The KJV (1611) and the NIV (1978) are both English Bibles. The KJV is public domain. The NIV is under active copyright held by Biblica. Printing NIV text on a t-shirt is infringement. Printing KJV text is not. The text may look almost identical to a casual reader; the legal status is completely different.

Full doctrine: [[translation-rights-rule|translation-rights-rule]].

## Per-faith safe/unsafe translation matrix

| Faith | SAFE editions (US, PD) | DO NOT USE (active copyright) |
|---|---|---|
| **Christianity** | King James Version (1611), Douay-Rheims (1582–1609), American Standard Version (1901), Young's Literal Translation (1898) | NIV, ESV, NASB, NLT, The Message, CSB, NKJV, and all modern study/annotated editions |
| **Islam** | Pickthall (1930 — verify US pub date) | Yusuf Ali (1934 — NOT YET PD in US until ~2030), Sahih International, all modern annotated editions |
| **Judaism** | JPS 1917 | NJPS 1985, JPS Study Bible, all modern annotated editions |
| **Buddhism** | Müller translations (Sacred Books of the East, 1879–1910), T.W. Rhys Davids translations (1881–1921) | Modern Pali Text Society translations post-1930, Thich Nhat Hanh, Bhikkhu Bodhi |
| **Hinduism** | Müller's Sacred Books of the East, Edwin Arnold's *Song Celestial* (Gita, 1885), early Monier-Williams translations | Swami Prabhupada's Gita (1968+), Barbara Stoler Miller, all modern scholarly editions |

See the individual faith entries for edition-specific detail and source URLs.

## Cultural sensitivity note

Copyright status and cultural respect are separate questions. Some sacred texts whose translations are legally free to use still carry **significant cultural sensitivity around commercial application**. Putting scripture on a t-shirt is normal for many Christian audiences; the same approach applied to certain texts or images may be deeply offensive in the faith tradition being referenced. The file [[cultural-sensitivity|cultural-sensitivity]] covers the specific areas where legal clearance does not mean it is a good business decision.

# Faith Niche — Operating Guide

The faith niche — specifically Christian scripture-based POD and content — is the **highest-revenue, lowest-platform-risk** controversial niche documented in this section. It has a large, loyal, purchase-active buyer base; its content is broadly permitted on major platforms; and the public-domain material available (the King James Version of the Bible) is rich enough to fuel an unlimited product catalog without ever touching a live copyright.

This is the primary revenue opportunity in section 20.

## The KJV-only rule

**All scripture text used on products in the faith-niche brand must come from the King James Version (1611).**

This is not a preference — it is the operating rule that keeps the brand legally clean:

- The KJV is the only widely-used complete English Bible that is unambiguously public domain in the US.
- Other safe-but-obscure translations (ASV 1901, Douay-Rheims) are available but the KJV is the culturally dominant choice for the US faith market and is the right default.
- No exceptions: if a buyer, designer, or content creator suggests using NIV, ESV, or any other modern translation "because it sounds better," the answer is no. Use the KJV text and adjust the design instead.

KJV text source: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10 (verify against National Archives or BibleGateway's KJV if checking specific verses).

## Market overview

### Who buys faith-niche content

The US faith-niche buyer is:
- Predominantly Christian (broadly Protestant; large Catholic segment)
- Buys for personal use, gifts, and home décor
- Values quality and meaning over price
- Highly loyal to brands that "get" their faith — repeats purchase and recommends
- Peaks around Christmas, Easter, Mother's Day, Father's Day, baptisms, confirmations, graduation

### Platform performance

Faith-niche products perform well on:
- **Amazon Merch** — large faith-buyer base; faith content is not restricted under Amazon's content policies; strong search traffic for scripture-verse products
- **Etsy** — faith niche over-indexes; buyers browse for "Christian gift" and "Bible verse print"
- **Redbubble** — active but smaller faith community
- **TeePublic** — viable

Faith content is largely unaffected by political-content restrictions on any major platform. This is a significant advantage over the political niche.

## High-performing product categories

### 1. Scripture-verse typography apparel
Single-verse typography on a t-shirt or hoodie. The verse is prominent text; background may be plain, gradient, or lightly textured. This is the highest-volume category.

**Top-performing verses** (KJV, all PD):
- Philippians 4:13 — "I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me."
- Psalm 23:1 — "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."
- John 3:16 — "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son..."
- Romans 8:28 — "And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God..."
- Isaiah 40:31 — "But they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength..."
- Jeremiah 29:11 — "For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, saith the LORD, thoughts of peace, and not of evil, to give you an expected end."
- Proverbs 3:5-6 — "Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding."

### 2. Devotional-quote posters and prints
Full Psalm text (e.g., Psalm 23 in its entirety), formatted as an art print. For home décor. Higher price point than apparel.

### 3. Seasonal faith products
- Christmas: Luke 2 (the Nativity narrative); "Joy to the World" (Isaac Watts, 1719 — PD hymn)
- Easter: John 11:25; Resurrection passages from the four Gospels
- Mother's Day: Proverbs 31:10-31 (the "wife of noble character" passage); mother-and-child scripture

### 4. Faith-and-nature overlay
Scripture verse paired with nature imagery — a mountain, ocean, forest, sunset. Both the text (KJV) and any AI-generated or PD reference imagery can be freely used. Strong seller in poster/print format.

### 5. Cross-themed designs
The cross as a symbol is ancient and has no trademark or copyright owner. Clean, modern cross designs with minimal scripture text consistently perform well.

## Content limits — what NOT to do

- **No modern translation text.** See the KJV-only rule above.
- **No denominational logos or crests.** Catholic, Methodist, Baptist, etc. denominational symbols may be trademarked by those organizations.
- **No specific church or pastor's name/brand.** Don't create products that appear to be official merchandise of a specific church or ministry without a formal arrangement.
- **No political overlay.** Keep the faith niche clean — no "Christian nationalist" framing, no political slogans alongside scripture, no partisan political content. Faith content appeals across the political spectrum; politicizing it cuts the market in half and raises platform-policy risk.
- **No depictions of Jesus in photorealistic style designed to evoke a specific historical person.** Traditional artistic depictions of Jesus as a figure (stylized, iconic, painterly) are common and generally accepted; photorealistic AI-generated "images of Jesus as a real person" risk appearing exploitative.

## UK Crown Copyright note

The KJV is subject to Crown Copyright in the UK. UK-market sales (UK Amazon, international shipping to UK buyers) sit in a gray zone. For a US-centric business focused on US platforms, this is low practical risk but should be flagged to Boss before any UK-targeted launch.

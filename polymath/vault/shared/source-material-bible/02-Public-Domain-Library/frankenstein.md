# Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus

> The original "creator destroyed by his creation" — the founding myth of science fiction, and a goldmine of safe gothic imagery *as long as you avoid the 1931 movie face*.

## Overview
Mary Shelley wrote *Frankenstein* in 1816–17 (famously begun during the "year without a summer" at Villa Diodati) and published it anonymously in 1818. It follows Victor Frankenstein, a young natural philosopher who assembles and animates a creature from dead matter, then abandons it in horror — setting off a chain of tragedy. The 1818 text is unambiguously public domain worldwide. The single most valuable legal point: **the famous monster *look* belongs to Universal's 1931 film, not to Shelley.** Shelley's creature is articulate, agile, and tragic, with yellow translucent skin and lustrous black hair — nothing like the lumbering, bolt-necked icon.

## Themes
- **Creation and responsibility** — the maker's duty to what he makes; abandonment as the true crime. The most modern, reusable reading: AI/biotech as "the modern creature."
- **Hubris / overreaching** — the Promethean subtitle; stealing fire from the gods.
- **Isolation and the outsider** — the creature's exclusion drives the horror; deeply relatable.
- **Nature vs. science** — sublime Romantic landscapes set against the unnatural laboratory.
- **Revenge and the cycle of harm** — mirror-image obsession between creator and creature.

## Symbols
- **Fire / the spark** — knowledge that both warms and destroys.
- **Lightning** — the violent animating force (note: "it's alive" galvanism is largely film embellishment; the text is coy about the method).
- **Ice and the Arctic** — the frame narrative's frozen wasteland; emotional coldness.
- **The double** — creator and creature as two halves of one self.

## Character archetypes
- **Victor Frankenstein** — the Overreacher / Tragic Creator. Name is PD.
- **The Creature** — the Abandoned Outsider, the Sympathetic Monster. Name/role PD; the *bolt-neck design* is NOT.
- **Captain Walton** — the Framing Witness / parallel overreacher (polar explorer).

## Visual motifs
- Candle-lit laboratory crowded with apparatus (described, PD).
- Sublime stormy alpine peaks (Mont Blanc, Chamonix) and the Sea of Ice.
- Arctic wasteland and a ship locked in pack ice.
- A gaunt, towering figure — render from the *text*, never the movie.

## Reusable concepts
- The "creator abandons creation" engine — transplantable to AI, robotics, parenthood, art.
- Frame narrative (letters within a story within a story).
- The articulate monster who argues its own humanity.

## Inspirational quotes

## Story opportunities
- "The Creature's Diary" — first-person retelling (the text already gives the creature a long monologue).
- Modern transplant: a software engineer who creates a sentient model and abandons it.
- Children's-book-safe version: gentle "the lonely made-friend who just wanted to belong" (strip horror; keep the outsider heart).

## POD opportunities
- "It's a-LIVE" is film-coded — prefer text-derived lines to stay clean.
- Motif posters: lightning-over-castle silhouette, anatomical spark diagrams.
- Pattern repeat: candles, beakers, lightning bolts in a gothic toile.

## Risks
- **Copyright (text):** none — PD worldwide.
- **Adaptation trap:** Universal 1931 Karloff/Pierce design (flat head, neck bolts, heavy brow, green skin) is RESTRICTED. So is the "Bride of Frankenstein" hair-streak design. Do not reproduce.
- **Trademark:** "FRANKENSTEIN" has live trademark registrations in costume/entertainment classes — don't brand a product line as Frankenstein in a way implying affiliation.
- **Right of publicity:** none for the work itself; do not depict Boris Karloff's likeness.

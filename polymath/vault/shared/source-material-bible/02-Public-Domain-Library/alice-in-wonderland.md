# Alice's Adventures in Wonderland

> The deepest well of surreal, instantly-recognisable imagery in PD literature — and Tenniel's original engravings are *yours to use*, while Disney's designs are not.

## Overview
Lewis Carroll's 1865 nonsense fantasy (with the 1871 sequel *Through the Looking-Glass*) follows Alice down a rabbit hole into a dream-logic world of talking animals, riddles, and absurd authority. John Tenniel's original wood-engraved illustrations are as iconic as the text and are **public domain** — a major asset. The caution is the same as Oz: the **Disney 1951** and **Burton 2010** designs are separate, protected adaptations.

## Themes
- **Nonsense and logic** — rules that subvert themselves; play with language.
- **Growing up / identity and change** — Alice's shifting size; "Who am I?"
- **Dream logic** — associative, rule-bending narrative (perfect for surreal AI art).
- **Authority as absurd** — the Queen's "Off with their heads!" tyranny is hollow.
- **Curiosity** — the engine of the whole adventure.

## Symbols
- **The rabbit hole** — descent into the irrational (now a universal metaphor).
- **The pocket watch** — anxious time, "I'm late!"
- **The key and the tiny door** — thresholds, the unreachable.
- **The mushroom** — transformation ("one side makes you grow taller").
- **The looking-glass** — reflection, the inverted world.
- **Playing cards** — arbitrary hierarchy; painted roses (covering a mistake).

## Character archetypes
- **Alice** — the Curious Child / level-headed dreamer (text PD; *Disney design* restricted).
- **White Rabbit** — the Anxious Messenger.
- **Mad Hatter / March Hare** — the Mad Host(s).
- **Queen of Hearts** — the Capricious Tyrant.
- **Cheshire Cat** — the Grinning Trickster / disappearing guide.

## Visual motifs
- White Rabbit with waistcoat and watch; Alice falling past floating objects.
- Mad tea party with mismatched cups; flamingo croquet; card soldiers painting roses red.
- The Cheshire grin fading in air; oversized mushroom and caterpillar.

## Reusable concepts
- **Portal-to-dream-world** structure; episodic dream encounters.
- "Down the rabbit hole" as a metaphor framework (research, obsession, the internet).
- Riddles, wordplay, and logical paradoxes as content engines.

## Inspirational quotes
> "We're all mad here." — the Cheshire Cat, *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland* (1865)

> "If you don't know where you are going, any road will take you there." (commonly paraphrased from the Cheshire Cat exchange) — *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland* (1865)

> "Why, sometimes I've believed as many as six impossible things before breakfast." — the White Queen, *Through the Looking-Glass* (1871)

## Risks
- **Copyright:** Carroll text + Tenniel illustrations PD.
- **Adaptation trap:** Disney 1951 and Burton 2010 character designs RESTRICTED. Avoid the specific blue-pinafore Disney Alice, Disney Cheshire/Hatter/Queen designs.
- **Trademark:** "Disney's Alice in Wonderland" trademarked; avoid implying affiliation.

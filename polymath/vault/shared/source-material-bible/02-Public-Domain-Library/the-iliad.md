# The Iliad

> The foundational war epic — rage, glory, and doom before the walls of Troy — free in substance, but bound by the same **translation trap** as the Odyssey.

## Overview
Homer's *Iliad* (c. 8th century BCE) covers a few weeks in the tenth year of the Trojan War, centered on the wrath of Achilles after a quarrel with Agamemnon, building to the death of Hector and the grief of his father Priam. The underlying epic is ancient and free; **specific modern translations are copyrighted** — the same caution as the Odyssey. Use a PD translation (Pope, Butler, Lang/Leaf/Myers) or paraphrase.

## Themes
- **Rage and its cost** — the poem literally opens on "the wrath of Achilles."
- **Glory (kleos) vs. mortality** — the hero's bargain: a short, blazing life.
- **Fate vs. honor** — even the gods cannot save a doomed favorite.
- **The futility and horror of war** — alongside its terrible glamour.
- **Grief and mercy** — Priam begging for Hector's body is the moral summit.
- **Pride** — Agamemnon's and Achilles's pride drive the tragedy.

## Symbols
- **The wrath of Achilles** — destructive anger as the engine of tragedy.
- **The walls of Troy** — the besieged, the inevitable fall.
- **The shield of Achilles** — the whole world depicted in miniature; art amid war.
- **The funeral pyre** — mortality, honor for the dead.
- **The heel** — fatal vulnerability ("Achilles' heel," a universal metaphor; note this detail is post-Homeric tradition).

## Character archetypes
- **Achilles** — the Doomed, Wrathful Hero.
- **Hector** — the Noble Defender / sympathetic enemy.
- **Priam** — the Grieving King.
- **Helen** — "the face that launched a thousand ships."
- **The Olympian gods** — the Meddling Powers.

## Visual motifs
- Bronze-Age warriors, plumed helmets, the great walls of Troy.
- Greek phalanx, single-combat duels, the ornate shield, ships drawn up on the shore, the funeral pyre.

## Reusable concepts
- The **tragic-warrior arc** (glory chosen over long life).
- "Rage" as a thematic study; the noble-enemy dynamic.
- The "Achilles' heel" metaphor for a hidden weakness.

## Inspirational quotes
> "Sing, O goddess, the anger of Achilles son of Peleus, that brought countless ills upon the Achaeans." — opening line, *The Iliad* (Butler PD translation, 1898)

> "Without a sign, his sword the brave man draws, and asks no omen but his country's cause." — Pope's verse *Iliad* (PD, 1715–20)

## Risks
- **Copyright (epic):** none — ancient.
- **Translation trap (primary risk):** modern translations copyrighted; **use pre-1930 PD translations** or paraphrase.
- **Adaptation trap:** avoid the 2004 *Troy* film designs and Brad Pitt's likeness.
- **Attribution:** the Trojan Horse and Achilles' heel are from later tradition, not the Iliad text — attribute carefully.

# Wildlife

## Description
Wild animals as subject and symbol: big cats, wolves, bears, foxes, raptors, elephants, primates, reptiles, insects, and birds, across savanna, forest, tundra, and jungle. The animals themselves are free; the deep public-domain layer is antique zoological engraving (Audubon's birds, Haeckel's *Kunstformen der Natur*, Victorian field guides). Distinct from [[ocean-life]] (marine) and [[pet-parent]] (domestic companions), though buyers cross over. Sub-flavors: realistic/scientific, geometric/low-poly, cute/kawaii, "spirit animal" symbolic.

## Why It Resonates (Audience Psychology)
Wildlife sells **identity through animal symbolism**. People project traits onto animals and wear them as self-description: the wolf for loyalty and independence, the lion for courage, the fox for cleverness, the owl for wisdom. It also taps the **protective/conservation** impulse (buyers feel virtuous supporting "save the [animal]" themes) and a deep, almost universal **affection** — animals are the single most reliable way to trigger warmth, which is why they dominate children's products and gift purchases. Few themes are as cross-generational: the same lion appeals to a toddler, a teenager, and a grandparent.

## Design / Story / POD Opportunities
- **POD/t-shirt:** geometric low-poly animal portraits (perennial bestseller); "spirit animal" trait tees; double-exposure animal-and-landscape; cute animal puns.
- **Poster:** restored antique zoological plates as premium prints; watercolor-splash animal art; minimalist animal-line sets; nursery animal alphabets.
- **Children's book:** animal-fact books; gentle animal-friendship stories; "who lives here" habitat books; an original animal-mascot series.
- **Podcast/video:** wildlife-storytelling; conservation explainers; "animal of the week."
- **Brand:** an original cute animal mascot (Z-named) anchoring a kids or conservation-themed line.

## Visual Motifs
Scientific engraving and field-guide plates, geometric/low-poly faceting, watercolor splash, double-exposure silhouettes, vintage taxidermy/cabinet-of-curiosities framing, linocut. See [[13]].

## Symbols
Lion (courage), wolf (loyalty/independence), eagle (freedom/vision), fox (cleverness), bear (strength/protection), owl (wisdom), elephant (memory/dignity), butterfly (transformation). See [[09]].

## Palette Pairings
Savanna gold + acacia green + dusk orange for big cats; slate + pine + snow-white for wolves/bears; warm earth tones for woodland; bright kawaii pastels for cute animals; muted antique sepia for engraving prints. See [[08]].

## Remix Combinations

- **Wildlife × Cute** → kawaii animals for kids/kawaii lines.
- **Wildlife × Affirmations** → "be brave like a lion" trait/affirmation crossovers.
- **Wildlife × Cottagecore** → woodland-critter cozy scenes.

## Evergreen vs Trend
**Evergreen — 5/5.** Animals are eternal and universally loved. Particular *styles* trend (low-poly had a peak; watercolor is steady), but the category never declines.

## Saturation / Competition Note
**Very high** for wolves, lions, and generic low-poly. Open lanes: **less-common animals** (red panda, axolotl, capybara — the last two are currently surging), **restored antique engravings as premium art**, **a consistent cute mascot style**, and **region-specific wildlife** for local markets. Compete on art style and species choice, not on "majestic wolf #4,000."

## Legal / Execution Notes
Real animals are free; *character* animals are not. Per [[01]]: avoid Disney/franchise animal designs entirely — note the staggered PD of A. A. Milne's Pooh characters (Pooh 2022, Tigger 2024) applies only to the original *book illustrations*, never Disney's versions. Build from real anatomy and original art. Pre-1930 zoological plates are public domain.

# Fantasy

## Description
The imaginative-secondary-world genre and its trope vocabulary: dragons, wizards, knights, elves and dwarves, enchanted forests, magic systems, lost kingdoms, and the hero's quest. The *genre conventions* are free — no one owns "wizard" or "dragon" — but the most valuable-looking executions sit dangerously close to heavily defended franchises. This category is therefore played through **original worldbuilding** that uses public tropes while inventing its own names, maps, and creature designs. Sub-flavors: high/epic fantasy, cozy/cottage fantasy, gaslamp fantasy, dark fantasy, fairytale fantasy.

## Why It Resonates (Audience Psychology)
Fantasy is the **escapism premium**. Buyers are purchasing a doorway out of the mundane and a membership badge for a tribe (TTRPG players, fantasy readers, gamers). It serves the "chosen one" fantasy of significance, the comfort of clearly-drawn good versus evil, and the deep human pull of *wonder*. The genre also benefits from an unusually devoted, high-spend fandom that buys maps, character art, dice, apparel, and decor — fantasy fans furnish their lives with the aesthetic, not just consume the stories.

## Design / Story / POD Opportunities
- **POD/t-shirt:** "class" archetype tees (rogue/mage/paladin without D&D's named classes); dragon line-art; "not all who wander are lost"-style original quote typography; tavern/guild crest designs.
- **Poster:** original fantasy maps (huge evergreen seller); creature bestiary plates; "magic system" diagram art.
- **Children's book:** gentle quest stories; a cozy-fantasy creature-companion series; original-creature alphabet books.
- **Podcast/video:** actual-play original campaigns; worldbuilding-craft channels; "lore of [invented world]" series.
- **Brand:** an original IP fantasy world (Z-named) — the single highest-ceiling play in the whole bible, because a coherent invented world can spawn books, merch, games, and licensing with zero IP exposure.

## Visual Motifs
Illuminated-manuscript borders and drop-caps, glowing arcane runes and magic circles, enchanted-forest depth, castle silhouettes against moons, dragon-scale texture, weathered treasure maps, crystal and gemstone glow. See [[13]].

## Symbols
Dragon (power/greed/wisdom), sword-in-stone (rightful destiny), crystal orb (divination), rune (encoded magic), crown (sovereignty), phoenix (rebirth), tome/grimoire (knowledge), magic circle (ritual). See [[09]].

## Palette Pairings
Emerald + gold + deep brown for high-fantasy richness; sage + cream + dusty rose for cozy fantasy; charcoal + ember-orange + blood-red for dark fantasy; teal + violet + silver for arcane/magical glow. See [[08]].

## Remix Combinations
- **Fantasy × Cottagecore** → "cozy fantasy," the breakout aesthetic of the decade (tea-drinking dragons, cottage-witch shops).
- **Fantasy × Dark Academia** → the magic-school aesthetic minus the Potter IP.
- **Fantasy × Steampunk** → gaslamp fantasy.
- **Fantasy × Cute** → chibi adventuring parties for kids and kawaii lines.
See [[15]].

## Evergreen vs Trend
**Evergreen — 5/5.** Fantasy is permanent and currently in a multi-decade boom. *Cozy fantasy* is a trend-leaning sub-flavor riding high right now; epic fantasy is bedrock.

## Saturation / Competition Note
**Very high** for generic dragon/wizard art. The open lanes are **cozy fantasy** (still under-served relative to demand), **original maps and worldbuilding**, and **a distinctive consistent art style**. The franchise-adjacent lane is crowded *and* legally hot — avoid it on both counts.

## Legal / Execution Notes
This is the category most likely to drift into infringement. Per [[01]]: tropes are free, named worlds are not, and Warner Bros. (Potter) and Tolkien's estate are litigious. Invent your own names, creatures, and maps. `risk_level: caution` is set deliberately as a reminder, not because the genre itself is unsafe.

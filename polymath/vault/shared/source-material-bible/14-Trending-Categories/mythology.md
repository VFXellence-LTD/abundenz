# Mythology

## Description
World mythologies — Greek, Norse, Egyptian, Celtic, Mesopotamian, Hindu, Japanese (Shinto/yokai), Aztec/Maya, Yoruba/Orisha, Slavic, Polynesian — treated as a shared global reservoir of gods, heroes, monsters, and origin stories. The underlying myths are ancient folk material with no living rights-holder; the public-domain *texts* that record them (the Iliad, the Poetic and Prose Edda, Ovid's Metamorphoses, the Epic of Gilgamesh) are freely usable. Scope covers deities, mythic creatures (phoenix, kraken, valkyries, kitsune, garuda), heroic cycles, and cosmological diagrams. Out of scope: any 20th/21st-century reinterpretation that has its own copyright or trademark.

## Why It Resonates (Audience Psychology)
Mythology sells **meaning at scale**. Buyers reach for it when they want to dress themselves in something larger than the everyday — strength (Norse), wisdom (Athena), rebellion (Prometheus), destiny (the Fates). It is identity armor: a gym-goer wears Spartan/Norse iconography to signal discipline; a reader of the classics wears Athena's owl to signal intellect. It also rides three durable cultural engines: the perennial popularity of Greek/Norse myth in games and film keeps it top-of-mind, the "dark academia" and "cosmic" aesthetics borrow heavily from it, and parents reliably buy myth retellings for children because the stories teach without preaching.

## Design / Story / POD Opportunities
- **POD/t-shirt:** minimalist line-art deity portraits; "patron god of [trait]" series; Norse rune typographic tees; constellation-myth crossovers; Spartan/stoic-quote apparel.
- **Poster:** marble-statue duotone prints; mythic-monster bestiary plates; pantheon "family tree" infographics.
- **Children's book:** gentle retellings of creation myths and trickster tales (original prose, original art); a "tiny gods" cute-pantheon series.
- **Podcast/video:** "one myth per episode" narrative format; comparative-mythology explainers; monster-of-the-week.
- **Brand:** an original storytelling brand built on a *fictional invented pantheon* (Z-named, see [[01]] naming) to sidestep all IP entirely while keeping the mythic flavor.

## Visual Motifs
Marble statuary and broken classical sculpture, the Greek-key meander border, gold-leaf fracture (kintsugi-adjacent), constellation line-art, weathered-stone relief carving, smoke-and-cloud divine atmospherics. See [[13]].

## Symbols
Lightning bolt (Zeus/sky-father), trident (sea), winged sandals (Hermes/psychopomp), laurel wreath (victory), ouroboros (eternal return), world-tree (Yggdrasil/axis mundi), raven (Odin/omen), ankh (Egyptian life). See [[09]].

## Palette Pairings
Aged marble + oxidized bronze + gold leaf for Greco-Roman gravitas; deep indigo + frost-white + blood-red for Norse; lapis blue + ochre + gold for Egyptian; midnight + star-gold for the cosmic-pantheon crossover. See [[08]].

## Evergreen vs Trend
**Evergreen — 5/5.** Myth has sold for three thousand years and shows no decay. Individual *flavors* trend (Norse spiked with the Vikings/Marvel waves; Greek with each game and film) but the category itself is permanent.

## Saturation / Competition Note
**Very high.** Greek and Norse are the most over-served niches in all of POD. Open lanes lie in **under-represented pantheons** (Slavic, Yoruba/Orisha, Polynesian, Mesopotamian, Filipino, Aztec) and in **distinctive execution** (a consistent original art style, an invented pantheon brand). Compete on art direction and depth of lore, not on "Zeus on a shirt."

## Legal / Execution Notes
The myth is free; the *modern rendering* may not be. Per [[01]]: never trace or closely imitate a film, game, or comic's character design. Build every deity from primary-source descriptions and your own original art. An invented Z-named pantheon is the zero-risk maximum-upside option.
